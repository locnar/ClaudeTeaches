# Consistency & Accuracy Review + Gap Analysis — 2026-10-30 (completed 2026-11-01)

**Scope.** All 76 chapters, 12 appendices, the preface, `chapters/README.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and `tools/book-pdf/README.md` (≈307,000 words).

**Method.**
1. A scripted structural sweep over every file (headers, section numbering, teaser chain, every `Ch. N` / `§N.M` / `Appendix X` reference, README outline vs. real headings).
2. An agent review: eight *book-wide* checks (cross-reference targets, chapter chain, reference-machine consistency, recurring latency numbers, duplication/placement, glossary & bibliography, Appendices C/D/H/I accuracy) plus a *two-lens per-chapter review* (technical-accuracy lens and editorial/conventions lens) over **all 76 chapters, all 12 appendices and the front matter**, in 25 chunks.
3. Verification: every per-chapter finding went through an **adversarial verifier** that re-read the passage, tried to refute it, and where it mattered compiled the listing (GCC 13.3 / Clang 18), ran it, or checked primary sources on the web. The verifier's ruling and severity are attached to every row of the findings tables. The author of this report additionally re-verified the headline items marked ✅ below. The eight book-wide checks (212 findings) were not put through a second verifier; they are marked as such in the tables.

**Totals.** 2,146 rows in the findings tables: **1,243 confirmed** (1,200 reviewer findings upheld + 43 found by the verifiers), 39 refuted (kept for transparency with the verifier's reason), 651 duplicates where both lenses found the same issue, 1 uncertain, and 212 book-wide-check findings. By the verifier's severity the confirmed set is 41 high, 372 medium, 830 low, spread over 92 of the 93 files reviewed. The refutation rate (39 of 1,282 ruled-on findings, 3 %) is the best available estimate of how much to discount the unverified book-wide checks.

## 7. Technical inaccuracies (reviewer findings, high confidence; ✅ = re-verified here)

- **Ch. 3:154** — "at N = 4096 … the flat array doesn't fit in L1": 4096 × 4 B = 16 KiB fits the 48 KiB L1d with room to spare; the binary search loses to ~12 unpredictable dependent branches, not cache misses. Two lenses independently. ✅ read
- **Ch. 5:62** — uprobes and *enabled* USDT markers are an `int3` trap per hit (~1–3 µs), not "nanosecond-scale, safe to run in production" (Ch. 61 §61.3 says so itself).
- **Ch. 6:52** — retpoline is a kernel-side compile-time mitigation; it does not slow your process's virtual calls unless you compile with `-mretpoline`. **Ch. 6:53** — Ice Lake-SP reports Meltdown/L1TF/MDS as *Not affected*, so KPTI and the MDS flushes are not active on the reference machine.
- **Ch. 6** never covers SMIs (`hwlat`, `turbostat --show SMI`) or the clocksource, although Ch. 45, Appendix C and Ch. 17 (six times) send readers to Ch. 6 for exactly that.
- **Ch. 9:151** — AArch64 tolerates ordinary misaligned loads/stores to normal memory; only exclusives/LDAR/STLR/atomics fault.
- **Ch. 9:195** — packing per se does not block vectorization; the 17-byte stride (not a multiple of 8) does.
- **Ch. 10:240/189/221** — `__builtin_nontemporal_store` is Clang-only; **Ch. 10** relies on Ch. 30 for the `sfence` requirement, but Ch. 30 never mentions `sfence`/weakly-ordered stores, and C++ release fences do **not** order `movnt*` stores (a trap the chapter should name).
- **Ch. 11:182/239/261** — "latency ÷ throughput = 4 ÷ 2 ⇒ 8" (should be latency × throughput in ops/cycle). ✅ read
- **Ch. 12:257** — "even if never executed, its bytes evict the hot loop" misstates the mechanism (only fetched/decoded bytes occupy L1i/DSB).

## 10. Gap analysis — missing topics

**Method.** Fourteen independent analysts each took one lens (the five areas the author named — application-level health monitoring, OS/network health monitoring, debugging and diagnostics reporting, implementation languages, design patterns, language-specific functionality — plus HFT-domain components, engineering lifecycle, hardware frontier, OS/kernel depth, observability depth, networking depth, algorithms/numerics, and resilience/HA). Each read the full topic index, brainstormed what an expert building a tick-to-trade system would expect, and **grepped the chapters for every candidate before claiming it missing** (the grep evidence is recorded with each raw proposal). They returned 163 proposals, which were merged by hand into the **92 items** below (71 duplicates across lenses collapsed; every merged item keeps its source tags so the raw scope bullets and evidence can be recovered from `review/2026-10-30-gap-proposals-raw.txt`). A second, skeptical pass is re-grepping every merged item against the chapters to confirm the "missing / partial / covered" status; its verdicts are recorded in the **Verified** column: **✅ missing** = nothing beyond passing mentions anywhere in the book (25 items); **◐ partial** = the closest existing material is a one-angle treatment, a bullet or a forward reference, and the gap stands as described (67 items). **No item was found to be already covered**, so none was dropped. **How to read the tables.** *Kind* is the smallest footprint that does the topic justice (new chapter, new section inside an existing chapter, expansion, or appendix). *Already in the book?* names the closest existing material so the author can judge the gap. *Priority*: **high** = missing and central to building or operating a tick-to-trade system in C++ on Linux, or explicitly requested; **medium** = valuable but specialised; **low** = nice-to-have.

### 10.13 Suggested sequencing

If the author wants the smallest set of additions that closes the gaps they named, the shortlist is: **G01, G07, G08, G09** (monitoring, four chapters), **G15, G16, G22, G23** (diagnostics, three chapters + one rewrite), **G24–G27 + G30** (languages Part), **G32–G38** (patterns mini-Part), **G41–G45 + G49** (modern C++). That is roughly 22 new chapters and a dozen new sections — about a 30 % expansion — plus the editorial fixes already listed in §§1–8. The remaining items (themes F–L) are the next tier and can be scheduled by the Part they extend.

**Placement notes from the verification pass.** The skeptic agreed with every "missing/partial" call but recommended a smaller footprint for several items: **G12** should be split (PCIe-link monitoring as Ch. 66 §66.4.4, the RAS/ECC/SMI material into Ch. 6 and the G07 chapter) rather than a standalone chapter; **G21** belongs in Ch. 71 (§71.4.4, exporting the binary log to a unified timeline), not Ch. 61; **G36** is one chapter in Part VII directly after Ch. 41, whose busy-poll-vs-block fork is exactly the split between its two halves; **G70** is one AMD companion appendix plus a per-generation section in Appendix E, not two appendices; **G75** (DRAM subsystem), **G84** (L2/L3 hygiene and redundancy) and **G92** (compression, RNG) fit as sections (Ch. 7/16, Ch. 51/60, Ch. 75/Appendix L) because they are mental model plus one measurement with few C++ techniques; **G32** sits best between Ch. 75 and Ch. 76 and **G33** after Ch. 74 (and after the G38 risk gate, which precedes the gateway on the order path). With those adjustments the shortlist above still stands at roughly 20 new chapters. Full per-item verdicts, grep evidence, refined placements and priority reasons are in [`2026-10-30-gap-verification.md`](2026-10-30-gap-verification.md).
