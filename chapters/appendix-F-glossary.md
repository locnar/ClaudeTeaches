# Appendix F — Glossary (HFT & Microarchitecture Terms)

> A quick reference for the acronyms and jargon used throughout the book. Each entry is a line or two with a pointer to the chapter that **defines and measures** it — follow the pointer for the real treatment; this is a reminder, not a substitute. Grouped by domain: trading (F.1), microarchitecture (F.2), memory & OS (F.3), concurrency (F.4), C++/toolchain (F.5), networking/I-O/transport (F.6), hardware acceleration & devices (F.7), and security/isolation/operations (F.8). Within each group, alphabetical.

## F.1 Trading & market-structure terms

- **A/B feed arbitration** — exchanges send market data on two redundant multicast lines (A and B); the feed handler takes whichever packet arrives first per sequence number and uses the other to fill gaps. (Ch. 70, 72; Appendix J.4)
- **BBO / Top-of-book** — Best Bid and Offer: the highest bid and lowest ask currently resting. The most-watched, most-updated level of the book. (Ch. 32; Appendix J.2)
- **Colocation (colo)** — placing your servers in the same datacenter as the exchange's matching engine to minimize wire distance (single-digit µs); physical proximity no software can substitute for. (Appendix K.1; Ch. 80 for the fabric and switches between you and the venue; Appendix A.7 for the cloud contrast)
- **Coordinated omission / HdrHistogram** — the benchmarking error of letting a slow response delay the *next* request, so the stall is counted once instead of for every request that should have been issued meanwhile — it hides exactly the tail you care about; **HdrHistogram** is the fixed-precision histogram (with a correction for it) used to record p99/p99.9 without allocation. (Ch. 3; Appendix L)
- **Cross-connect** — the physical fiber (or copper) run from your cage to the venue's access switch inside the colocation facility, typically latency-equalized so every participant's run is the same length. (Appendix K.1–K.2)
- **Dark feed** — a market-data channel that has stopped arriving with no sequence gap and no drop counter — typically a lapsed multicast membership (no IGMP querier on the VLAN, a refused join, `rp_filter`) or an upstream prune; detected only by time (per-channel last-packet age against the venue's heartbeat cadence) and by comparing the A and B lines. (Ch. 74, 72 §72.4.4)
- **Drop-copy** — a real-time copy of your order/fill activity fed to risk and compliance systems, separate from the order-entry session; the independent ledger that order-state recovery reconciles against and that the position-drift kill trigger compares with. (Ch. 105 §105.2.4, §105.4.5; Ch. 104 §104.4.5; Ch. 69, 101 — where it appears as a session on the RX / untrusted-input path)
- **FAST** — FIX Adapted for STreaming: a compression encoding for market data (templates, implicit fields). (Ch. 70; Appendix J.5)
- **Feed handler** — the component that decodes the exchange's market-data feed off the wire, normalizes it, handles sequencing/gaps, and publishes a clean stream. (Ch. 70, 103; Appendix J.7)
- **HFT** — High-Frequency Trading: automated, latency-sensitive trading (market making, arbitrage) whose edge depends on reacting to market data within microseconds — the domain framing of this book and the reason its north-star metric is tail tick-to-trade latency. (Ch. 1; Appendix J)
- **FIX** — Financial Information eXchange: a tag-value text protocol for order entry and (FIX/FAST) market data. Verbose; often replaced on the hot path by binary encodings. (Ch. 70; Appendix J.5)
- **ITCH** — Nasdaq's binary market-data feed protocol (fixed-layout messages); the canonical zero-copy decode target. (Ch. 70; Appendix J.5)
- **IOC / FOK / TIF** — Time-In-Force variants: **Day** rests until the close, **IOC** (Immediate-Or-Cancel) takes what is available now and cancels the rest, **FOK** (Fill-Or-Kill) fills entirely or not at all; part of the order state machine the gateway tracks. (Appendix J.3)
- **Jitter** — run-to-run variation in the latency of the *same* operation (frequency changes, C-state exits, interrupts, migrations, GC); the enemy of the tail, measured as a distribution with `cyclictest` and the §120.3 decomposition. (Ch. 1, 6, 59)
- **Kill-switch** — a control that immediately stops new orders and cancels working ones to reach a safe state on failure; mandated by market-access risk rules (SEC Rule 15c3-5, MiFID II RTS 6 Art. 12). Must live in a *surviving* process (the order gateway); triggered by runtime monitors (reject rate, position drift vs drop-copy, drawdown, feed staleness, heartbeat loss, operator) writing one word the gate reads; enumerates working orders via a live bitmap (4 µs for 400 orders) and uses the venue's mass cancel where offered. (Ch. 104 §104.2.4, §104.4.5–§104.4.6; Ch. 103 §103.4.4; Ch. 101)
- **Pre-trade risk gate** — the mandatory per-order check inventory (SEC 15c3-5, MiFID II RTS 6, venue rules: size/notional, price collar, position and open-exposure limits, credit, rate throttles, duplicate and self-match prevention, enable flags, the kill word) evaluated as a pure function over flat per-symbol/per-account records in ~11–15 ns, with checked arithmetic and a bitmask verdict journaled with its inputs. (Ch. 104)
- **Maker / Taker** — a *maker* posts resting liquidity (a limit order on the book); a *taker* removes it (a marketable order). Exchanges price the two differently (maker-taker fees). (Appendix J.1)
- **Matching engine** — the exchange component that matches incoming buy/sell orders against the book by price-time priority; the thing you race to. (Appendix J.1; Ch. 1; Ch. 80 for the fabric between you and it)
- **Microburst** — a sudden spike of packets (classically at the market open) that overflows NIC/switch buffers and manufactures the latency tail. (Ch. 70, 80)
- **NBBO** — National Best Bid and Offer: the best bid/ask across all US venues; the consolidated top-of-book. (Appendix J.2; Ch. 32 for the book data structure)
- **Order book / Limit order book (LOB)** — the set of resting buy/sell orders organized by price level; the core data structure a strategy reads and a venue matches against. (Ch. 32; Appendix J.2)
- **Order gateway** — the process that owns the venue session (order-entry connection, sequence numbers, the in-flight order table and working-order state) and executes the kill-switch even when a strategy crashes. (Ch. 103, 105; Appendix J.7)
- **OMS / EMS** — Order / Execution Management System: the components that own order state (submission → ack → fills/cancels → terminal state) and routing; in a latency-sensitive system the OMS role is the order gateway process. (Appendix J.3, J.7; Ch. 103)
- **OUCH** — Nasdaq's binary order-entry protocol (the order-entry counterpart to ITCH). (Ch. 70; Appendix J.5)
- **Price level / depth** — a *price level* is one price on one side of the book with its aggregate resting quantity (and queue of orders); *depth* is how many levels away from the BBO a feed or book carries. The level, not the order, is usually the unit of the hot data structure. (Ch. 32; Appendix J.2)
- **Reg NMS / MiFID II** — the US (Regulation National Market System: order protection, the NBBO, market-access risk rules) and EU (Markets in Financial Instruments Directive II: clock-sync and reporting mandates, RTS 25) regulatory frameworks that shape venue connectivity, timestamping precision and pre-trade risk. (Ch. 78; Appendix J.1, K.4, K.7)
- **SBE** — Simple Binary Encoding: a fixed-layout binary message encoding (FIX community) designed for zero-copy, branch-free decode. (Ch. 70; Appendix J.5)
- **Sequence gap** — a discontinuity in a feed's sequence numbers signaling lost message(s); the trigger for recovery. (Ch. 72)
- **SLI / SLO** — Service Level Indicator / Objective: the measured quantity (wire-to-wire p99.9 per venue, feed age, post-A/B gaps, oldest unacknowledged order age, PTP offset) and the budget it must meet, set *per session phase* because the open is a different regime; alerts fire on SLI symptoms with multi-window conditions and hysteresis, never on averaged percentiles. (Ch. 100 §100.4.4)
- **Snapshot / retransmission recovery** — refilling a feed gap from a full book image (snapshot) or a resend of specific missing messages (retransmission) — the recovery channels beyond A/B. (Ch. 72)
- **Stale-data kill** — pulling quotes and refusing to take on an instrument whose book age exceeds its phase threshold, whose invariants (crossed/locked/zero-size/collar) fail, or whose channel is dark on both lines — the market-data counterpart of the kill-switch, driven by one per-instrument flag the hot path reads with a predicted branch. (Ch. 72 §72.4.4, 74)
- **Tail latency / p99 / p99.9** — the high percentiles of the latency *distribution* (the slowest 1 % / 0.1 % of events); the quantity trading systems are judged on, because the mean hides the lost races. Always report and reason about the distribution, never the average. (Ch. 1, 3)
- **Tick-to-trade** — the end-to-end latency from a market-data packet arriving on the wire to the resulting order leaving on the wire; the book's north-star metric. (Ch. 1, 120; Appendix J.8)
- **Tick size** — the minimum price increment for an instrument; drives price representation (scaled integers) and book layout. (Ch. 35)
- **Trace header / correlation ID** — a fixed 56-byte structure carried in every ring slot: the message's own identity as the join key (venue sequence number for market data, `ClOrdID` for orders), a parent ID linking an order to the tick that caused it, and a vector of raw-TSC stage stamps; the mechanism behind per-stage, per-message budget attribution across processes. (Ch. 99)
- **`SendingTime` / `TransactTime` / `TrdRegTSTimeIn`·`Out` / `TrdRegTSTimePriority`** — venue-written UTC timestamps (CME MDP 3.0 and iLink 3; Eurex EOBI/ETI; Nasdaq ITCH/OUCH carry nanoseconds since midnight) that let a desk split a lost race into *you* (NIC-to-NIC), *the wire* (TX → gateway-in) and *them* (gateway-in → matching engine); compared to PHC stamps, never to the TSC. (Ch. 99 §99.4.5, 78; Appendix J.5)
- **One-way feed latency** — venue `SendingTime`/`TransactTime` to your NIC's hardware RX stamp, both on PTP-disciplined UTC; flat on a co-located box, a spike on one packet with flat neighbours is the venue's or the fabric's, not yours. (Ch. 99 §99.4.5)


- **Session layer (FIX / SoupBinTCP / iLink 3 / BOE)** — the protocol state machine beneath the orders: Logon with agreed sequence numbers, Heartbeat/TestRequest liveness, `MsgSeqNum` both ways, `ResendRequest`/`SequenceReset-GapFill` recovery, `PossDupFlag` resends; built as a deterministic machine with a persisted 32-byte sequence page and a shared-memory resend store, encode ~50–95 ns, reconnect + resync 123–291 µs on loopback. (Ch. 106 §106.2.1–§106.2.2, §106.3)
- **PossDup / GapFill** — `43=Y` marks a resent application message so a venue that already has it ignores it by `ClOrdID`; `35=4 123=Y 36=N` skips a run of administrative messages instead of resending them; IOCs and cancelled orders are gap-filled, never resent. (Ch. 106 §106.4.3, Ch. 105 §105.2.3)
- **Cancel-on-disconnect (COD)** — a per-session venue setting that cancels the session's working orders when the venue declares the session dead (its own heartbeat/TCP timeout, sometimes after a grace delay); the firm's last safety net on failover — it fired at 3 ms in the venue double of Ch. 112 §112.3, before a standby with a 10 ms lease could cancel, and never with a 2 ms lease. Recorded per session; the takeover sequence branches on it. (Ch. 106 §106.4.4, Ch. 112 §112.2.4)
- **Markout** — the signed mid-price change at +1 / +10 / +100 ms after a fill: the realised adverse selection; fills won slowly are the adversely selected ones. Computed by the post-trade join. (Ch. 120 §120.4.5)
- **Race outcome / race margin** — for a liquidity-taking order, whether ours reached the venue before the competing order that took the same liquidity, and by how many ns, from the venue's own timestamps; the fill-rate-vs-reaction-latency curve's knee is the measured value of a nanosecond. (Ch. 120 §120.4.5)
- **Venue conformance (certification) session** — the scripted session scenarios (sequence gaps at logon, resend across a reconnect, heartbeat miss, mass cancel, COD) a new order-entry build must pass in the venue's test environment before connecting; treated as a release step with evidence and as a fault-injecting venue double with ground truth. (Ch. 119 §119.4.6, Ch. 116 §116.4.3)
- **Market-by-order (MBO, L3) vs market-by-price (MBP, L2) feed** — an MBO feed carries every order's add/modify/cancel/execute with a venue-assigned id (ITCH, CME MBO), so the book holds orders and an id map and knows queue position; an MBP feed carries per-level aggregates ("level p now has quantity q"), ten times cheaper per message and a hundred times smaller in state, and cannot tell you what is ahead of your order. (Ch. 33 §33.2.1)
- **Quote-pull latency** — the market maker's critical path: from the underlying tick that made a resting quote wrong (hardware RX stamp) to the first cancel of the dangerous side on the wire (hardware TX stamp); the second row in the ledger beside tick-to-trade, reported per series class. (Ch. 107 §107.4.2)
- **Cancel-first discipline** — on a requote, the cancel of the side the market moved toward is the first message on the wire, then the other side, then the replacements; never a replacement before its cancel is sent. (Ch. 107 §107.2.1)
- **Requote fan-out / delta threshold / hysteresis** — one underlying tick invalidates N series; the engine requotes only series whose theo moved past an asymmetric threshold measured against the theo at the *last requote*, under a per-tick message budget. (Ch. 107 §107.2.2–§107.2.3)
- **Mass quote / mass cancel / purge port** — venue primitives that carry up to *n* two-sided quotes, or cancel every order in a scope, in one message; the purge port is a separate session for mass cancels that bypasses a wedged order port and is owned by the supervisor. (Ch. 107 §107.2.4)
- **Quote protection (market-maker protection)** — a venue-side circuit breaker that pulls or re-prices a maker's quotes after a configured number of fills/notional in a window; configured, tested at the gate, treated as a kill when it fires. (Ch. 107 §107.2.4)
- **Symbology / dense instrument id** — the start-of-day mapping from every venue's key (8-byte symbol, `SecurityID`, `Locate`, ISIN) to one `[0, N)` internal id, looked up once at the edge and used as the array index everywhere after. (Ch. 73 §73.2.2)
- **Consolidated top (vs NBBO)** — the ranking of per-venue BBO lines with a venue bitmask and O(1) back-out that *your* system computes from direct feeds; not the SIP's regulatory NBBO, which has its own rules and is kept as a separate line. (Ch. 73 §73.2.3)
- **Smart order routing (SOR)** — the venue choice for a marketable order as a score over price, fee/rebate, fill probability and *measured one-way latency*, with coordinated departures for multi-venue sends. (Ch. 73 §73.4.5)
- **Pro-rata allocation** — a matching rule that fills resting orders at a level in proportion to size rather than time (with a FIFO remainder, and on some venues a FIFO top); changes queue priority from time to size and the per-trade cost to O(orders at the level). (Ch. 117 §117.2.2; Appendix J.1)
- **Self-trade prevention (STP) modes** — cancel-newest / cancel-oldest / cancel-both / decrement-and-cancel: what the venue does when an incoming order would match the same firm's resting order; applied at match time. (Ch. 117 §117.2.2)
- **Auction uncross** — the single price maximising executable volume (tie: minimum imbalance) at which accumulated call-phase orders execute; its dissemination is the market-open microburst. (Ch. 117 §117.4.3)
- **Exchange simulator / latency model** — a deterministic venue (matching core, allocation rule, STP, auctions) whose delays are sampled from *empirical* captured ack/fill/dissemination distributions with the tail intact, and whose faults are a seeded schedule. (Ch. 117)
- **Kill functionality (RTS 6)** — the regulatory name for the kill-switch requirement: cancel all unexecuted orders immediately, from a mechanism that survives the algorithm's failure. (Appendix K.8; Ch. 104, 107)
- **Order-to-trade ratio (OTR)** — messages (orders, modifies, cancels) divided by executions, capped per instrument by venues and MiFID II Art. 48; a requote storm breaches it. (Appendix K.8; Ch. 107 §107.2.2)
- **CAT / RTS 24 record** — the mandated per-event order record (fields, timestamps, linkage keys, retention) that is the journal's schema. (Appendix K.8; Ch. 109)
- **Queue position** — the quantity resting ahead of your order at its price level, in price-time priority; known only from an MBO book, and the input every passive strategy prices. (Ch. 33 §33.2.1)
- **Order-flow imbalance (OFI)** — the Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov signal: the net change in resting quantity at the best bid minus that at the best ask between two BBO snapshots, six integer compares and adds per quote; a running sum that is exact. (Ch. 40 §40.4.4)
- **Microprice** — the size-weighted mid `(bid·askQ + ask·bidQ) / (askQ + bidQ)`, computed from the BBO line with integer numerator and denominator and one division at read time. (Ch. 40 §40.4.4)
- **VWAP** — volume-weighted average price, `Σ px·qty / Σ qty`, kept as two integer accumulators and divided only when read. (Ch. 40 §40.4.4)
- **Layout-pinned parsing** — parsing a text message (a FIX NewOrderSingle on a given session) by *expecting* each tag in its known order with one small compare, scanning only the values and skipping the scan on fixed-width fields, with a generic parser as the fallback when the expectation fails; the structural 1.4× on the FIX decode path. (Ch. 71 §71.4.4)

## F.2 Microarchitecture terms

- **Row buffer / row hit / row conflict** — each DRAM bank holds one open row (~8 KiB per rank) in its sense amplifiers; a column access to the open row is a *row hit* (tCL ≈ 14 ns at DDR4-3200), to a closed bank a *row miss* (+tRCD), to a bank with a different row open a *row conflict* (+tRP + tRCD) — the ~30 % bimodality inside "~80 ns" that no cache counter shows. (Ch. 9 §9.2.2, §9.3)
- **Bank group / bank / rank / channel** — the DRAM hierarchy below the memory controller: a channel carries DIMMs, each rank is a set of chips answering together, each chip has bank groups of banks, each bank has rows; accesses to different banks/channels proceed in parallel, to one bank serialize. The reference machine has 8 DDR4-3200 channels per socket. (Ch. 9 §9.2.1)
- **Loaded latency (the knee)** — DRAM access latency as a function of the bandwidth everyone else is using on the same channels; flat to ~60 % of peak, rising per Little's law to 2× at the knee (~70 %) and beyond — a capture writer on another core slows the hot core's misses with no counter on the hot core changing. Measured with `mlc --loaded_latency`. (Ch. 9 §9.2.3, §9.3)
- **tRFC / refresh stall** — the time a rank is unavailable while refreshing (~350–550 ns for 8–16 Gb DDR4, every 7.8 µs per rank; halved interval above 85 °C); a miss landing on a refreshing rank waits — a ~0.5 µs tail mode. (Ch. 9 §9.2.4)
- **Hardware prefetcher control (MSR 0x1A4)** — `IA32_MISC_FEATURE_CONTROL`: four per-core bits disabling the L2 streamer, the L2 adjacent-line, the DCU streamer and the DCU IP prefetchers (1 = off); plus the uncore LLC/XPT/UPI prefetch switches in firmware and AMD's socket-wide BIOS switches. Off on hot cores where measured, on for batch cores, re-applied at boot, read back at the gate. (Ch. 12 §12.4.3; Appendix C.1, C.3)
- **BMI / BMI2** — Bit Manipulation Instruction sets: `tzcnt`/`lzcnt`/`popcnt`, `blsr`, `pdep`/`pext` — single-instruction bit tricks for bitmap-based book occupancy and free lists; enabled by `-march=` (Haswell+ / x86-64-v3). (Ch. 37, Appendix D.2)
- **BTB** — Branch Target Buffer: predicts the *target* of indirect branches/calls (vtables, function pointers — Ch. 16); a BTB miss is an indirect-branch mispredict. (Ch. 15, 16)
- **Cache line** — the unit of transfer between cache levels and of coherence: 64 bytes on x86-64 (128 on some ARM cores, 256 on A64FX); every layout, padding and false-sharing decision is about which data shares a line. (Ch. 8 — the memory hierarchy; Ch. 11, 44; Appendix A.3)
- **DSB / µop cache** — Decoded Stream Buffer: caches decoded µops so the front-end skips re-decoding hot code; staying in the DSB avoids legacy-decode front-end stalls. (Ch. 14)
- **Flame graph** — a visualization of sampled stacks (`perf record -g` → `stackcollapse`/`flamegraph.pl`) in which width is time: the fastest way to see where a hot path's cycles go. (Ch. 2)
- **HITM** — "Hit Modified": a cache load that hits a line *modified* in another core's cache, forcing a cross-core transfer — the signature of false sharing / contention (`perf c2c`). (Ch. 44)
- **ILP** — Instruction-Level Parallelism: how many independent instructions the out-of-order core can have in flight at once; a long dependency chain serializes it, independent accumulators/lanes restore it. (Ch. 13)
- **IPC** — Instructions Per Cycle: throughput measure; low IPC signals stalls (cache/branch/front-end). The starting point of top-down analysis. Not the inter-process sense — see **IPC (inter-process communication)** in F.6. (Ch. 2, 13)
- **LFB** — Line Fill Buffer (MSHR): tracks outstanding cache misses; its count bounds **MLP** (how many misses can be in flight). Running out of LFBs stalls further misses. (Ch. 12)
- **LLC** — Last-Level Cache: the socket-shared L3; an LLC miss is a DRAM access, so `LLC-load-misses` (and LLC MPKI) is the counter that matters most. Partitionable with CAT. (Ch. 8, 2, 58)
- **MESI / MOESI** — cache-coherence protocols (Modified/Exclusive/Shared/Invalid, +Owned) that keep per-core caches consistent; the machinery behind HITM and false-sharing cost. (Ch. 8, 44)
- **MLP** — Memory-Level Parallelism: how many independent cache misses are serviced concurrently; high MLP hides DRAM latency — prefetching and independent chains raise it. (Ch. 12)
- **MPKI** — Misses Per Kilo-Instruction: cache (or TLB/branch) misses normalized to 1,000 retired instructions — the form in which miss counts are comparable across runs and against a baseline. (Ch. 2, 8)
- **µop (micro-op)** — the internal RISC-like operation x86 instructions decode into; the unit the back-end actually executes. (Ch. 13, 14)
- **PEBS** — Processor Event-Based Sampling: PMU sampling that records the precise instruction, address and latency of the sampled event (the basis of `perf mem`/`perf c2c` and skid-free attribution). (Ch. 2)
- **PMU** — Performance Monitoring Unit: the per-core hardware counters (`cycles`, `instructions`, cache/branch/TLB events) read through `perf_events`/`perf stat`; the instrument behind "measure first" and top-down analysis. (Ch. 2)
- **Prefetcher** — hardware that predicts and pre-loads cache lines ahead of demand; software prefetch (`__builtin_prefetch`) supplements it for irregular patterns. (Ch. 8, 12)
- **ROB** — Re-Order Buffer: holds in-flight instructions for out-of-order, speculative execution and in-order retirement; its size bounds the out-of-order window, and it is one of the resources two SMT siblings partition. (Ch. 13, 57)
- **SIMD / SSE / AVX / AVX-512** — Single Instruction, Multiple Data: vector instructions on 128/256/512-bit registers; auto-vectorization, intrinsics, layout-for-vectorization, and the AVX frequency-license (downclocking) caveat. (Ch. 39; Appendix A.2 for NEON/SVE)
- **SMT / Hyper-Threading** — Simultaneous Multi-Threading: two hardware threads sharing one core's resources (front-end, execution ports, L1/L2); a throughput-vs-latency trade — HFT hot cores usually run the sibling idle or SMT off. (Ch. 57)
- **STLB** — Second-level (shared) TLB: the larger, slower unified TLB behind the split L1 iTLB/dTLB; an STLB miss is a page-table walk. Huge pages multiply its reach. (Ch. 17)
- **Store buffer** — a per-core buffer holding pending stores before they reach cache; the source of x86-TSO's one permitted reordering (store→load). (Ch. 41)
- **TLB** — Translation Lookaside Buffer: caches virtual→physical page translations; a TLB miss triggers a page-table walk. Huge pages reduce TLB pressure. (Ch. 17)
- **Top-down analysis** — a PMU methodology attributing each cycle to front-end-bound / back-end-bound / bad-speculation / retiring, to find the dominant bottleneck. (Ch. 2)
- **TSC / `rdtsc` / `rdtscp`** — Time Stamp Counter: the per-core cycle counter read by `rdtsc` (`rdtscp` additionally waits for prior instructions to complete and returns the core ID); the *invariant* TSC ticks at a constant rate regardless of frequency scaling, which makes it the cheapest hot-path clock — once calibrated against a reference. (Ch. 20; Appendix A.4 for `CNTVCT_EL0`)
- **Uncore PMU (IMC / CHA / UPI / IIO / M2M)** — the counters outside the core: memory controller CAS counts (→ DRAM bandwidth), caching/home agent LLC lookups by source, cross-socket flits, PCIe inbound/outbound data per stack. Socket-scoped, counting-only, programmed through a housekeeping CPU — so they never touch an isolated core. (Ch. 2 §2.4.4, 18, 76, 87)
- **IBS (Instruction-Based Sampling)** — AMD's precise sampler (Zen has no PEBS): tags one µop every *N* and records its address, data source, latency and TLB behaviour with zero skid; what `perf mem`/`perf c2c` use on Zen. (Ch. 2 §2.4.4)
- **RAPL** — Running Average Power Limit: the energy MSRs (`power/energy-pkg/`, `-ram/`, `-cores/`) that say whether the socket is power-limited; beside the AVX frequency-licence events (`core_power.lvl*_turbo_license`) the counter form of the downclocking caveat. (Ch. 2 §2.4.4, 6, 39, 114)
- **Intel PT (Processor Trace)** — hardware recording of every taken branch of a CPU into a per-CPU buffer at 2–15 % slowdown, frozen on a trigger (`perf record -e intel_pt//u --snapshot`) and decoded offline to the instruction; the deepest answer to "what ran during the 41 µs". (Ch. 2 §2.4.4, 113 §113.4.3)
- **LBR (Last Branch Record)** — 32 taken branches the CPU records for free; read by `perf` at a sample (`--call-graph lbr`, `-j any,u`) as a cheap, shallow call history and as BOLT's profile input. (Ch. 2, 27, 120 §120.4.4)
- **`rdpmc`** — user-space read of a core performance counter: `perf_event_open` once from the thread, `mmap` the event page, then a seqlock'd `rdpmc` using the page's index — ~20–40 cycles, no syscall, no IPI; the always-on per-stage counter instrument beside the TSC stamp. (Ch. 120 §120.4.4)
- **Debug registers / hardware data breakpoint** — the four x86 debug-address registers; armed on a live PID from a sidecar via `perf_event_open(PERF_TYPE_BREAKPOINT, HW_BREAKPOINT_W)` they cost the target nothing until the write and then deliver the writer's IP — the production answer to "who corrupted this?". (Ch. 5 §5.4.5)
- **CCD / CCX** — AMD *core complex die* / *core complex*: the chiplet of 8 (16 for Zen 4c/5c) cores sharing one private 32 MiB L3; since Zen 3 one CCD = one CCX. Two cores in one CCD exchange a cache line in ~19 ns, across CCDs in ~85–100 ns through the I/O die. (Ch. 19, Appendix Q)
- **Infinity Fabric / GMI / xGMI** — AMD's on-package (GMI) and socket-to-socket (xGMI) interconnect between CCDs, the I/O die and sockets; its clock (FCLK) is the AMD "uncore" clock to pin. (Ch. 19, 60; Appendix Q.7)
- **IOD (I/O die)** — the AMD die carrying the memory controllers, PCIe root ports and the Infinity Fabric switch, in four quadrants that NPS4 exposes as NUMA nodes; every cross-CCD transfer and every NIC DMA passes through it. (Ch. 19)
- **Tile / EMIB** — Intel's compute and I/O chiplets (Sapphire/Emerald/Granite Rapids) bridged by *Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge* into one logical mesh; a request that crosses a bridge pays ~5–15 ns more. (Ch. 19, Appendix R)
- **SNC / NPS** — *Sub-NUMA Clustering* (Intel: SNC2/SNC3) and *Nodes Per Socket* (AMD: NPS1/2/4): firmware modes that split one socket into several NUMA nodes along its L3 slices / memory-controller groups; "L3 cache as NUMA domain" (AMD) reports every CCD as a node. (Ch. 18 §18.2.3, Ch. 19 §19.4.2)
- **Core-to-core latency map** — the matrix of one-way cache-line transfer latencies over every CPU pair, measured by a ping-pong harness and read as blocks (SMT sibling < same L3 domain < cross-domain < cross-socket); the placement input for every queue, pipeline stage and IRQ. (Ch. 19 §19.3, Ch. 56 §56.4.3, Appendix L.7)
- **V-Cache (3D V-Cache, "X3D")** — AMD's stacked SRAM adding 64 MiB to one CCD's L3 (96 MiB total) at a few hundred MHz lower clock on that CCD. (Ch. 7, 19; Appendix Q, R)
- **P-core / E-core, Thread Director** — Intel's hybrid client cores since Alder Lake (performance cores with SMT and the full ISA; efficiency cores in 4-core clusters sharing an L2, no SMT, no AVX-512) and the hardware hint table the scheduler uses to place threads; a pinning hazard on x86 that Appendix A.5 attributes to ARM only. (Ch. 7 §7.2.4, §7.4.4)
- **Turbo ratio / bin; PL1 / PL2 (RAPL); ICCmax** — the per-active-core-count frequency multiplier table and its 100 MHz steps; the package's long-term and short-term power limits (mirrored in `/sys/class/powercap`) and the voltage-regulator current limit, all of which pull the clock below the requested ratio — package-scope, so a neighbour's load throttles the hot core. (Ch. 7 §7.2.3, Ch. 60 §60.2.4)
- **Precision Boost (PB2) / PBO / Curve Optimizer** — AMD's continuous boost controller (power, current and thermal limits rather than a ratio table), the firmware option that raises those limits, and the per-core voltage-offset table. (Ch. 7 §7.2.3, Appendix Q.7)
- **HWP / EPP** — *Hardware P-states* (the core picks its frequency inside an OS-written `[min, max]` window) and the *energy-performance preference* hint (0 = performance … 255 = power) that biases it; "make the window a point, EPP = performance" is the deterministic posture. (Ch. 60 §60.2.3, §60.4.3)
- **AVX frequency licence** — the per-core frequency level (0 / 1 / 2) a core drops to while executing wide-vector instructions, persisting ~ms and shared with the SMT sibling; Intel only — Zen has none. (Ch. 39 §39.2, Ch. 60 §60.2.6, Appendix Q.4)
- **Uncore frequency (UFS)** — the clock of the L3 slices, mesh/ring, memory controllers and PCIe root, scaled independently of the cores on Intel server parts; at its floor after a lull, the first L3 hit costs ~2×. Pin min = max. (Ch. 60 §60.2.5)
- **`rdpru`** — AMD's user-space instruction (Zen 2+) reading `MPERF`/`APERF` without an MSR access: an IPI-free effective-frequency read from the hot thread. (Ch. 60, Appendix Q.5)
## F.3 Memory & OS terms

- **EEVDF** — Earliest Eligible Virtual Deadline First, the `SCHED_OTHER` fair scheduler since Linux 6.6 (CFS before): a task is *eligible* when its *lag* (entitlement minus received service) is ≥ 0, and among eligible tasks the one with the earliest *virtual deadline* (eligible time + requested slice ÷ weight) runs; the slice is settable per task with `sched_setattr(sched_runtime)` — short for latency-sensitive housekeeping, long for batch. The old `sched_min_granularity_ns`-family sysctls are gone (`/sys/kernel/debug/sched/base_slice_ns`). (Ch. 59 §59.2.1)
- **CBS / GRUB (`SCHED_DEADLINE`)** — the Constant Bandwidth Server throttles a deadline task once it has consumed its `sched_runtime` in the current `sched_period`, so a misbehaving task cannot exceed what it declared; admission control (`EBUSY`) refuses a task whose bandwidth would push the root domain past `sched_rt_runtime_us`; GRUB (`SCHED_FLAG_RECLAIM`) lets a throttled task use the domain's unused deadline bandwidth. Admission is per root domain (make the core a cpuset partition), a DEADLINE task may not be pinned narrower than its domain (`EPERM`) nor clone (`EAGAIN`), and CBS accounting is tick-granular unless `HRTICK_DL`. (Ch. 59 §59.2.2, §59.4.3)
- **Fair deadline server** — the Linux 6.12 replacement for the RT throttle: a per-CPU `SCHED_DEADLINE` server that runs fair-class tasks only when some are runnable, so a lone `SCHED_FIFO` spinner on an isolated core is never stalled. (Ch. 59 §59.4.2)
- **`stalld`** — a housekeeping-core daemon that detects a task starved on a CPU by an RT spinner and boosts it to `SCHED_DEADLINE` for a tiny budget (e.g. 20 µs/s) so it progresses — the selective alternative to the RT throttle's safety net; a boost is a configuration error to find. (Ch. 59 §59.4.3)
- **Threaded IRQs (`threadirqs`, PREEMPT_RT)** — every interrupt handler runs as a `SCHED_FIFO 50` kernel thread (`irq/N-name`) that can be pinned and prioritized below the hot threads; created with the default affinity, so pin them. (Ch. 59 §59.4.3, §59.5)
- **`cpu.max` / CFS bandwidth quota** — the cgroup v2 CPU *quota* (`cpu.cfs_quota_us` in v1; Docker `--cpus`, Kubernetes `limits.cpu`, systemd `CPUQuota=`): the group may run *q* µs per period *p* and is then stopped until the period ends — a 50 % quota is a measured 49 ms stall every 100 ms. Confine with `cpuset`, prioritize with `cpu.weight`, never quota a latency-sensitive group; `nr_throttled` must read 0. (Ch. 59 §59.4.4)
- **Double-mapped ("magic") ring** — a byte ring backed by a `memfd` (or hugetlbfs file) mapped twice, back to back, so byte `size + k` aliases byte `k` and a message straddling the end is contiguous — no split copy, no bounce buffer, no wrap branch; costs virtual address space and a second TLB entry per page. (Ch. 34 §34.4.3)
- **`MADV_POPULATE_READ`/`WRITE`, `MCL_ONFAULT`, `MADV_COLLAPSE`** — kernel 5.14's way to pre-fault an *existing* range in one call (~10× cheaper per page than a user-space touch loop); `mlockall`'s 4.4 flag that locks pages as they fault instead of populating every future mapping (thread stacks, libraries) up front; kernel 6.1's synchronous, on-demand THP collapse of a range at setup instead of `khugepaged`'s at 10:00. (Ch. 34 §34.4.3)
- **`userfaultfd`** — page faults on a registered range delivered to a user-space thread that supplies the page; ~3 µs per fault — a cold-path tool for lazy checkpoint restore, never a hot-path one; `vm.unprivileged_userfaultfd=0` since 5.11. (Ch. 34 §34.4.3)
- **TLB shootdown (self-inflicted)** — when any thread of a process `munmap`s, `mprotect`s or `MADV_DONTNEED`s, the kernel invalidates the stale entries on every CPU running the process by IPI — the hot core included (the `TLB` row of `/proc/interrupts`); a separate process's churn sends none. Map once, never unmap in-session, churn in another process. (Ch. 34 §34.5; Ch. 61)
- **`Type=notify` / `READY=1` / `WatchdogSec=`** — the systemd protocol by which the trading unit reports readiness *after* warm-up and the gate (one datagram on `$NOTIFY_SOCKET`), so dependants start only then and a hung warm-up fails loudly (`TimeoutStartSec`); `WATCHDOG=1` from the supervisor loop keeps systemd as the supervisor of last resort. (Ch. 103 §103.4.9)
- **`kexec`** — booting a new kernel from the running one, skipping firmware POST: ~5 s instead of ~2 min for a software reboot; signed images only under lockdown. Part of the boot-to-ready budget. (Ch. 103 §103.4.9)
- **Live patching (`kpatch`/`livepatch`)** — replacing kernel functions at run time via `ftrace`'s `text_poke`: an IPI to every CPU and a per-task consistency transition that a syscall-free task never completes; a maintenance-window event, never in-session. (Ch. 103 §103.4.9)
- **Timer slack** — the per-thread window (`timer_slack_ns`, default 50 µs for `SCHED_OTHER`, 0 for RT policies) within which the kernel may deliver a sleep's wake-up late to coalesce timers; applies to `nanosleep`, futex and `epoll_wait` timeouts, not to `timerfd`. Set to 1 with `prctl(PR_SET_TIMERSLACK)`. (Ch. 55 §55.2.4, §55.3)
- **Timing wheel (hierarchical)** — a deadline queue of 2ᵇ slots per level, each an intrusive list; arm and cancel are O(1), a poll costs O(1) per tick plus the fires, far timers cascade down a level on wrap (bounded per poll, or coarsened as the Linux wheel does since 4.8). The structure for the TTL-shaped bulk of a trading engine's 10⁴–10⁶ armed timers. (Ch. 55 §55.2.2, §55.4.1)
- **Slot map / generational handle** — dense values + sparse indirection + free list + per-slot generation; a 64-bit handle (generation ≪ 32 | index) dereferenced by one indexed load and validated by one compare, so a stale handle returns null instead of a reused object. Relocatable, allocation-free, meaningful across processes. The in-flight order table and the timer queue are slot maps. (Ch. 32 §32.4.4; Ch. 105 §105.2.1; Ch. 55 §55.4.1)
- **RT throttle** — `sched_rt_runtime_us`/`sched_rt_period_us` (default 950 000 / 1 000 000): the kernel suspends all `SCHED_FIFO`/`RR` tasks on a CPU for the remainder of each period once they have consumed the runtime, so a busy-polling RT thread on a non-isolated core stalls ~50 ms per second. Disabled (−1) on isolated cores. (Ch. 55 §55.3; Ch. 59 §59.5)
- **Arena / pool / slab allocator** — allocation strategies that replace `malloc` on the hot path: an *arena* hands out bump-pointer slices of one large block and frees them together; a *pool* recycles fixed-size objects through a free list; a *slab* is a pool per size class. Zero-allocation steady state is built from these. (Ch. 31)
- **cgroup** — Linux control groups: partition CPU/memory/I-O among process groups; used to confine housekeeping (and everything non-hot) off the isolated cores. (Ch. 59, Appendix C)
- **C-states / P-states** — processor *idle* states (C0 active, C1 … C6 progressively deeper sleep with µs-to-100-µs exit latency on the first event after idle) and *performance* states (frequency/voltage steps, turbo); both are variance sources a quiet box pins down — cap at C1 or `idle=poll`, fixed frequency, `performance` governor. (Ch. 6; Appendix C.1–C.3, E.1)
- **Context switch** — the kernel swapping the thread running on a core: a *direct* cost (save/restore state, run the scheduler) of ~µs and an *indirect* one — the incoming thread runs with cold caches, TLB and branch predictors; a single involuntary switch blows a tick-to-trade budget, hence pinning and never blocking on the hot path. (Ch. 54; Appendix E)
- **Futex** — Fast Userspace muTEX: the Linux primitive under mutexes/condvars; uncontended lock/unlock stays in userspace (fast), contention falls into a syscall to sleep/wake. (Ch. 43)
- **Huge pages** — larger page sizes (2 MiB, 1 GiB) that cover more memory per TLB entry, cutting TLB misses (Ch. 17); *explicit* (reserved) vs *transparent* (THP, kernel-managed, can add jitter). (Ch. 17, Appendix C)
- **IPI (inter-processor interrupt) / cross-call** — one CPU interrupting another: reschedule (`RES`), function call (`CAL` — `smp_call_function`, used by perf, MSR reads, `text_poke`, RCU expedited grace periods, cpufreq), TLB shootdown (`TLB`), IRQ work (`IWI`). On an isolated core each is a few µs stolen from whatever message was in flight; the `/proc/interrupts` rows are the fingerprint, the Ch. 61 catalog names the sender and the knob. (Ch. 56, 61, 114)
- **IRQ affinity / `irqbalance`** — steering each device interrupt to chosen cores via `/proc/irq/N/smp_affinity` (and stopping the `irqbalance` daemon that would redistribute them) so NIC/NVMe/timer IRQs land on housekeeping cores, never the isolated hot core; managed IRQs reject the file and need `isolcpus=managed_irq`. (Ch. 56, 75; Appendix C.3)
- **`isolcpus`** — kernel cmdline parameter removing CPUs from the scheduler's general balancing so only explicitly-pinned threads run there; the foundation of a quiet core. (Ch. 59, Appendix C)
- **KPTI** — Kernel Page-Table Isolation: the Meltdown mitigation that gives the kernel its own page tables, so every syscall/interrupt switches `CR3` and flushes or re-tags the TLB — the main reason a null syscall costs ~150–400 ns with mitigations on vs ~50–100 ns off. (Ch. 6, 54; Appendix E)
- **`mlockall`** — locks a process's pages resident in RAM so they're never swapped or paged out, eliminating page-fault stalls on the hot path. (Ch. 34)
- **`nohz_full`** — kernel feature stopping the periodic scheduler-tick interrupt on a core running a single task (tickless), removing that recurring jitter source. (Ch. 59, Appendix C)
- **Noise catalog (kernel)** — the finite set of things that can still execute on an `isolcpus`/`nohz_full` core — per-CPU kthreads, unbound workqueues, RCU and expedited grace periods, IPIs, managed device vectors, NMI/PMI, the memory-management daemons, the clocksource watchdog, SMIs — each with a trace fingerprint and a knob. (Ch. 61 §61.2)
- **NUMA** — Non-Uniform Memory Access: multi-socket systems where each CPU has faster access to its *local* memory node than to a remote node; node-local placement matters. (Ch. 18)
- **osnoise / timerlat / hwlat (`rtla`)** — the kernel's noise-attribution tracers: **osnoise** spins on a CPU and accounts every gap in its execution to IRQ, softirq, NMI, thread or `HW` (firmware); **timerlat** measures a periodic timer's IRQ and thread latency separately; **hwlat** spins with interrupts off to expose SMI/hardware stalls. All three stop the trace on a threshold so the ring buffer holds the spike; `rtla` is their front end. (Ch. 61 §61.3; Ch. 6 §6.4.4)
- **Page fault** — a trap when a touched virtual page isn't mapped/resident; *minor* (map a zero or already-resident page) costs ~0.3–1 µs for 4 KiB, *major* (from disk) far more — both forbidden on the hot path. (Ch. 30, 34)
- **PSI (pressure stall information)** — `/proc/pressure/{cpu,memory,io}`: the fraction of time tasks stalled waiting for a resource; near zero on the housekeeping side of a tuned box, rising when the consumers of the hot threads' rings are oversubscribed. (Ch. 114)
- **`rcu_nocbs`** — kernel cmdline offloading RCU callback processing off specified cores, keeping grace-period work off the isolated hot cores. (Ch. 59, Appendix C)
- **Shared memory (`mmap(MAP_SHARED)` / `shm_open` / `memfd_create`)** — a memory segment mapped into several processes' address spaces: the zero-copy data plane between feed-handler, strategy and gateway processes (single-writer rings, seqlock snapshots), with the pointers-across-address-spaces trap. (Ch. 34, 67, 103)
- **SMI (System Management Interrupt)** — a firmware interrupt that drops the core into System Management Mode: the OS is suspended, sees no interrupt and logs nothing; tens to hundreds of µs later it resumes. Invisible to `/proc/interrupts`; counted by `MSR_SMI_COUNT` (0x34, read from a housekeeping CPU) and by `osnoise`'s `HW` column; disabled only in the BIOS. (Ch. 6 §6.4.4, 61, 114; Appendix C.1)
- **Swappiness / swap** — the kernel's willingness to page memory to disk; on a trading box, disabled entirely (a swapped hot page is a multi-ms fault). (Ch. 30, Appendix C)
- **THP** — Transparent Huge Pages: kernel-managed automatic huge pages; convenient, but fault-time 2 MiB allocation (with the direct compaction the `defrag` setting may allow) and the background `khugepaged` collapse of 4 KiB pages are jitter sources — often disabled in favor of explicit huge pages. (Ch. 17, Appendix C)
- **Tick dependency** — the reason a `nohz_full` CPU's periodic tick cannot stop, named by the `timer:tick_stop` tracepoint: a second runnable task (`SCHED`), an attached perf event (`PERF_EVENTS`), a POSIX interval timer, an unstable clocksource, an expedited RCU grace period. (Ch. 59 §59.5, 61 §61.4.3)
- **TLB shootdown** — the IPI that flushes another core's TLB when a page mapping in a shared address space changes (`munmap`, `mprotect`, `madvise(DONTNEED)`, THP split, compaction, KSM); the most common self-inflicted intrusion on an isolated core, caused by the hot process's own housekeeping threads. (Ch. 34, 61 §61.4.3)
- **Async-signal-safe** — the property of a function that may be called from a signal handler (POSIX `signal-safety(7)`): syscall wrappers with no library state — `write`, `sigaction`, `_exit`… — and *not* `malloc`, `printf`, mutexes, `exit`, `dlopen`, or glibc's `backtrace()` on its first call. (Ch. 108 §108.2.2)
- **`sigaltstack`** — a per-thread alternate signal stack, so a `SIGSEGV` from a stack overflow is still handled; needs `SA_ONSTACK` on the `sigaction` and a call in *every* thread. (Ch. 108 §108.4.1)
- **`coredump_filter` / `MADV_DONTDUMP`** — the per-process bitmask (`/proc/PID/coredump_filter`) and the per-range `madvise` flag that decide which mappings a core dump includes; excluding the hugepage-backed book and rings turns a 40 GB, tens-of-seconds dump into a 60 MB, sub-second one. (Ch. 108 §108.4.4)
- **`core_pattern` pipe handler** — `kernel.core_pattern = |/path/handler %P %s …`: the kernel pipes the core to a program; `RLIMIT_CORE` is ignored for pipes, so the handler bounds itself (`taskset` to housekeeping cores, `nice`/`ionice`, `timeout`, a separate disk); `core_pipe_limit` caps concurrent handlers. (Ch. 108 §108.4.4)
- **Minidump** — Breakpad/Crashpad's small crash file (thread registers and stack tops, module list with build IDs) written by an out-of-process handler; the fleet default, insufficient when the question is heap corruption. (Ch. 108 §108.4.4)
- **`process_vm_readv`** — copy another process's memory without stopping any of its threads; with a seqlock-versioned snapshot the reader rejects torn copies and the hot thread never knows it was read. (Ch. 5 §5.4.4)
- **`ptrace_scope` (Yama)** — `kernel.yama.ptrace_scope`: who may `ptrace` whom (0 same uid, 1 descendants or `CAP_SYS_PTRACE`, 2 admin only, 3 never); set to 2 on a trading host and run inspection as a capability-holding sidecar. (Ch. 5 §5.4.4)
- **Guard page** — a `PROT_NONE` page after a pool slab so an overflow faults at the write (`SEGV_ACCERR`) instead of corrupting the neighbour; costs address range, TLB reach and one VMA per guard. (Ch. 5 §5.4.5)
- **C-state exit latency / target residency** — per idle state, the advertised wake-up time and the minimum sleep for which entering it pays; `intel_idle`'s tables (Ice Lake-SP C6: 170 µs / 600 µs) and ACPI `_CST` on AMD; the first message after a lull pays the exit *plus* cold caches and the uncore ramp. (Ch. 60 §60.2.1)
- **PM QoS (`/dev/cpu_dma_latency`, `pm_qos_resume_latency_us`)** — the kernel's runtime "do not sleep deeper than X µs" request: global while a file descriptor is held open (what `tuned` does; gone when the holder dies), or per CPU via `sysfs`. (Ch. 60 §60.2.2, §60.4.2)
- **`cpuidle` governor (`menu`/`teo`)** — the predictor that picks an idle state from the recent wake-up history; its mispredictions (`above`/`below` counters) are tail events by construction. (Ch. 60 §60.2.1)
- **APERF / MPERF** — the actual- and maximum-performance MSR counters whose ratio × nominal TSC frequency is the effective core clock (`turbostat`'s `Bzy_MHz`); `cycles / ref-cycles` is the PMU equivalent. (Ch. 60 §60.3)
- **NAPI** — the kernel's "new API" receive model: an interrupt disables further interrupts for the queue and schedules a poll that drains up to a budget of packets in softirq context; busy-polling drives the same poll from the reader's syscall. (Ch. 64 §64.2.1–§64.2.2, Ch. 63 §63.4.1)
- **Softirq / `ksoftirqd`** — deferred interrupt-context work (`NET_RX`, `NET_TX`, `TIMER`, `RCU`…) run on the way out of a hardirq, or in the per-CPU `ksoftirqd/N` thread once `netdev_budget`/`netdev_budget_usecs` is exhausted (`softnet_stat`'s `time_squeeze`); network softirq on an isolated core is the unnamed jitter source of Part VII. (Ch. 64 §64.2.2, Ch. 61 §61.2)
- **GRO / LRO** — generic (stack) / large (NIC) receive offload: coalescing a flow's TCP segments within one poll batch into one `sk_buff`; a throughput feature that holds the first segment until the batch ends. (Ch. 64 §64.2.1, Ch. 75)
- **qdisc (`noqueue`, `mq`/`pfifo_fast`, `fq`, `fq_codel`, `etf`)** — the transmit queueing discipline between `sendmsg` and the driver: no queue, per-TX-queue FIFO (the server default), per-flow pacing (BBR/ETF need it; adds latency by design), and the earliest-TxTime-first qdisc of Ch. 79. (Ch. 64 §64.2.3)
- **BQL (byte queue limits)** — the per-TX-queue bound on bytes handed to the NIC but not yet completed, auto-tuned from completions, that stops the driver ring from becoming a latency buffer; `queues/tx-N/byte_queue_limits/{limit,inflight}`. (Ch. 64 §64.2.3)
- **RPS / RFS / XPS** — receive packet steering (software RSS: re-queue to another CPU's backlog with an IPI), receive flow steering (to the consuming socket's CPU) and transmit packet steering (CPU → TX queue map); an RPS mask that names a hot core is a softirq on it. (Ch. 64 §64.2.2, Ch. 77)
- **netfilter / conntrack / `notrack`** — the packet-filter hooks (PREROUTING … POSTROUTING) every packet traverses, the connection-tracking module any `ct`-referencing rule engages for every packet (~0.25 µs per packet per hook, measured), and the `raw`-table verdict that exempts a flow from it. (Ch. 64 §64.2.4, §64.3, §64.4.4)
- **`veth` / `macvlan` / SR-IOV VF** — the ways a network namespace reaches the wire: a virtual pair (a second full stack pass in softirq), a sub-interface of the NIC (one pass), or a hardware virtual function with its own queues and IRQs. (Ch. 64 §64.2.4, §64.4.5)
- **Dense tick ladder** — the order book's price-level array indexed by `tick − base`: O(1) to any level, 16 bytes per level, an occupancy bitmap for "next best", a window of `WINDOW` ticks around the touch with a cold sorted overflow beyond it. (Ch. 33 §33.4.1–§33.4.2)
- **Re-centring** — moving the dense ladder's window when an add that *improves the best* lands near or beyond its edge: levels that fall off migrate to the cold store, cold levels that come back are pulled in, the bitmap is rebuilt; a `WINDOW`-proportional tail event (~26 µs at 4096 ticks) that happens a few times per session. (Ch. 33 §33.4.1)
- **Direct addressing (order ids)** — mapping a near-monotone venue order id to its pool slot by `id & (SLOTS − 1)` with the id stored in the slot as the validation — no hash, one predictable compare, consecutive ids on consecutive bytes; needs `SLOTS` above the span of ids in flight. (Ch. 33 §33.4.3)
- **Eytzinger layout** — a sorted array stored in BFS (heap) order, `t[1]` the root and `2k`/`2k+1` the children, so a binary search's first levels share a cache line and later levels are prefetchable 16 nodes ahead; static (rebuilt on insert), the right layout for reference data searched past L2. (Ch. 32 §32.4.6)

## F.4 Concurrency terms

- **ABA problem** — a lock-free hazard where a value reads as A, changes to B and back to A, fooling a CAS into thinking nothing changed; addressed with tagged pointers / hazard pointers / epochs. (Ch. 45, 47)
- **Acquire / Release** — memory-ordering constraints: an *acquire* load sees all writes that happened-before the matching *release* store; the one-way barriers that make lock-free publication correct (cheap on x86-TSO, real instructions `LDAR`/`STLR` on ARM). (Ch. 41, Appendix A.1)
- **CAS** — Compare-And-Swap (`compare_exchange_weak`/`_strong`): the atomic read-modify-write — "if the location still holds *expected*, store *desired*" — that every lock-free algorithm is built from; the CAS *loop* retries until it wins, and ABA is its characteristic hazard. (Ch. 45, 41)
- **Disruptor** — a high-performance ring-buffer + sequence-barrier pattern (LMAX) for single-producer/multi-consumer pipelines with batching and mechanical sympathy. (Ch. 48)
- **Slot-ownership protocol** — the lifecycle of a zero-copy ring slot (free → claimed → published → consumed-by-all → free) stated as rights granted by sequence transitions, enforced with move-only `[[nodiscard]]` handles and a poison/outstanding-view test. (Ch. 49 §49.2.4, §49.4.4–§49.4.5)
- **Hop (cross-core hand-off)** — moving a message between two cores through a ring: at least two cache-line transfers (index + slot) on the consumer's path, ~100–150 ns on the reference machine with both ends spinning, microseconds if the consumer must be woken; the unit of the topology cost model. (Ch. 49 §49.2.2, §49.3)
- **Conflation** — replacing a queue of updates to one key with the key's latest value plus a dirty set, so a slow consumer acts on state that is at most one drain pass old instead of a growing backlog; correct for state (BBO, levels, positions), never for facts (fills, acks, trade prints). Built as per-instrument seqlock slots + a dirty bitmap drained with `tzcnt`. (Ch. 50 §50.2.2, §50.4.2)
- **Epoch-based reclamation (EBR)** — a safe-memory-reclamation scheme where readers enter an epoch and memory is freed only after all readers have advanced past it; cheap reads, but reclamation is *not* bounded — one stalled reader pins the epoch for everyone (contrast hazard pointers, which bound memory by construction). (Ch. 47)
- **False sharing** — two unrelated variables on the *same cache line* written by different cores, causing the line to ping-pong (HITM) as if they were shared; fixed by padding to `hardware_destructive_interference_size`. (Ch. 44)
- **Hazard pointer** — a safe-memory-reclamation scheme where readers publish the pointers they're using so a deleter knows what's unsafe to free yet. (Ch. 47)
- **Lock convoy** — under sustained contention threads pile up behind a lock and proceed in lockstep, each wake-up handing the lock to the next sleeper; throughput collapses and latency spikes. Cure: shorter critical sections, sharding, or no lock at all. (Ch. 43)
- **Lock-free / wait-free (progress guarantees)** — *lock-free*: at least one thread always completes in a bounded number of steps, so no thread's stall can block the system; *wait-free*: *every* thread completes in bounded steps — the latency ideal, since it bounds the per-operation tail; *obstruction-free* is weaker than both. (Ch. 45)
- **MCS / CLH queue locks** — fair spinlocks in which each waiter spins on its *own* cache line (a per-thread queue node) and the unlocker hands off to its successor — eliminating the cache-line bounce of a plain spinlock under high contention. (Ch. 43)
- **Memory fence / happens-before** — a *fence* (`atomic_thread_fence`, `mfence`/`DMB`) orders memory operations around it without being tied to one variable; *happens-before* is the C++ memory model's relation — established by a release store synchronizing with the acquire load that reads it — that makes one thread's prior writes visible to another. Correctness on ARM depends on getting it right, not on luck. (Ch. 41; Appendix A.1)
- **Memory model / `memory_order`** — the rules (C++11+) governing how atomic operations on different threads become visible; `relaxed`/`acquire`/`release`/`acq_rel`/`seq_cst`. (Ch. 41)
- **RCU** — Read-Copy-Update: a synchronization scheme giving readers a lock-free fast path; writers publish a new version and reclaim the old after a grace period when no reader can hold it. (Ch. 47, 102)
- **Ring buffer** — a fixed-size circular array indexed by monotonically increasing producer/consumer sequence numbers (masked by a power-of-two size): the zero-allocation, cache-friendly backbone of SPSC/MPMC queues, the Disruptor, shared-memory IPC, NIC descriptor rings and the async logger. (Ch. 45, 48, 67, 98)
- **Seqlock** — sequence lock: a single-writer/multi-reader scheme where the writer bumps a counter around writes and readers retry if the counter changed mid-read; tear-free publication of small snapshots. (Ch. 46, 102)
- **Staleness budget** — the maximum age (wire or ingress timestamp to the moment of use) a consumer may act on, per channel; measured as a histogram, published as telemetry, and breached into a degraded mode or the kill-switch. (Ch. 50 §50.2.3, §50.4.3)
- **Shared-nothing** — partitioning work so that each thread (or process) owns its data outright and there is no shared mutable state; coordination by message passing/rings — the partitioning that actually scales (the scalability curve stays linear), and the book's default architecture. (Ch. 42, 103)
- **Single-writer principle** — assigning exactly one writer per data structure / memory segment so publication is a simple release store with no inter-writer coordination. (Ch. 46, 103)
- **SMR (Safe Memory Reclamation)** — the family of schemes (RCU, hazard pointers, EBR) that defer freeing a node unlinked from a lock-free structure until no reader can still reference it. (Ch. 47)
- **Spinlock / TTAS / backoff** — a lock acquired by spinning on an atomic; **TTAS** (test-and-test-and-set) spins on a plain *read* until the lock looks free before attempting the atomic, and **exponential backoff** (with `pause`) spaces out retries — both to stop waiters hammering the holder's cache line. Safe only on isolated cores where the holder cannot be preempted — otherwise you are spinning across the scheduler. (Ch. 43)
- **SPSC / MPMC** — Single/Multi Producer, Single/Multi Consumer: the contention classes of lock-free queues/rings; SPSC is the cheapest (no inter-producer/consumer CAS). (Ch. 45)
- **TSO (Total Store Order)** — x86's relatively strong memory model (only store→load reordering); contrast ARM's weaker model where more reorderings are allowed and orderings cost real instructions. (Ch. 41, Appendix A.1)
- **USL / Amdahl's law** — Amdahl: the serial fraction caps speed-up no matter how many cores you add; the Universal Scalability Law (Gunther) adds a *coherence* term, so throughput actually *falls* past some thread count — the model behind the measured scalability curve. (Ch. 42)

- **Lease** — a time-bounded grant of a role (primary emitter) on a monotonic clock, renewed at half-life, expiring on its own so a dead holder frees it; acquired by CAS on a lease word, its epoch is the fencing token; checked by the holder before every send; sized from the longest healthy pause, not the wished-for RTO (0.5/2/10 ms leases → promotion at 0.95/1.8/6.8 ms). (Ch. 112 §112.2.3, §112.4.1)
- **Split-brain** — two gateways both believing they own one venue session and both emitting: doubled position, sequence chaos, a regulatory event; prevented by lease + fencing token + venue nets, never by failure detection alone. (Ch. 112 §112.1, §112.2.1)
- **φ-accrual failure detector** — a detector that models heartbeat inter-arrival times and reports a suspicion level φ = −log₁₀ P(late heartbeat still coming) instead of a fixed timeout; 7× faster detection than a 10-heartbeat timeout on the VM and 35 false suspicions in 20 trials — tolerable only because the lease, not the detector, permits a takeover. (Ch. 112 §112.2.2, §112.4.2)
- **Two-path failure detection** — declaring a peer dead only when an in-host shm heartbeat *and* a cross-host side channel are both silent, so a dropped packet, a stalled link or one stalled process is not a death. (Ch. 112 §112.2.2)
- **Zombie (paused primary)** — a primary that stopped for longer than its lease and resumed believing it is primary; defeated by the pre-send lease check, bounded pauses, the fencing token at the receiver, and unacked sends as a step-down signal. (Ch. 112 §112.4.4)

## F.5 C++/toolchain terms

- **Minimax polynomial (vs Taylor)** — coefficients fitted (Remez/Chebyshev) to minimise the maximum error over an interval; the same degree as a Taylor expansion with 10–100× less error. (Ch. 36 §36.2.3)
- **Range reduction / reconstruction** — the reduction of a transcendental's argument to a small interval (`x = k·ln2 + r`) and the integer rebuild of the scale (`2^k` via the exponent bits). (Ch. 36 §36.2.2)
- **Horner vs Estrin** — polynomial evaluation as a dependent FMA chain (throughput) vs a tree of partial products (latency). (Ch. 36 §36.2.3)
- **Fibonacci (multiplicative) hashing** — `k · 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15` with the *high* bits taken as the slot; the one-multiply hash for integer keys. (Ch. 38 §38.4.3)
- **Perfect hash (CHD)** — a collision-free hash for a closed key set built at compile time: a searched multiplier for small sets, hash-and-displace with per-bucket seeds for an instrument universe; lookup with no probe loop. (Ch. 38 §38.4.4)
- **Hash flooding** — an attacker choosing keys that collide in an unkeyed hash to drive a table to its worst case; defended with a per-process seeded hash (SipHash where the argument must be cryptographic). (Ch. 38 §38.4.6)
- **CRC32C** — the Castagnoli CRC with a hardware instruction (`crc32q`, 8 bytes per 3 cycles); a checksum for records and frames, not a hash for hostile keys. (Ch. 38 §38.4.5)
- **Schema evolution / compatibility window / layout fingerprint** — additive-only record changes with length-driven skipping, the declared `(reader, writer)` version pairs that must interoperate, and a build-time hash of the layout compared at attach. (Ch. 67 §67.4.4; Ch. 119 §119.4.2)
- **Strong type (units)** — a trivially copyable single-member wrapper (`Price`, `Qty`, `Notional`, `SymbolId`, `ClOrdId`) with an explicit allowed-operations matrix, passed in a bare register exactly like its member; the compile-time defence against price + quantity and tick/scale confusion. (Ch. 22 §22.4.4, §22.5; Ch. 35)
- **Type-state** — encoding a protocol object's state in its type (`Session<LoggedOn>`, `Order<Acked>`) so that illegal transitions do not compile; rvalue-qualified transitions consume the old state. The runtime table still stores the state as a byte; type-state is the API over it. (Ch. 22 §22.4.5; Ch. 105 §105.4.6)
- **Static dependency injection / null-object policy** — supplying collaborators as template parameters and substituting empty inline policies (`NullLogger`, `NoRisk`, `FixedClock`) in tests and benchmarks with no runtime seam. (Ch. 22 §22.4.3)
- **BOLT** — Binary Optimization and Layout Tool: a post-link optimizer that re-lays-out the final binary using a `perf` profile for I-cache/I-TLB locality. (Ch. 27, Appendix D)
- **Borrow checker / `unsafe`** — Rust's compile-time ownership and borrowing analysis, which rejects use-after-free, data races and aliased `&mut` at zero runtime cost; `unsafe` blocks switch specific checks off for raw pointers, FFI and hardware access, marking where the C++-style freedoms live. (Ch. 94 §94.2.6, §94.4.3; Appendix B)
- **Coroutine / HALO** — C++20 stackless coroutines (suspendable functions); HALO (Heap-Allocation eLision Optimization) elides the coroutine frame allocation when the compiler can prove the lifetime. (Ch. 51)
- **Clobber (asm)** — a register, the flags (`"cc"`) or memory (`"memory"`) that an inline-asm statement destroys beyond its declared outputs; an undeclared clobber silently corrupts a value the compiler kept live across the statement. (Ch. 93 §93.2.1, §93.6)
- **`const fn` / `comptime`** — Rust's and Zig's compile-time evaluation: `const fn` is a restricted function that must evaluate in a `const` context (no silent runtime fallback), `comptime` is a mode in which any function runs in the compiler and types are values; both produce the same `.rodata` as a C++ `constexpr` table. (Ch. 97 §97.4.3)
- **Deoptimisation** — a JIT discarding compiled code whose speculation (type, branch, inlining) failed and falling back to the interpreter mid-run; `made not entrant` in HotSpot's compilation log. A cold start in the middle of the session. (Ch. 96 §96.2.3, §96.3)
- **Early clobber (`=&r`)** — an asm output constraint saying the output is written before all inputs are read, so it must not share a register with any input. (Ch. 93 §93.2.1, §93.4.4)
- **Extended asm** — GCC/Clang's `asm("template" : outputs : inputs : clobbers)` form: the template is opaque to the optimizer, the constraints are the whole contract. `volatile` marks a side effect; `asm goto` jumps to C++ labels. (Ch. 93 §93.2)
- **Flyweight (garbage-free)** — in a managed runtime, a reusable view object whose accessors read fields straight from the wire bytes (`VarHandle`, Agrona `UnsafeBuffer`, `MemorySegment`, `Span<byte>`) so that decoding allocates nothing; the technique that puts Java's decode at 2 ns per message with zero collections. (Ch. 96 §96.4.1)
- **Hourglass interface** — a wide C++ (or other-language) implementation and a wide client joined by a narrow `extern "C"` waist of opaque handles and POD arguments; the shape of every language boundary in the book. (Ch. 95 §95.2.1)
- **Monomorphization** — generating one specialised copy of a generic function per concrete type (C++ templates, Rust generics/traits, Zig `anytype`): static dispatch, inlinable, zero-cost; the alternative is a vtable (`virtual`, `dyn Trait`). (Ch. 94 §94.4.5; Ch. 22)
- **`noalias` (`&mut`)** — the LLVM attribute rustc attaches to every `&mut` argument because the borrow rules make it the only live reference: the guarantee `__restrict__` asks a C++ programmer to promise, checked. Raw pointers carry none. (Ch. 94 §94.2.5, §94.5)
- **Safepoint / time-to-safepoint** — a point where a managed runtime knows every thread's stack and references, at which stop-the-world phases run; threads poll for it at method returns and loop back-edges, and the delay until the last thread arrives (time-to-safepoint) is latency the GC log's pause time does not include. (Ch. 96 §96.2.2, §96.3)
- **Tiered compilation** — a JIT's ladder from interpreter (or tier-0 code) through a fast compiler with profiling (HotSpot C1 / .NET tier-0) to the optimising compiler (C2 / tier-1 with PGO), with on-stack replacement for running loops; the JIT warm-up curve is this ladder seen from the hot thread. (Ch. 96 §96.2.3, §96.3)
- **CRTP** — Curiously Recurring Template Pattern: static (compile-time) polymorphism via `template<class D> Base`, avoiding the virtual-call indirection of runtime dispatch. (Ch. 22, 16)
- **`constexpr` / `consteval`** — compile-time evaluation: `constexpr` *may* run at compile time, `consteval` *must*; moves work off the runtime hot path. (Ch. 21)
- **Devirtualization / vtable** — a *vtable* is the per-class table of function pointers a `virtual` call indirects through (an indirect branch, BTB-predicted); *devirtualization* is the compiler replacing that indirect call with a direct (inlinable) one when it can prove the dynamic type — via `final`, LTO whole-program analysis, or PGO speculation. (Ch. 16, 27)
- **EBO / `[[no_unique_address]]`** — Empty-Base Optimization: an empty base class occupies no storage in the derived object; C++20's `[[no_unique_address]]` extends that to empty *members* (allocators, policies), so zero-size policy objects don't pad a hot struct. (Ch. 11, 22)
- **`std::execution` (senders / receivers / scheduler)** — the C++26 standard async model: lazily-composed **senders** describe async work, **receivers** are the typed continuation (value/error/stopped), and a **scheduler** places work on an execution context — a zero-cost structured-async abstraction. (Ch. 52)
- **`std::expected`** — a C++23 vocabulary type holding either a value or an error, for hot-path error handling without exceptions or sentinel codes. (Ch. 24)
- **FFI** — Foreign Function Interface: calling across language runtimes, in practice through a C ABI (`extern "C"`); the boundary belongs between latency tiers, never on a per-message hot path — 2.5 ns from an AOT language, 10–470 ns across a runtime. (Ch. 95 §95.3; Appendix B)
- **`std::flat_map` / `std::flat_set`** — C++23 sorted-vector adaptors: ordered containers stored contiguously (keys and values in separate vectors), so lookups are cache-friendly binary searches rather than pointer chases through tree nodes; the right shape for small-to-medium ordered hot data such as price levels. (Ch. 32; Appendix H)
- **FTZ / DAZ** — Flush-To-Zero / Denormals-Are-Zero: FPU modes treating subnormal floats as zero to avoid the 10-100× denormal-operation penalty; set explicitly on FP hot paths. (Ch. 35, Appendix D)
- **GC pause** — a stop of the application thread(s) by a managed runtime's garbage collector, from tens of µs (ZGC/Shenandoah/C4) to milliseconds (a full GC): a tail event (Ch. 1) the runtime, not you, schedules — what keeps GC'd languages off the order path — 8–10 ms per stop-the-world young collection, zero with a garbage-free hot thread. (Ch. 96 §96.3; Appendix B)
- **Heisenbug / observer effect** — a bug that changes or vanishes when you observe it, because a `printf`/breakpoint/heavy probe perturbs timing; debug via low-overhead capture and offline replay instead. (Ch. 5)
- **Intrusive container** — a list/tree whose link fields live *inside* the element (`boost::intrusive`-style) instead of in separately allocated nodes: no per-insert allocation, one fewer pointer chase, and an element can be unlinked in O(1) from a pointer to it — the order-book idiom for orders within a price level. (Ch. 32)
- **JIT vs AOT** — just-in-time compilation (JVM, .NET) optimizes hot code at runtime after interpreting it, so the first executions are slow and a *deoptimization* can re-introduce a stall mid-session — the Ch. 62 cold-start problem built into the runtime; ahead-of-time compilation (C++, Rust, C, Zig, GraalVM native-image) is fully optimized before the first tick — 12.6 µs per message for the first hundred calls vs 13 ns at steady state. (Ch. 96 §96.3; Appendix B, Ch. 62)
- **LTO** — Link-Time Optimization: optimizing across translation-unit boundaries at link (cross-module inlining, devirtualization); ThinLTO is the scalable variant. (Ch. 27, Appendix D)
- **`noexcept`** — a specifier promising a function won't throw; enables optimizations (no unwinding setup) and is required for some move-aware container fast paths. (Ch. 24)
- **Open addressing / Robin Hood hashing** — hash tables that store entries in the bucket array itself (probing on collision) instead of chaining through heap nodes; *Robin Hood* probing steals slots from entries with shorter probe distances to keep the maximum probe length — the tail — short. The cache-friendly replacement for `std::unordered_map`. (Ch. 32)
- **PGO** — Profile-Guided Optimization: a build that uses a runtime profile (branch/hotness data) to drive inlining, branch layout, and code placement with data instead of heuristics. (Ch. 27, Appendix D)
- **PMR** — Polymorphic Memory Resources (`std::pmr`): standard-library support for custom allocators (arenas/pools) plugged into containers. (Ch. 31)
- **RAII** — Resource Acquisition Is Initialization: tying resource lifetime to object scope (constructor acquires, destructor releases); deterministic cleanup, no GC. (Ch. 52 — RAII applied to async scopes; Ch. 31 for destructor discipline in arenas/pools; Appendix B)
- **`restrict` / `__restrict__`** — a pointer qualifier promising no aliasing through it, letting the optimizer reorder/vectorize loads and stores it otherwise couldn't. (Ch. 26)
- **`rr`** — a record-and-replay debugger: records an execution once, then replays it deterministically forwards *and backwards* (reverse execution) — the reproduce-then-debug tool. (Ch. 5) Limits: one process tree on one core (threads serialised; `-h` chaos mode for races), no visibility of memory written by peers or DMA (shared-memory rings, NIC rings), needs the retired-branch counter (`perf_event_paranoid ≤ 1`, rarely available in VMs), traps `rdtsc`/`cpuid` — so it records the replay *harness*, never the live consumer of a shared segment (Ch. 5 §5.4.2).
- **RTTI** — Run-Time Type Information: the per-type data behind `dynamic_cast` and `typeid`; `-fno-rtti` removes it when static polymorphism (CRTP, `std::variant`) replaces runtime type queries. (Ch. 24; Appendix D.4)
- **Sanitizer (ASan / UBSan / TSan / MSan)** — compiler instrumentation catching, respectively, memory errors, undefined behavior, data races, and uninitialized reads; a test/CI/fuzzing tool (ASan ~2×, TSan ~5–15× — Ch. 53), never production. (Ch. 53, 101)
- **SBO (small-buffer optimization)** — storing a small payload inline inside the handle object (as `std::string`, `std::function` and `std::any` implementations do) instead of heap-allocating it; the hot-path version is a *fixed-capacity* inline buffer that never allocates. (Ch. 16, 32)
- **SoA / AoS** — Structure-of-Arrays vs Array-of-Structures: data layouts; SoA groups each field contiguously for cache/SIMD efficiency, AoS groups whole records. (Ch. 10, 39)
- **`std::span` / `std::mdspan`** — non-owning views over contiguous (1-D) / multi-dimensional data carrying their bounds; bounds-aware zero-copy access. (Ch. 101, 4; availability in Appendix H)
- **Split debug info** — keeping `-g` debug info in a separate file (`-gsplit-dwarf` / `objcopy`) so a stripped production binary's cores and profiles stay symbolizable offline. (Ch. 5, Appendix D)
- **Structured concurrency** — async work with scope-bound lifetime and propagating cancellation (stop tokens) — RAII applied to concurrency; no dangling continuations. (Ch. 52)
- **Zero-cost abstraction** — an abstraction (templates, RAII, iterators) that compiles to the same code you'd write by hand — no runtime penalty for the abstraction. (Ch. 22, 24)
- **Frame-pointer walk / DWARF CFI / SFrame** — the three ways to recover a call stack: follow the saved-`rbp` chain (2–4 cycles per frame, no library, async-signal-safe; leaves and tail calls leave no frame), interpret `.eh_frame` (~200 cycles per frame; `_Unwind_Backtrace`, libunwind, `backtrace()`), or walk the compact `.sframe` table (binutils ≥ 2.41) the kernel's deferred user unwinder consumes. (Ch. 27 §27.4.4, 108 §108.4.2)
- **Non-stop mode (gdb)** — `set non-stop on` before `attach`: only the thread you interrupt stops, the rest run; the sanctioned way to look at a housekeeping thread of a live trading process. An all-stop `gdb -p` stops every thread for the session (254 ms measured). (Ch. 5 §5.4.4)
- **Build ID** — the `NT_GNU_BUILD_ID` note (`readelf -n`) that keys a stripped binary to its archived debug info and a crash record or core to the right symbols; `debuginfod` serves by it. (Ch. 5 §5.4.1, 108 §108.4.5, Appendix N.2)
- **`std::source_location` / `std::stacktrace` / `<debugging>`** — C++20 zero-cost call-site metadata (one `lea` of a static), C++23 stack capture (~4k cycles) with separate symbolisation (~40k), and C++26 `std::breakpoint`/`is_debugger_present`; the first is hot-path safe, the others belong on the terminate/assert path. (Ch. 24 §24.4.4; Appendix H.2)
- **Contracts (C++26)** — `pre`/`post`/`contract_assert` with evaluation semantics *ignore*/*observe*/*enforce*/*quick_enforce* (GCC 15 experimental); the standard form of "a check is a predictable compare, a report is a cold path". (Ch. 24 §24.4.4; Appendix H.3)
- **Hardened library mode** — `_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS` / `_LIBCPP_HARDENING_MODE`: the standard library's preconditions as predictable compare-and-trap, +3–8 % measured on a decode loop; the shippable middle between nothing and ASan. (Ch. 5 §5.4.5, 24 §24.4.4, 101 §101.3)

- **Concept / `requires` / subsumption** — a named, checkable compile-time requirement on a template parameter (C++20); constrained overloads are ranked by *subsumption* (a concept defined as `A<T> && B<T>` is more constrained than `A<T>`), which works only through named concepts; checks syntax, not semantics; costs no code. (Ch. 22 §22.4.6)
- **NTTP (non-type template parameter), class-type / `fixed_string`** — a template argument that is a *value*, since C++20 including a structural class object such as a `char[N]` wrapper, so a string literal can name a field at compile time (`get<"price">()`). (Ch. 23 §23.2.2)
- **Compile-time decoder / field table** — a `consteval`-parsed description of a wire message (name, encoding, offset, size) from which typed overlay accessors are generated by `if constexpr`; `static_assert`ed against the venue document and proven instruction-identical to the hand-written decoder. (Ch. 23)
- **Closure type** — the unnamed class a lambda expression denotes: one member per capture, an `operator()` holding the body (a template for generic lambdas); passed as a template parameter it inlines, stored in `std::function` it is called indirectly. (Ch. 25 §25.2.1)
- **Sentinel / view (ranges)** — a range's end marker of possibly different type than its iterator; a *view* is a lightweight adaptor range (`transform`, `filter`, `take`); `filter`'s iterator searches for its next element, turning a counted loop into a searched one that does not vectorise. (Ch. 25 §25.2.2)
- **Symbol as integer (`Sym8`) / interning** — an 8-character venue symbol loaded as one `uint64_t` (one `cmp`, one-multiply hash) or mapped once at startup to a dense id that indexes arrays; the hot-path replacement for `std::string` keys. (Ch. 25 §25.4.4)
- **Fixed-capacity string / `inplace_vector`** — an inline-storage, allocation-free, trivially copyable sequence with a compile-time maximum (`std::inplace_vector<T, N>`, C++26; `static_vector`/`etl` today). (Ch. 25 §25.4.4, Ch. 32 §32.4.3)
- **SysV classification (INTEGER / SSE / MEMORY) / `sret` / invisible reference** — the x86-64 ABI rule assigning arguments and returns to registers or memory: trivially copyable aggregates ≤ 16 bytes travel in up to two registers; larger ones through a hidden pointer (`sret`); any type with a non-trivial destructor by *invisible reference* regardless of size. (Ch. 4 §4.2.1, Ch. 24 §24.4.5)
- **Copy elision (guaranteed) / NRVO** — a prvalue is constructed directly in its destination (C++17, guaranteed); a *named* return value may be (NRVO, permitted) and is not when the function can return two different named objects. (Ch. 24 §24.4.5)
- **`[[clang::trivial_abi]]`** — a Clang attribute making a destructor-bearing class pass in registers with the callee destroying it; an ABI break for any TU that does not see it. (Ch. 24 §24.4.5)
- **Guard variable / magic static** — the byte (`_ZGV…`) a function-local `static` tests on every call (an acquire load and a branch) and locks via `__cxa_guard_acquire` on the first; replaced by `constinit` or explicit startup initialisation. (Ch. 24 §24.4.6)
- **TLS model (local-exec / initial-exec / general-dynamic)** — how a `thread_local` is addressed: one `fs:`-relative load in the executable, a GOT-indirect `fs:` load with `-ftls-model=initial-exec`, or a `__tls_get_addr` call (plus a per-thread first-touch allocation) in a default-built or `dlopen`'d shared object. (Ch. 24 §24.4.6)
- **Static initialization order (fiasco) / `init_priority`** — dynamic initialisers run in `.init_array` order, unspecified across translation units; `[[gnu::init_priority(N)]]` orders them, `constinit` removes them. (Ch. 24 §24.4.6)
- **Implicit-lifetime type / implicit object creation (P0593)** — scalars, arrays and trivially constructible/destructible aggregates whose objects `malloc`, `memcpy`/`memmove`, byte-array creation and `bit_cast` bring into existence without a constructor; the rule that makes `memcpy` of a wire struct well-defined. (Ch. 26 §26.2)
- **`std::start_lifetime_as`** — C++23's statement that an implicit-lifetime object now exists in given bytes; zero instructions; the sanctioned struct-over-receive-buffer overlay (polyfill: `memmove(p, p, n)` + `launder`). (Ch. 26 §26.4.2)
- **`function_ref` / `move_only_function` / in-place function** — the lighter type erasers: a two-word non-owning callable reference (C++26), an owning move-only callable with no empty check (C++23), and a fixed-capacity owning callable that cannot allocate; all one indirect call. (Ch. 16 §16.4.5)
- **`atomic_ref`** — atomic operations applied to a *plain* object for the duration of the reference; the defined way to read a wire/shm field another thread or process writes; same `mov` as the plain read. (Ch. 41 §41.4.3)
- **DWCAS / 16-byte CAS / libatomic trap** — a compare-and-swap on a 16-byte pair (tagged pointer, seq+value): `cmpxchg16b` inline on Clang with `-mcx16`, a libatomic call on GCC (lock-free at runtime but `is_always_lock_free == false`), a real lock when misaligned or without `cx16`. (Ch. 41 §41.4.3, Ch. 45)
- **`std::simd` / `native_simd` / `fixed_size_simd`** — the data-parallel type (C++26; `std::experimental::simd` today) whose lane count follows the target `-march` (`native_simd`) or is fixed (`fixed_size_simd<T, N>`), the latter required in any type shared across TUs. (Ch. 39 §39.4.4)
- **`-ftime-trace` / ClangBuildAnalyzer** — Clang's per-TU build profile (header parse, instantiation, optimisation, per event) and the tool that aggregates it across a build; the `perf` of compile time. (Ch. 28 §28.4.1)
- **Unity (jumbo) build / precompiled header (PCH)** — compiling many `.cpp` files as one TU so headers are parsed and templates instantiated once (−78 % here), vs serialising the parsed headers once and loading them per TU (−22…−46 %); both carry ODR/flag-uniformity traps. (Ch. 28 §28.4.3)
- **Named module / BMI / global module fragment** — a C++20 `export module` compiled once to a binary module interface (`.pcm`/`.gcm`) and `import`ed; the GMF (`module; #include …`) is where non-modular headers go. (Ch. 28 §28.4.4)
- **Hermetic build / reproducible build / `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH`** — a build whose outputs depend only on declared inputs (pinned toolchain, no host headers), and one proven so by bit-identical rebuilds (`SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH`, prefix maps, `-frandom-seed`, `--build-id=sha1`). (Ch. 28 §28.4.6)
- **Meaning-class vs codegen-class flag** — a compiler flag that changes a type's layout or an inline function's semantics (must be identical across every TU that shares the type: `-fno-exceptions`, `-fwrapv`, `-fno-strict-aliasing`, sanitizers, an ISA-dependent `-march`) vs one that changes only generated code (`-O3`, unrolling, alignment). (Ch. 28 §28.4.5)
- **UB licence / buy-back flag** — an undefined-behaviour rule read as the assumption the optimizer is entitled to make, and the flag (`-fwrapv`, `-fno-strict-aliasing`, `-fno-delete-null-pointer-checks`, `-ftrivial-auto-var-init`) that revokes it at a measured cost. (Appendix P.3)
- **Welford's algorithm** — the online mean/variance recurrence `d = x − μ; μ += d/n; M2 += d·(x − μ)` (West's form), one division per update and no catastrophic cancellation; the naive `E[x²] − E[x]²` is 3400× wrong on a price series. (Ch. 40 §40.4.2)
- **EWMA / EW variance** — exponentially weighted moving average `μ += (1−w)(x − μ)` and variance `v = w(v + (1−w)d²)`: one-pole IIR filters that forget rounding error geometrically; `w` precomputed, or for irregular arrivals `exp(−Δt/τ)` looked up in a table — never `exp()` on the hot path; FTZ/DAZ mandatory (a quiet input decays into denormals: 33×). (Ch. 40 §40.4.3)
- **Monotonic deque (sliding-window min/max)** — a fixed-capacity ring of (value, position) candidates kept monotone: push pops dominated candidates from the back, the front is dropped when it leaves the window, the front is the answer; amortised O(1) per update. (Ch. 40 §40.4.4)
- **Branchless binary search** — Khuong & Morin's half-interval search with the select written as arithmetic (`base += (base[half] < key) * half`), a `cmov` chain with no mispredicts and no speculation; faster than `std::lower_bound` on unpredictable keys, slower on a predictable walk; Clang 18 turns the *ternary* form back into a branch. (Ch. 32 §32.4.6)
- **Sorting network** — a fixed sequence of compare-exchange (min/max) pairs that sorts a small fixed-size array in flat, branch-free time (Batcher's odd-even merge: 19 for 8 elements); for ranking ≤ 16 venue quotes. (Ch. 32 §32.4.6)
- **SWAR (SIMD within a register)** — treating a 64-bit word as eight byte lanes: eight ASCII digits become their value with one subtract and three multiply-shift-mask steps, validated with two masks; the fixed-width integer parser at 1.5 ns. (Ch. 71 §71.4.1)
- **Digit-pair table** — the `constexpr` `"00".."99"` string that formats two digits per lookup; what `std::to_chars` already uses internally, and what fixed-width field formatting (`put_fixed8`) is built from. (Ch. 71 §71.4.6, Ch. 21 §21.4.2)
- **Performance contract** — a measured latency invariant written as a `static_assert` next to the type it governs (layout, shm-safety, lock-freedom, power-of-two capacity, `noexcept`, folding), with the chapter in the message, collected in a `contracts_test.cpp` compiled on every toolchain. (Ch. 29 §29.4.3)
- **`--wrap` trap** — partially linking the hot-path objects with `ld -r --wrap=malloc --wrap=_Znwm` so their allocator references resolve to `__wrap_*` definitions that abort in test builds: a link-time no-malloc assertion whose unit is the translation unit. (Ch. 29 §29.4.4)
- **Hot-path lint scope** — marking hot functions (`[[gnu::hot]]`, a namespace, a TU list) so that clang-tidy's `performance-*` set and the custom AST-matcher checks (`hasAttr("attr::Hot")`) enforce the book's rules there as errors and stay quiet on the cold path. (Ch. 29 §29.2.2, §29.4.2)

## F.6 Networking, I/O & Transport terms

- **Aeron / eRPC / Homa** — purpose-built low-latency transports: **Aeron** (reliable UDP/multicast/IPC messaging, LMAX lineage), **eRPC** (µs RPC on commodity NICs), **Homa** (receiver-driven, connectionless, HOL-free). (Ch. 85)
- **aRFS** — accelerated Receive Flow Steering: the kernel programs the NIC to steer each flow to the core its socket is consumed on, automatically. (Ch. 77)
- **Busy-poll / poll-mode** — spinning on a NIC/socket for new data instead of taking interrupts — removing interrupt and wakeup latency at the cost of a burned core. (Ch. 63, 82)
- **Cut-through / store-and-forward / layer-1** — switch types: **cut-through** starts forwarding after the header (frame-size-independent, sub-µs); **store-and-forward** receives the whole frame first (serialization delay, grows with size); **layer-1** is a physical cross-connect/replicator (~4–5 ns port-to-port, no parsing). (Ch. 80)
- **DCQCN** — Data Center Quantized Congestion Notification: the RoCEv2 congestion-control algorithm that reacts to ECN marks by cutting rate, keeping buffers below the PFC threshold. (Ch. 84)
- **DDIO (Data Direct I/O)** — Intel feature that DMAs inbound NIC data straight into L3 (skipping DRAM) — a receive-latency win that can also pollute the hot working set. (Ch. 76)
- **devmem TCP** — kernel receive path that splits headers to the host and DMAs the payload directly into device memory (GPU/FPGA), never touching host DRAM. (Ch. 66)
- **DPDK** — Data Plane Development Kit: the vendor-neutral kernel-bypass framework (poll-mode drivers, hugepage mempools, userspace stacks). (Ch. 82)
- **Drop reason (`skb:kfree_skb`)** — the enumerated cause (`SKB_DROP_REASON_*`: `SOCKET_RCVBUFF`, `IP_RPFILTER`, `NO_SOCKET`, `TCP_OFO_DROP` …) and the kernel function recorded by the tracepoint every discarded packet passes through; read with `dropwatch`, `perf trace`/`record` or `bpftrace` — the forensic layer beneath the counters. Hardware drops (ring, port FIFO) never reach it. (Ch. 115 §115.4.2)
- **eBPF** — extended Berkeley Packet Filter: a verified in-kernel VM for low-overhead production tracing (bpftrace) and programmable packet paths (XDP). (Ch. 81)
- **ECN** — Explicit Congestion Notification: a switch *marks* (rather than drops) a congested packet, signaling the sender to slow — the proactive layer of lossless Ethernet. (Ch. 84)
- **EDT (Earliest Departure Time)** — the Linux egress model where each packet carries a departure time; pacing and precise scheduling are both EDT settings. (Ch. 79)
- **ef_vi / Onload** — Solarflare/AMD kernel-bypass APIs: **ef_vi** is the raw, lowest-latency layer-2 API; **Onload** transparently accelerates existing socket apps (userspace TCP). **ExaNIC / `libexanic`** (Cisco/Exablaze) is the other proprietary bypass API — direct access to the NIC's RX/TX rings with hardware timestamps and an on-card FPGA option. (Ch. 75, 82)
- **`epoll` / readiness vs completion** — the Linux readiness-notification multiplexer: register fds, `epoll_wait` reports which are *ready*, then you issue the `read`/`write` yourself (two syscalls per event; level- vs edge-triggered); io_uring's *completion* model instead hands back finished operations. (Ch. 63, 65)
- **Flow steering (Flow Director / `rte_flow`)** — directing a *specific* flow to a *specific* NIC queue/core by exact-match rules, overriding RSS's random hash. (Ch. 77)
- **GRO / GSO / TSO / LRO** — receive/transmit segmentation offloads that batch segments for CPU efficiency at a latency cost; GRO/LRO are turned off on the hot RX path. (Ch. 69)
- **HOL blocking (head-of-line)** — TCP's ordered byte stream stalls *every* later message behind a single lost segment until it's retransmitted; message transports avoid it. (Ch. 85)
- **IGMP snooping / querier** — a switch learns which ports joined a multicast group by snooping IGMP (IPv4) / MLD (IPv6) membership reports and forwards group traffic only there; the state is a soft lease refreshed by hosts' answers to periodic **General Queries** from a **querier**. No querier → entries age out after the Group Membership Interval (~260 s with RFC defaults) → the feed goes dark on every host with the host's own membership table unchanged. (Ch. 74 §74.2, 80)
- **Incast** — many senders hitting one receiver simultaneously (a fan-in / scatter-gather), congesting the fabric and collapsing TCP throughput. (Ch. 84, 85)
- **InfiniBand** — the lossless, credit-flow-controlled fabric RDMA was designed for (native verbs, no PFC/ECN tuning needed); **RoCEv2** carries the same verbs over Ethernet. (Ch. 83)
- **io_uring / SQE / CQE / SQPOLL** — the modern Linux async-I/O interface: the application writes **SQEs** (submission-queue entries) into a shared ring and reads **CQEs** (completion-queue entries) back; **SQPOLL** has a kernel thread poll the submission ring (no `io_uring_enter` syscall), **IOPOLL** polls the device for completions; plus registered/provided buffers, multishot, and zero-copy send/receive. (Ch. 65)
- **IPC (inter-process communication)** — moving data between processes: pipes, sockets and message queues (a syscall per message) vs **shared-memory** rings and seqlock snapshots (no syscall on the hot path) — the latter is the data plane of a process-per-role trading system. Not Instructions Per Cycle (F.2). (Ch. 67; Ch. 34, 103)
- **Kernel bypass** — mapping the NIC's rings into userspace and polling them, sending/receiving packets with no kernel, no syscalls, no copies, no interrupts. (Ch. 82)
- **LAUNCHTIME / `SO_TXTIME`** — NIC-hardware / socket mechanism to transmit a packet at an exact scheduled nanosecond, via the ETF qdisc. (Ch. 79)
- **`MSG_ZEROCOPY` / `TCP_ZEROCOPY_RECEIVE`** — kernel zero-copy send/receive: the NIC DMAs to/from *your* buffer, at the cost of asynchronous buffer-lifetime management (send) or page alignment (receive). (Ch. 66)
- **Nagle / delayed ACK** — TCP throughput heuristics whose interaction can inject a ~40 ms stall into small request-response flows; disabled with `TCP_NODELAY` / `TCP_QUICKACK`. (Ch. 69)
- **NAPI** — the kernel's interrupt-mitigation receive model: the first packet's interrupt disables further RX interrupts and switches the driver to polling the ring until it is drained (then re-arms); `busy_poll` extends the same polling into the socket layer. (Ch. 63 — native I/O; Ch. 75)
- **NAK** — Negative Acknowledgment: a receiver requesting retransmission of specific missing messages (reliable multicast); a *NAK storm* is many subscribers requesting at once. (Ch. 72)
- **One-sided RDMA** — a remote READ/WRITE that completes on the NIC without involving the remote CPU (vs two-sided send/recv). (Ch. 83)
- **Pacing** — spreading sends over time to a smooth rate (`SO_MAX_PACING_RATE` / FQ) to avoid self-inflicted microbursts. (Ch. 79)
- **Pause frame / PFC** — Ethernet flow control: a NIC or switch whose buffers fill sends PAUSE (or per-priority PFC) and the sender stops, moving the drop into the sender's buffers; `tx_pause` on a market-data host means *this host is too slow*, `rx_pause` means the switch is congested toward it. Off on market-data NICs by convention; on, by design and monitored, on lossless RDMA fabrics. (Ch. 84, 115 §115.2.3)
- **PFC (Priority Flow Control)** — link-level pause frames (802.1Qbb) that make Ethernet lossless per traffic class; the backstop under RoCEv2, with head-of-line-blocking and deadlock hazards. (Ch. 84)
- **PGM** — Pragmatic General Multicast: a NAK-based reliable-multicast protocol; a model for feed recovery. (Ch. 72)
- **PMD (poll-mode driver)** — a userspace NIC driver that polls the RX ring instead of taking interrupts — the mechanism of kernel bypass. (Ch. 82)
- **PTP / PHC** — Precision Time Protocol (IEEE-1588): sub-µs clock synchronization across hosts via NIC hardware timestamps; the **PHC** is the PTP Hardware Clock. The basis of true wire-to-wire measurement. (Ch. 78)
- **`recvmmsg` / `sendmmsg`** — receive/send many datagrams in one syscall, amortizing the per-message syscall cost for high-rate UDP. (Ch. 77)
- **RoCEv2** — RDMA over Converged Ethernet: RDMA carried on an Ethernet fabric, requiring PFC/ECN/DCQCN to be lossless. (Ch. 83, 84)
- **RSS (Receive Side Scaling)** — the NIC hashes a flow tuple to spread packets across RX queues/cores — good for bulk load, bad for the hot path (random placement, feed splitting). (Ch. 75, 77)
- **`rx_missed` vs `rx_fifo` / port discards** — the two NIC-side drop addresses: **ring full** (`rx_missed_errors`, `rx_out_of_buffer`, `rx_nodesc_drop_cnt` — the host did not poll fast enough) vs **port FIFO** (`rx_fifo_errors`, `rx_discards_phy`, `port.rx_dropped` — the DMA/PCIe path could not drain). Same symptom, different owner; every driver names them differently. (Ch. 115 §115.2.2)
- **RS-FEC / PAM4** — the forward error correction mandatory on most 25/100 G links and the four-level signalling of 50/100 G-per-lane links; ~100–250 ns per link of fixed latency, which is why 10 G NRZ with no FEC is often the lowest-latency link for small frames. (Ch. 80 §80.2)
- **`active-backup` bond / `miimon` / gratuitous ARP** — the failover-only bonding mode for an order session (one TCP flow cannot be split), its link-monitoring interval (the failover time), and the ARP broadcast that moves the MAC to the new port; never a bond over market-data NICs. (Ch. 80 §80.4.5)
- **Permanent neighbour** — an ARP/ND entry pinned with `nud permanent` so the first packet after a quiet spell never waits for a resolution. (Ch. 68 §68.4.3)
- **kTLS** — the kernel's TLS record layer (`TCP_ULP "tls"`): encrypt/decrypt in the socket path, removing the user-space copies and library overhead; offloadable to the NIC. (Ch. 86 §86.2.1)
- **0-RTT (early data)** — TLS 1.3 application data in the client's first flight; replayable by construction and therefore never used for orders. (Ch. 86 §86.2.2)
- **WebSocket frame / masking** — the 2–14-byte framing over an upgraded HTTP connection; client-to-server payloads are XOR-masked with a 4-byte key. (Ch. 86 §86.2.3)
- **On-Demand JSON parsing** — forward-only, no-DOM decoding (simdjson) that validates with SIMD and hands back views into the buffer; ~100 ns per book update vs ~2 µs for a DOM. (Ch. 86 §86.4.4)
- **ENA / LLQ / placement group** — AWS's SR-IOV-class adapter, its low-latency queue mode, and the instance placement that keeps your hosts on one low-latency partition; the cloud host's levers. (Ch. 86 §86.4.6)
- **Microwave regeneration / fade** — each tower demodulates and re-modulates (µs per hop, so hop count sets latency); rain and ducting produce error bursts the modem's FEC absorbs, then loses, then the link drops — detected from signal level and per-line loss before it reaches the book. (Appendix K.3)
- **Serialization delay** — the time to clock a frame onto the wire (frame_bits ÷ link_rate); why cut-through and high, uniform link speeds matter. (Ch. 80)
- **`SO_REUSEPORT`** — multiple sockets bound to one port with kernel/BPF load-balancing of flows across threads/cores. (Ch. 77)
- **SSM (source-specific multicast)** — an (S,G) join (`MCAST_JOIN_SOURCE_GROUP`, IGMPv3 source filter, groups in 232/8) that forwards only one sender's traffic for the group: no rendezvous point, no rogue senders, faster first-on-link joins. (Ch. 74 §74.4.1)
- **`time_squeeze`** — `/proc/net/softnet_stat`'s count of NAPI poll rounds that exhausted `netdev_budget`/`netdev_budget_usecs` with packets still in the ring and deferred the rest to `ksoftirqd`; latency, not loss — and the precursor of ring-full drops on the market-data path. (Ch. 63, 115 §115.2.3)
- **TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking)** — IEEE 802.1 standards for scheduled, deterministic Ethernet (the time-aware shaper, 802.1Qbv). (Ch. 79)
- **USDT** — User Statically-Defined Tracing: `DTRACE_PROBE`-style probe points compiled into your binary (a `nop` until a tracer attaches) that `bpftrace`/eBPF can hook with near-zero cost when disabled — the production-safe way to expose hot-path events. (Ch. 81)
- **Verbs / QP / CQ / MR** — the RDMA API: **Queue Pairs** (send/recv work queues), **Completion Queues**, and **Memory Regions** (registered/pinned memory the NIC may DMA). (Ch. 83)
- **White Rabbit / grandmaster** — sub-nanosecond clock distribution extending PTP; the *grandmaster* is the root clock, disciplined by GPS/PPS. (Ch. 78)
- **XDP (eXpress Data Path) / AF_XDP** — in-driver eBPF packet processing (drop/redirect/filter at the earliest hook); **AF_XDP** is a zero-copy socket for userspace fast paths — a middle ground between the kernel stack and full bypass. (Ch. 81)
- **Zero-copy** — the NIC DMAs to/from the *final* buffer, eliminating the intermediate kernel copy (`MSG_ZEROCOPY`, io_uring zero-copy, devmem TCP). (Ch. 66)

- **`TPACKET_V3` ring (`AF_PACKET` `PACKET_RX_RING`)** — the kernel's memory-mapped capture ring: frames written into large blocks handed to user space when full or after `tp_retire_blk_tov`, one `poll()` per block, `PACKET_FANOUT` across cores, `PACKET_STATISTICS` for the kernel's drops; kept every frame of a 1.1 Mpps burst that a UDP socket with 1 µs of work per packet lost half of. (Ch. 110 §110.2.2, §110.3, §110.4.1)
- **Capture-point fidelity** — what each capture point loses: in-app journal (what the app applied), host `AF_PACKET` (NIC/ring drops, software stamps), NIC mirror, switch SPAN (silent drops under oversubscription, µs of queueing jitter, no hardware stamp), passive optical tap + L1 replicator (nothing; the reference), capture card/appliance (hardware stamps ≤ ~10 ns). Capture below the layer that drops. (Ch. 110 §110.2.1, Ch. 80 §80.4.4)
- **SPAN vs tap** — a switch's mirror port shares an oversubscribable egress queue and delays/drops under exactly the bursts being measured; a passive tap copies every bit with a fixed propagation delay; measure SPAN against the tap before trusting it. (Ch. 80 §80.4.4, Ch. 110 §110.2.1)
- **Timing-faithful replay** — sending each captured packet at its captured instant relative to the first; only a spinning pacer on a dedicated core (gap error 40 ns p50 / 2 µs p99 on the VM) or hardware launch time (`SO_TXTIME`, DPDK) reproduces µs burst cadence; `nanosleep` pacing stretched 2 µs gaps by ~10 µs and unpaced replay compressed the open 9×; validated by capturing the replay at a tap. (Ch. 110 §110.2.4, §110.3, §110.4.3)
- **`TCP_USER_TIMEOUT` / keepalive / `tcp_retries2`** — the three TCP liveness timers: the per-socket cap on unacked data (2 s → `ETIMEDOUT` at 2.25 s), the idle-session probes (idle 1 s / intvl 1 s / cnt 3 → 4.1 s), and the system-wide retransmission budget that keeps a dead peer ESTABLISHED for ≈ 924 s by default; set so TCP fails before the session heartbeat would. (Ch. 69 §69.4.4, Ch. 106 §106.2.3)

## F.7 Hardware Acceleration & Devices

- **VFIO** — the kernel's framework for safe userspace drivers: a *container* (an IOMMU domain), a *group* (the unit of ownership), a *device fd* through which regions (BARs) are mmap'd, DMA memory is pinned and IOMMU-mapped (`VFIO_IOMMU_MAP_DMA`), and MSI-X vectors are wired to eventfds; the device can reach only what the process mapped. DPDK, SPDK, QEMU passthrough and a custom FPGA driver all use it. (Ch. 88 §88.2.1, §88.4.2)
- **UIO** — `uio_pci_generic`/`igb_uio`: a minimal kernel driver exporting BARs as `/dev/uioN` mmap regions and one legacy interrupt as a `read()`; no DMA mapping (the process computes physical addresses itself) — unsafe with an IOMMU, deprecated. (Ch. 88 §88.2.1)
- **IOMMU group / ACS** — the smallest set of devices the IOMMU can isolate from each other, decided by the PCIe topology: devices behind a bridge without *Access Control Services* share a group and must all be handed to VFIO together ("not viable" otherwise). (Ch. 88 §88.2.2)
- **IOVA / IOTLB** — the address a device puts on the bus (I/O virtual address), translated by the IOMMU through its own page tables and cached in the IOTLB; hugepage-backed DMA regions keep the IOTLB warm; `iommu=pt` identity-maps trusted kernel-driver devices. (Ch. 87 §87.2.2, Ch. 88 §88.2.2, §88.4.4)
- **SR-IOV / virtual function (VF)** — a lightweight PCIe function a NIC carves out of its physical function (own BARs, MSI-X vectors, queues; shared link, DDIO window and usually PHC), passed to a guest or a process through VFIO; the PF keeps the control plane. (Appendix S §S.5; Ch. 88)
- **VM exit / EPT (NPT)** — the trap from a guest to the hypervisor when the hardware cannot virtualise an operation in place (`cpuid` ~6 µs in the test guest; `HLT`; MSR access; an *EPT violation* when the extended page tables — the guest-physical → host-physical level, making a guest TLB miss a two-dimensional walk — lack a mapping). (Appendix S §S.2)
- **Posted interrupts / APICv** — hardware delivery of an interrupt straight to a running vCPU without a VM exit (Intel APICv + VT-d posted interrupts, AMD AVIC); what makes a passed-through VF's MSI-X cheap in a guest. (Appendix S §S.2, §S.5)
- **`kvm-clock`** — the paravirtual clocksource: a host-updated per-vCPU page the guest vDSO reads (24.8 vs 20.9 ns for `clock_gettime` via the TSC on the test guest); steppable by the host, not a PHC — a guest that timestamps uses the invariant TSC (`-cpu host,+invtsc`, `clocksource=tsc`) and a VF's PHC. (Appendix S §S.3)
- **CXL memory expander / CPU-less NUMA node** — DRAM behind a CXL link presented as a NUMA node with no CPUs at ~170–250 ns; capacity, not latency; kept off the hot process with `--membind` and `numa_balancing=0` (tiering demotion/promotion is a page-migration noise source). (Ch. 9 §9.2.5, Ch. 18 §18.2.4)
- **BAR (Base Address Register)** — PCIe config that maps a device's registers/memory into the host address space for MMIO. (Ch. 87)
- **CUDA / warp / occupancy** — NVIDIA's GPU programming model; a **warp** is 32 threads executed in lockstep; **occupancy** is how fully the GPU's warp slots are used. (Ch. 89)
- **DMA (Direct Memory Access)** — a device reading/writing host memory without the CPU; the bulk-transfer mechanism behind NICs and accelerators. (Ch. 87)
- **DOCA / P4** — DPU programming models: **DOCA** (NVIDIA BlueField SDK, C/C++ on ARM cores), **P4** (a declarative packet-processing language for match/action pipelines). (Ch. 92)
- **DPU / SmartNIC** — a NIC with a programmable processor on it (ARM cores and/or FPGA) between wire and host, for offloading packet processing, RDMA, security, and even feed handling. (Ch. 92)
- **FPGA / HLS / RTL** — Field-Programmable Gate Array (configurable hardware, deterministic-nanosecond pipelines); described in **RTL** (Verilog/VHDL at register-transfer level — cycle-exact registers and the logic between them, which synthesis lowers to a gate-level netlist; lowest latency) or **HLS** (C/C++ compiled to hardware, easier). (Ch. 91)
- **GPUDirect / P2P DMA** — peer-to-peer DMA directly between devices (NIC↔GPU/FPGA) bypassing host memory. (Ch. 87, 89)
- **IOMMU** — I/O Memory Management Unit: translates and protects device DMA addresses (a TLB for devices). (Ch. 87)
- **MMIO** — Memory-Mapped I/O: accessing device registers as memory; a non-posted *read* is a full PCIe round-trip. (Ch. 87)
- **MPI** — Message Passing Interface: the standard for distributed compute across cluster nodes (point-to-point + collectives), used for backtesting/risk. (Ch. 90)
- **MSI-X** — Message-Signaled Interrupts: a device signals a completion by writing memory (vs polling). (Ch. 87)
- **PCIe** — Peripheral Component Interconnect Express: the bus every NIC/GPU/FPGA sits behind; its round-trip latency is the floor under every offload decision. (Ch. 87)
- **Posted vs non-posted** — a *posted* write is fire-and-forget (~tens of ns); a *non-posted* read stalls for a full round-trip (~hundreds of ns) — why device-register reads are costly. (Ch. 87)
- **VFIO** — the kernel framework that hands a PCIe device to userspace safely (IOMMU-protected DMA, MMIO mapping, interrupts) — how DPDK and other bypass stacks bind a NIC without a kernel driver. (Ch. 82, 87)
- **Write-combining / doorbell** — *write-combining* memory collects adjacent stores into one bus transaction before posting it to the device; a *doorbell* is the single MMIO write that tells a NIC/accelerator new descriptors are ready — the posted (cheap) direction of PCIe. (Ch. 12, 87)

## F.8 Security, Isolation & Operations

- **LSM (SELinux / AppArmor)** — Linux Security Modules: hooks on security-relevant syscalls resolved through a policy cache (SELinux's AVC); ~30–200 ns per hooked syscall on housekeeping threads, ms on a policy reload, nothing on a syscall-free hot path — kept *enforcing* for the box with the trading unit in a permissive domain rather than `selinux=0`. (Ch. 101 §101.4.9)
- **Audit (`auditctl`, `auditd`)** — the kernel's syscall audit trail: ~1–3 µs per audited syscall, ~50–100 ns per unaudited one for an auditable task; `auditctl -a task,never` first, before the trading unit starts, exempts it — the targeted alternative to `audit=0`. (Ch. 101 §101.4.9)
- **SSBD / the seccomp trap** — Speculative Store Bypass Disable: a per-task mitigation that forbids loads from bypassing older stores with unresolved addresses (a measured 3.5× on disambiguation-bound code, 0 on code without the shape); set by `prctl` — or, on a `spec_store_bypass_disable=seccomp` kernel, by installing *any* seccomp filter without `SECCOMP_FILTER_FLAG_SPEC_ALLOW`. Boot `=prctl`, pass `SPEC_ALLOW`, assert `Speculation_Store_Bypass: thread vulnerable` per hot thread. (Ch. 101 §101.4.9; Ch. 6 §6.2.3)
- **Kernel lockdown / Yama `ptrace_scope`** — zero-latency restrictions that constrain tooling: lockdown (`integrity` under Secure Boot) refuses `/dev/mem`, `resourceN` mmap, MSR writes, unsigned modules and `kexec`, and under `confidentiality` kernel-memory reads by BPF/`perf`; Yama limits who may `ptrace`-attach (`rr`, `gdb -p`). Do MSR writes from a signed boot unit; grant the forensics user `CAP_SYS_PTRACE`. (Ch. 101 §101.4.9; Ch. 12 §12.4.3; Ch. 88 §88.5)
- **Microcode revision** — the CPU's loaded microcode (`/proc/cpuinfo`), a latency variable under change control: it carries mitigations that slow user-space instructions (Downfall's gathers), is loaded early (initramfs) or late (refused since 6.6 for feature-changing revisions), and is part of the host fingerprint. (Ch. 6 §6.4.5)
- **Fault catalogue / game day** — the table of every fault the stack can suffer with its blast radius, detection, response owner and injection method; a scheduled, scripted drill of one of them on the production-shaped environment with recovery measured as a distribution and gated. (Ch. 116 §116.4.7)
- **Faults as sequenced inputs** — a fault (gap, hang, slow ack) expressed as an input event at a sequence number, scheduled from the seed and journaled, so the run with the fault replays bit-identically and shrinks. (Ch. 116 §116.4.7; Ch. 117 §117.4.6)
- **RPO / RTO / MTTR** — recovery point objective (how much of the sequenced log a recovered site may be missing), recovery time objective (time from loss to trading), and the measured mean time to recover with its tail over drills. (Appendix K.9; Ch. 103 §103.3, Ch. 112 §112.3)
- **Cold / warm / hot standby (site tiers)** — nothing running / replicas folding the shipped journal with sessions unconnected / an armed second instance with its own sessions and feeds; RPO and RTO from hours to seconds, cost rising with each. (Appendix K.9)
- **Journal shipping** — asynchronous cross-site replication of the sequenced log, the only kind the speed of light permits on the order path (5 ns/m). (Appendix K.9; Ch. 111)
- **Failover (detect-to-promote)** — the measured time from a primary's death to the standby's first accepted order, floored by the lease length (~1.8 ms with a 2 ms lease, ~0.96 ms with 0.5 ms). (Ch. 112 §112.3)
- **NIC baseline triple** — the pinned kernel + driver + firmware under which a NIC's wire-to-app histogram was recorded; re-run after any change to any of the three or to a module parameter, attested at the gate. (Ch. 75 §75.3, §75.4.5)
- **In-flight order table** — the gateway's preallocated, single-writer table of working orders indexed by the ClOrdID's slot, with a live bitmap and per-symbol/per-strategy live lists; ack/fill correlation is index + one generation compare (~21 ns including the timestamp); rebuilt from the journal after a crash. (Ch. 105 §105.2.2, §105.3)
- **Unknown-state matrix** — the per-order-state × per-order-type table that says, after a crash, whether an order may be resent (only if the journal proves it never left — and usually not even then), must be queried, waited for, or cancelled idempotently; IOCs are never resent. (Ch. 105 §105.2.3)
- **Journal-before-send** — the gateway writes the send record (ClOrdID, intent, gate decision) to the journal *before* the bytes leave, so a post-crash rebuild knows every id that may be at the venue; generations are restored from the journal so no pre-crash id is reissued. (Ch. 105 §105.4.1)
- **Degraded mode (load shedding)** — the journaled, single-owner state machine `Normal → Conflating → Pulled → SafeState` entered on staleness-budget breaches, each mode a written shedding plan; read by the hot path as one word. (Ch. 50 §50.2.4, §50.4.4)
- **ASLR / PIE** — Address Space Layout Randomization / Position-Independent Executable: load-time memory-safety hardening, ~free on the hot path. (Ch. 101)
- **CAT (Cache Allocation Technology) / RDT** — Intel Resource Director Technology partitions the shared L3 (and, via MBA, memory bandwidth) so a noisy neighbor can't evict the hot working set even when the core is pinned. (Ch. 58)
- **CET / CFI** — Control-flow Enforcement Technology (a hardware shadow stack) / Control-Flow Integrity: hardening against control-flow hijacking; CFI's cost tracks indirect-call density. (Ch. 101)
- **CLOS (Class of Service)** — an RDT group assigned cache ways (CAT) and/or a bandwidth throttle (MBA); cores or tasks are assigned to a CLOS. (Ch. 58)
- **CMT / MBM** — Cache Monitoring Technology / Memory Bandwidth Monitoring: per-group L3 occupancy and memory-bandwidth counters — the "see who's in the cache" half of RDT. (Ch. 58)
- **Crash-only design** — the only way to stop a process is to crash it, and the only way to start is to recover — so the recovery path is always tested and fast. (Ch. 103)
- **Deterministic state machine** — a pure, side-effect-free decision core (time and randomness passed *in* as inputs) that replays a captured input stream bit-for-bit; the basis of debugging, backtesting, and compliance. (Ch. 103)
- **Sequencer** — the single process (or switch/FPGA) that stamps a global sequence number on every input — ticks after A/B arbitration, acks/fills, timer fires, commands, config changes, strategy decisions — and fans the sequenced stream out over shared-memory rings or multicast; the sequenced log is the journal. One hop on the critical path; the system's single point of failure, with its own standby. (Ch. 111; Appendix J.7)
- **Replicated state machine (state-machine replication)** — several copies of one deterministic `apply(state, input)` fold, fed the same totally ordered input stream, hold bit-identical state; verified by comparing running digests at equal sequence numbers. The sequencer is the degenerate agreement protocol (one proposer). (Ch. 111 §111.2.2; Ch. 103 §103.2.2)
- **Fencing token** — a monotonically increasing number issued with a lease or leadership grant and carried on every output, so that a downstream (the venue session, a storage node) rejects writes from a stale leader; the mechanism that keeps two emitters from both acting after a messy failover. Defined here; the order-path failover design that uses it is Ch. 112 (§112.2.3, §112.4.1: the epoch of the lease is the token; checked by the venue double, the gateway's session layer or the sequencer's emitter-control event). (Ch. 111 §111.2.3, §111.4.5)
- **Drift (configuration)** — a tuned box silently untuning itself: package/kernel updates re-applying profiles, shared configuration roles flipping sysctls, agents attaching perf events, firmware updates resetting BIOS policy, the environment (thermal throttling, aging DIMMs). Prevented by configuration as code (Appendix C.6), detected by the host-health sampler and the pre-open attestation. (Ch. 114 §114.2.3, Appendix M)
- **Fingerprint (host)** — the recorded baseline a box is signed off with: kernel/cmdline/microcode/BIOS/NIC-firmware provenance, isolation masks and IRQ map, sysctls, `ethtool`/`resctrl` settings, steady-state interrupt-row rates, SMI count — plus the micro-suite's latency numbers; generated from the release's declaration and compared against the live box at T-60. (Ch. 61 §61.4.3, 114 §114.4.4, Appendix M.2/M.6)
- **`_FORTIFY_SOURCE`** — compile-time (and light runtime) bounds checks where the buffer size is known; usually free, kept on. (Ch. 101)
- **High-water mark** — the maximum a gauge reached since an epoch (queue depth, worst stage latency, pool occupancy): written by the hot thread with a compare and a conditional store, reset by the scraper bumping an epoch the thread acknowledges — never by writing the thread's line. (Ch. 100 §100.2.1)
- **Hot reload** — changing config / strategy parameters / code in a *running* process without dropping a tick, via atomic pointer swap or seqlock/RCU-published snapshots and safe reclamation. (Ch. 102)
- **Invariant alert ("any nonzero delta")** — the alert class for quantities whose correct value on a tuned, warmed-up box is exactly zero (involuntary switches, migrations, minor faults, IPI rows, SMIs, post-A/B gaps mid-session, `operator new` calls): no window, no percentile, no threshold — the first delta is the ticket, with the fingerprint-table diagnosis attached. (Ch. 100 §100.4.4, 114 §114.2.1)
- **MBA (Memory Bandwidth Allocation)** — RDT throttling of a class's memory bandwidth, to stop a streaming neighbor (capture, logging) from saturating the memory controller. (Ch. 58)
- **Readiness gate / state machine** — the per-process word `cold → warming → syncing → reconciled → armed → trading → disarmed` published in the telemetry segment and read by the order path with one predicted branch, advanced only by the timed pre-open gates (T-60 host attestation, T-30 clock/feed/network, T-5 session/positions/warm-up/kill-switch) and dropped to `disarmed` by any failing invariant. (Appendix M; Ch. 103 §103.4.4)
- **`resctrl`** — the Linux pseudo-filesystem (`/sys/fs/resctrl`) interface to Intel RDT (CAT / MBA / CMT / MBM). (Ch. 58)
- **Scraper (telemetry)** — the off-core process that maps the telemetry segment read-only, snapshots cumulative counters and histogram buckets at 1–10 Hz, diffs for interval histograms, merges by adding buckets, computes percentiles last and exports them — and reads the host and NIC counters on the same timeline. Its interference on the hot core (an RFO per touched line per scrape) is measured, not assumed. (Ch. 100 §100.3, §100.4.3)
- **`seccomp`** — Linux syscall filtering; installed at the steady-state boundary it costs ~nothing (the hot path makes no syscalls) while containing a compromise. (Ch. 101)
- **Spectre / Meltdown** — speculative-execution vulnerabilities; the mitigations (retpoline, IBRS, `mitigations=`) carry a latency cost you may deliberately reduce on an isolated, single-tenant box. (Ch. 6, 101)
- **Telemetry segment** — the versioned shared-memory layout in which each hot thread owns cache lines of monotonic counters, a seqlock-guarded gauge set, high-water marks and per-stage log-linear histograms, written with plain stores and never read by the thread's own peers; the substrate for metrics, heartbeats, progress sequences and the readiness word. (Ch. 100 §100.4.1)
- **UB (Undefined Behavior)** — behavior the standard leaves undefined; in low-latency C++ it is simultaneously a correctness bug, a security vulnerability, and a source of nondeterminism (and the optimizer may delete your checks around it). (Ch. 26, 101)
- **WAL (Write-Ahead Log)** — append-only journaling: persist the event durably *before* acting on it; the capture-journal and replay model. (Ch. 109)
- **Flight recorder** — an always-on, per-thread ring of fixed-size binary records (the last ~65k things the thread did, TSC-stamped) that is *frozen* on a trigger by swapping the ring pointer so the hot thread keeps writing while a housekeeping core drains the frozen copy; dumped raw by the crash handler on a fault. (Ch. 113 §113.4.2, 108 §108.4.3)
- **Always-on / targeted-live / offline-on-replay** — the three observation tiers: stores and `rdpmc` the hot thread pays for; bounded, trigger-frozen tools a housekeeping core arms per investigation (`perf --overwrite`, LBR/PT snapshots, hardware breakpoints, non-stop gdb); and the heavy tools on a deterministic replay. (Ch. 120 §120.2, §120.4.4)
- **Spike forensics** — the method from one over-budget trace record to a mechanism: localise the TSC window, convert every evidence stream into it, test each suspect against its fingerprint in the frozen rings, confirm with a knob or a replay. (Ch. 113)
- **State digest / divergence forensics** — an incremental crc32c of each mutation chained per event (+0.1 cycles), journaled beside the output, so a replay that disagrees with production is localised to the *first diverging event* by a digest diff or checkpoint bisection; with a determinism lint for the usual causes. (Ch. 103 §103.4.6)
- **Incident bundle** — the alignable (clock anchors), attributable (build IDs, flags, configuration hash, host fingerprint) and checkable (raw rings and sample windows with a manifest) collection a bounded script gathers on a housekeeping core after a spike, crash or drop. (Appendix N, Ch. 113 §113.4.5)
- **Kill-switch handoff** — the crash handler's first act: eight bytes to a pre-opened `eventfd` the gateway blocks on, so the dead shard's orders are being cancelled ~20 µs after the fault rather than after a core dump finishes. (Ch. 108 §108.4.3, 103 §103.4.4)


- **Deploy calendar** — the written, tooling-enforced map from change class to window and mechanism: parameters and strategy code intraday via hot reload, process binaries pre-open or by blue/green under canary rules, shm layout versions pre-open, kernel/firmware/BIOS on weekends, nothing during freezes (expiries, rebalances, macro releases). (Ch. 119 §119.2.1)
- **Blue/green cutover (process-per-role)** — start vN+1 beside vN on a reserved core attached to the live rings under its compatibility window, warm it, shadow it with digest parity, flip the gateway's accepted-writer epoch with one release store, keep vN running suppressed for instant rollback; measured: 400 000 ticks, 0 lost, 0 duplicated, a 1-tick gap, 10.5 µs worst tick after the flip. (Ch. 119 §119.3, §119.4.3)
- **Compatibility window** — the set of shared-memory layout versions (and message schema versions) a binary declares it can attach to; attach refuses outside it; layout changes ship read-both then write-new, or in a pre-open restart. (Ch. 119 §119.2.2, §119.4.2; Ch. 103 §103.4.8)
- **Shadow run** — the candidate as a replica on the live stream (or a layer-1-replicated feed) with outputs structurally suppressed (no venue credentials), compared with the incumbent on per-seq decision digest, timing and divergence content; auto-stop on parity below budget. (Ch. 119 §119.2.3, §119.4.4; Ch. 111 §111.2.2)
- **Canary shard** — the first emitting stage of a rollout: one symbol/venue shard under tighter risk limits for a bounded time, its SLIs (fill rate, reject rate, markout, tick-to-trade) compared with its own history and the sibling shards; rolled back by the flip. (Ch. 119 §119.2.3, §119.4.4)
- **A/A test (noise-floor calibration)** — running the *same* binary against itself to measure what a comparison can see: per-statistic run-to-run spread on a runner class (p50 exact, p99 22 %, p99.9 196 % on the VM), the tolerance any gate must exceed; applied to the paired production design too, where it exposed a 49–50 ns read-order offset between identical shadows. (Ch. 118 §118.3, §118.4.1; Ch. 119 §119.3, §119.4.5)
- **Performance gate** — a CI check that fails only when the bootstrap confidence interval's lower bound on the candidate−baseline difference clears the A/A-derived tolerance *and* a rank test agrees, on the median (per PR) and p99 (with tolerance), with the tail gated nightly on the tuned box; deterministic proxies (`instructions:u`, cachegrind, an asm hash) where timing is blind. (Ch. 118 §118.2.2, §118.4.2–§118.4.3)
- **Paired production comparison** — incumbent and candidate consuming the same ticks at the same instant, candidate orders suppressed, per-tick latency differenced so the host's noise cancels; needs its own A/A offset subtracted and counterbalancing, and cannot resolve effects under ~10 ns. (Ch. 119 §119.3, §119.4.5)
- **Deterministic simulation testing (DST)** — the real shell code (gateway, session machine) run single-threaded against a simulated clock and a fault-injecting venue double whose every effect is a scheduled event; the seed decides the faults; thousands of seeds per second; a failing seed reproduces under a debugger. 5 000 seeds in 117 ms; a resend bug found at seed 1. (Ch. 116 §116.2.3, §116.4.3)
- **Property-based test / shrinking** — random input sequences checked against invariants (conservation, non-negativity, idempotency, determinism) rather than expected values; a delta-debugging shrinker reduces a failing sequence to a minimal counterexample (200 → 2 inputs). (Ch. 116 §116.2.2, §116.4.2)
- **Golden master** — a small capture slice plus the digest and end state the core must reproduce; committed, updated only with a stated behaviour change. (Ch. 116 §116.4.4, Ch. 120 §120.4.1)
- **Test double as policy** — the clock, wire and venue injected as template parameters so production and simulation instantiate the same code; the production instantiation compiles to seam-free instructions, where a `virtual` seam adds indirect calls and devirtualization compares. (Ch. 116 §116.2.4, §116.4.5)
- **Profile lifecycle (PGO/AutoFDO/BOLT)** — train on a named replayed capture → merge (weighted) → store by revision → use → gate (fatal mismatch diagnostics, capture age, a two-mix replay check) → refresh on hot-code changes; a stale-workload profile cost +3–6 % and a stale-source profile +13–17 % measured. (Ch. 27 §27.4.5, Ch. 118 §118.4.5)
- **Release manifest** — the signed record of a release: build IDs and flags, layout windows, profile ids and training captures, configuration version, host fingerprint it was gated on, CI evidence and the rollout plan; the supervisor refuses binaries whose manifest does not match the host. (Ch. 119 §119.4.1)
- **Checkpoint (fork/COW vs double buffer)** — a (state, last-applied seq) pair at a sequence boundary; `fork()` blocks the caller ~16 ms/GiB and puts 1.3–3.7 µs COW faults on the hot writes' tail while the child lives (worse with THP); a seqlock-stable double buffer copied by a housekeeping core with a dirty-line bitmap costs +10 ns per write and copies only what changed; a replica snapshots for free. (Ch. 103 §103.4.7)
- **Segment epoch / torn-write recovery** — a shared segment's header carries an epoch bumped by each writer that takes ownership; records are committed by a sequence word stored last, so a reader accepts a record iff `commit == seq`; after a writer dies the reattaching process validates the header, proves the owner dead, discards the uncommitted tail and consumers roll back to the last committed seq; robust mutexes return `EOWNERDEAD`. (Ch. 103 §103.4.8)
