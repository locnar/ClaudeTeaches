# Appendix F — Glossary (HFT & Microarchitecture Terms)

> A quick reference for the acronyms and jargon used throughout the book. Each entry is a line or two with a pointer to the chapter that **defines and measures** it — follow the pointer for the real treatment; this is a reminder, not a substitute. Grouped by domain: trading (F.1), microarchitecture (F.2), memory & OS (F.3), concurrency (F.4), C++/toolchain (F.5), networking/I-O/transport (F.6), hardware acceleration & devices (F.7), and security/isolation/operations (F.8). Within each group, alphabetical.

## F.1 Trading & market-structure terms

- **A/B feed arbitration** — exchanges send market data on two redundant multicast lines (A and B); the feed handler takes whichever packet arrives first per sequence number and uses the other to fill gaps. (Ch. 54, 55; Appendix J.4)
- **BBO / Top-of-book** — Best Bid and Offer: the highest bid and lowest ask currently resting. The most-watched, most-updated level of the book. (Ch. 25; Appendix J.2)
- **Colocation (colo)** — placing your servers in the same datacenter as the exchange's matching engine to minimize wire distance (single-digit µs); physical proximity no software can substitute for. (Appendix K.1; Ch. 62 for the fabric and switches between you and the venue; Appendix A.7 for the cloud contrast)
- **Coordinated omission / HdrHistogram** — the benchmarking error of letting a slow response delay the *next* request, so the stall is counted once instead of for every request that should have been issued meanwhile — it hides exactly the tail you care about; **HdrHistogram** is the fixed-precision histogram (with a correction for it) used to record p99/p99.9 without allocation. (Ch. 3; Appendix L)
- **Cross-connect** — the physical fiber (or copper) run from your cage to the venue's access switch inside the colocation facility, typically latency-equalized so every participant's run is the same length. (Appendix K.1–K.2)
- **Dark feed** — a market-data channel that has stopped arriving with no sequence gap and no drop counter — typically a lapsed multicast membership (no IGMP querier on the VLAN, a refused join, `rp_filter`) or an upstream prune; detected only by time (per-channel last-packet age against the venue's heartbeat cadence) and by comparing the A and B lines. (Ch. 56, 55 §55.4.4)
- **Drop-copy** — a real-time copy of your order/fill activity fed to risk and compliance systems, separate from the order-entry session. (Ch. 53, 76 — where it appears as a session on the RX / untrusted-input path)
- **FAST** — FIX Adapted for STreaming: a compression encoding for market data (templates, implicit fields). (Ch. 54; Appendix J.5)
- **Feed handler** — the component that decodes the exchange's market-data feed off the wire, normalizes it, handles sequencing/gaps, and publishes a clean stream. (Ch. 54, 78; Appendix J.6)
- **HFT** — High-Frequency Trading: automated, latency-sensitive trading (market making, arbitrage) whose edge depends on reacting to market data within microseconds — the domain framing of this book and the reason its north-star metric is tail tick-to-trade latency. (Ch. 1; Appendix J)
- **FIX** — Financial Information eXchange: a tag-value text protocol for order entry and (FIX/FAST) market data. Verbose; often replaced on the hot path by binary encodings. (Ch. 54; Appendix J.5)
- **ITCH** — Nasdaq's binary market-data feed protocol (fixed-layout messages); the canonical zero-copy decode target. (Ch. 54; Appendix J.5)
- **IOC / FOK / TIF** — Time-In-Force variants: **Day** rests until the close, **IOC** (Immediate-Or-Cancel) takes what is available now and cancels the rest, **FOK** (Fill-Or-Kill) fills entirely or not at all; part of the order state machine the gateway tracks. (Appendix J.3)
- **Jitter** — run-to-run variation in the latency of the *same* operation (frequency changes, C-state exits, interrupts, migrations, GC); the enemy of the tail, measured as a distribution with `cyclictest` and the §82.3 decomposition. (Ch. 1, 6, 45)
- **Kill-switch** — a control that immediately cancels working orders and stops new ones to reach a safe state on failure; mandated by market-access risk rules (SEC Rule 15c3-5). Must live in a *surviving* process (the order gateway). (Ch. 76, 78)
- **Maker / Taker** — a *maker* posts resting liquidity (a limit order on the book); a *taker* removes it (a marketable order). Exchanges price the two differently (maker-taker fees). (Appendix J.1)
- **Matching engine** — the exchange component that matches incoming buy/sell orders against the book by price-time priority; the thing you race to. (Appendix J.1; Ch. 1; Ch. 62 for the fabric between you and it)
- **Microburst** — a sudden spike of packets (classically at the market open) that overflows NIC/switch buffers and manufactures the latency tail. (Ch. 54, 62)
- **NBBO** — National Best Bid and Offer: the best bid/ask across all US venues; the consolidated top-of-book. (Appendix J.2; Ch. 25 for the book data structure)
- **Order book / Limit order book (LOB)** — the set of resting buy/sell orders organized by price level; the core data structure a strategy reads and a venue matches against. (Ch. 25; Appendix J.2)
- **Order gateway** — the process that owns the venue session (order-entry connection, sequence numbers, working-order state) and executes the kill-switch even when a strategy crashes. (Ch. 78; Appendix J.6)
- **OMS / EMS** — Order / Execution Management System: the components that own order state (submission → ack → fills/cancels → terminal state) and routing; in a latency-sensitive system the OMS role is the order gateway process. (Appendix J.3, J.6; Ch. 78)
- **OUCH** — Nasdaq's binary order-entry protocol (the order-entry counterpart to ITCH). (Ch. 54; Appendix J.5)
- **Price level / depth** — a *price level* is one price on one side of the book with its aggregate resting quantity (and queue of orders); *depth* is how many levels away from the BBO a feed or book carries. The level, not the order, is usually the unit of the hot data structure. (Ch. 25; Appendix J.2)
- **Reg NMS / MiFID II** — the US (Regulation National Market System: order protection, the NBBO, market-access risk rules) and EU (Markets in Financial Instruments Directive II: clock-sync and reporting mandates, RTS 25) regulatory frameworks that shape venue connectivity, timestamping precision and pre-trade risk. (Ch. 60; Appendix J.1, K.4, K.7)
- **SBE** — Simple Binary Encoding: a fixed-layout binary message encoding (FIX community) designed for zero-copy, branch-free decode. (Ch. 54; Appendix J.5)
- **Sequence gap** — a discontinuity in a feed's sequence numbers signaling lost message(s); the trigger for recovery. (Ch. 55)
- **SLI / SLO** — Service Level Indicator / Objective: the measured quantity (wire-to-wire p99.9 per venue, feed age, post-A/B gaps, oldest unacknowledged order age, PTP offset) and the budget it must meet, set *per session phase* because the open is a different regime; alerts fire on SLI symptoms with multi-window conditions and hysteresis, never on averaged percentiles. (Ch. 75 §75.4.4)
- **Snapshot / retransmission recovery** — refilling a feed gap from a full book image (snapshot) or a resend of specific missing messages (retransmission) — the recovery channels beyond A/B. (Ch. 55)
- **Stale-data kill** — pulling quotes and refusing to take on an instrument whose book age exceeds its phase threshold, whose invariants (crossed/locked/zero-size/collar) fail, or whose channel is dark on both lines — the market-data counterpart of the kill-switch, driven by one per-instrument flag the hot path reads with a predicted branch. (Ch. 55 §55.4.4, 56)
- **Tail latency / p99 / p99.9** — the high percentiles of the latency *distribution* (the slowest 1 % / 0.1 % of events); the quantity trading systems are judged on, because the mean hides the lost races. Always report and reason about the distribution, never the average. (Ch. 1, 3)
- **Tick-to-trade** — the end-to-end latency from a market-data packet arriving on the wire to the resulting order leaving on the wire; the book's north-star metric. (Ch. 1, 82; Appendix J.7)
- **Tick size** — the minimum price increment for an instrument; drives price representation (scaled integers) and book layout. (Ch. 27)

## F.2 Microarchitecture terms

- **BMI / BMI2** — Bit Manipulation Instruction sets: `tzcnt`/`lzcnt`/`popcnt`, `blsr`, `pdep`/`pext` — single-instruction bit tricks for bitmap-based book occupancy and free lists; enabled by `-march=` (Haswell+ / x86-64-v3). (Ch. 28, Appendix D.2)
- **BTB** — Branch Target Buffer: predicts the *target* of indirect branches/calls (vtables, function pointers — Ch. 14); a BTB miss is an indirect-branch mispredict. (Ch. 13, 14)
- **Cache line** — the unit of transfer between cache levels and of coherence: 64 bytes on x86-64 (128 on some ARM cores, 256 on A64FX); every layout, padding and false-sharing decision is about which data shares a line. (Ch. 7 — the memory hierarchy; Ch. 9, 33; Appendix A.3)
- **DSB / µop cache** — Decoded Stream Buffer: caches decoded µops so the front-end skips re-decoding hot code; staying in the DSB avoids legacy-decode front-end stalls. (Ch. 12)
- **Flame graph** — a visualization of sampled stacks (`perf record -g` → `stackcollapse`/`flamegraph.pl`) in which width is time: the fastest way to see where a hot path's cycles go. (Ch. 2)
- **HITM** — "Hit Modified": a cache load that hits a line *modified* in another core's cache, forcing a cross-core transfer — the signature of false sharing / contention (`perf c2c`). (Ch. 33)
- **ILP** — Instruction-Level Parallelism: how many independent instructions the out-of-order core can have in flight at once; a long dependency chain serializes it, independent accumulators/lanes restore it. (Ch. 11)
- **IPC** — Instructions Per Cycle: throughput measure; low IPC signals stalls (cache/branch/front-end). The starting point of top-down analysis. Not the inter-process sense — see **IPC (inter-process communication)** in F.6. (Ch. 2, 11)
- **LFB** — Line Fill Buffer (MSHR): tracks outstanding cache misses; its count bounds **MLP** (how many misses can be in flight). Running out of LFBs stalls further misses. (Ch. 10)
- **LLC** — Last-Level Cache: the socket-shared L3; an LLC miss is a DRAM access, so `LLC-load-misses` (and LLC MPKI) is the counter that matters most. Partitionable with CAT. (Ch. 7, 2, 44)
- **MESI / MOESI** — cache-coherence protocols (Modified/Exclusive/Shared/Invalid, +Owned) that keep per-core caches consistent; the machinery behind HITM and false-sharing cost. (Ch. 7, 33)
- **MLP** — Memory-Level Parallelism: how many independent cache misses are serviced concurrently; high MLP hides DRAM latency — prefetching and independent chains raise it. (Ch. 10)
- **MPKI** — Misses Per Kilo-Instruction: cache (or TLB/branch) misses normalized to 1,000 retired instructions — the form in which miss counts are comparable across runs and against a baseline. (Ch. 2, 7)
- **µop (micro-op)** — the internal RISC-like operation x86 instructions decode into; the unit the back-end actually executes. (Ch. 11, 12)
- **PEBS** — Processor Event-Based Sampling: PMU sampling that records the precise instruction, address and latency of the sampled event (the basis of `perf mem`/`perf c2c` and skid-free attribution). (Ch. 2)
- **PMU** — Performance Monitoring Unit: the per-core hardware counters (`cycles`, `instructions`, cache/branch/TLB events) read through `perf_events`/`perf stat`; the instrument behind "measure first" and top-down analysis. (Ch. 2)
- **Prefetcher** — hardware that predicts and pre-loads cache lines ahead of demand; software prefetch (`__builtin_prefetch`) supplements it for irregular patterns. (Ch. 7, 10)
- **ROB** — Re-Order Buffer: holds in-flight instructions for out-of-order, speculative execution and in-order retirement; its size bounds the out-of-order window, and it is one of the resources two SMT siblings partition. (Ch. 11, 43)
- **SIMD / SSE / AVX / AVX-512** — Single Instruction, Multiple Data: vector instructions on 128/256/512-bit registers; auto-vectorization, intrinsics, layout-for-vectorization, and the AVX frequency-license (downclocking) caveat. (Ch. 29; Appendix A.2 for NEON/SVE)
- **SMT / Hyper-Threading** — Simultaneous Multi-Threading: two hardware threads sharing one core's resources (front-end, execution ports, L1/L2); a throughput-vs-latency trade — HFT hot cores usually run the sibling idle or SMT off. (Ch. 43)
- **STLB** — Second-level (shared) TLB: the larger, slower unified TLB behind the split L1 iTLB/dTLB; an STLB miss is a page-table walk. Huge pages multiply its reach. (Ch. 15)
- **Store buffer** — a per-core buffer holding pending stores before they reach cache; the source of x86-TSO's one permitted reordering (store→load). (Ch. 30)
- **TLB** — Translation Lookaside Buffer: caches virtual→physical page translations; a TLB miss triggers a page-table walk. Huge pages reduce TLB pressure. (Ch. 15)
- **Top-down analysis** — a PMU methodology attributing each cycle to front-end-bound / back-end-bound / bad-speculation / retiring, to find the dominant bottleneck. (Ch. 2)
- **TSC / `rdtsc` / `rdtscp`** — Time Stamp Counter: the per-core cycle counter read by `rdtsc` (`rdtscp` additionally waits for prior instructions to complete and returns the core ID); the *invariant* TSC ticks at a constant rate regardless of frequency scaling, which makes it the cheapest hot-path clock — once calibrated against a reference. (Ch. 17; Appendix A.4 for `CNTVCT_EL0`)

## F.3 Memory & OS terms

- **Arena / pool / slab allocator** — allocation strategies that replace `malloc` on the hot path: an *arena* hands out bump-pointer slices of one large block and frees them together; a *pool* recycles fixed-size objects through a free list; a *slab* is a pool per size class. Zero-allocation steady state is built from these. (Ch. 24)
- **cgroup** — Linux control groups: partition CPU/memory/I-O among process groups; used to confine housekeeping (and everything non-hot) off the isolated cores. (Ch. 45, Appendix C)
- **C-states / P-states** — processor *idle* states (C0 active, C1 … C6 progressively deeper sleep with µs-to-100-µs exit latency on the first event after idle) and *performance* states (frequency/voltage steps, turbo); both are variance sources a quiet box pins down — cap at C1 or `idle=poll`, fixed frequency, `performance` governor. (Ch. 6; Appendix C.1–C.3, E.1)
- **Context switch** — the kernel swapping the thread running on a core: a *direct* cost (save/restore state, run the scheduler) of ~µs and an *indirect* one — the incoming thread runs with cold caches, TLB and branch predictors; a single involuntary switch blows a tick-to-trade budget, hence pinning and never blocking on the hot path. (Ch. 41; Appendix E)
- **Futex** — Fast Userspace muTEX: the Linux primitive under mutexes/condvars; uncontended lock/unlock stays in userspace (fast), contention falls into a syscall to sleep/wake. (Ch. 32)
- **Huge pages** — larger page sizes (2 MiB, 1 GiB) that cover more memory per TLB entry, cutting TLB misses (Ch. 15); *explicit* (reserved) vs *transparent* (THP, kernel-managed, can add jitter). (Ch. 15, Appendix C)
- **IPI (inter-processor interrupt) / cross-call** — one CPU interrupting another: reschedule (`RES`), function call (`CAL` — `smp_call_function`, used by perf, MSR reads, `text_poke`, RCU expedited grace periods, cpufreq), TLB shootdown (`TLB`), IRQ work (`IWI`). On an isolated core each is a few µs stolen from whatever message was in flight; the `/proc/interrupts` rows are the fingerprint, the Ch. 46 catalog names the sender and the knob. (Ch. 42, 46, 80)
- **IRQ affinity / `irqbalance`** — steering each device interrupt to chosen cores via `/proc/irq/N/smp_affinity` (and stopping the `irqbalance` daemon that would redistribute them) so NIC/NVMe/timer IRQs land on housekeeping cores, never the isolated hot core; managed IRQs reject the file and need `isolcpus=managed_irq`. (Ch. 42, 57; Appendix C.3)
- **`isolcpus`** — kernel cmdline parameter removing CPUs from the scheduler's general balancing so only explicitly-pinned threads run there; the foundation of a quiet core. (Ch. 45, Appendix C)
- **KPTI** — Kernel Page-Table Isolation: the Meltdown mitigation that gives the kernel its own page tables, so every syscall/interrupt switches `CR3` and flushes or re-tags the TLB — the main reason a null syscall costs ~150–400 ns with mitigations on vs ~50–100 ns off. (Ch. 6, 41; Appendix E)
- **`mlockall`** — locks a process's pages resident in RAM so they're never swapped or paged out, eliminating page-fault stalls on the hot path. (Ch. 26)
- **`nohz_full`** — kernel feature stopping the periodic scheduler-tick interrupt on a core running a single task (tickless), removing that recurring jitter source. (Ch. 45, Appendix C)
- **Noise catalog (kernel)** — the finite set of things that can still execute on an `isolcpus`/`nohz_full` core — per-CPU kthreads, unbound workqueues, RCU and expedited grace periods, IPIs, managed device vectors, NMI/PMI, the memory-management daemons, the clocksource watchdog, SMIs — each with a trace fingerprint and a knob. (Ch. 46 §46.2)
- **NUMA** — Non-Uniform Memory Access: multi-socket systems where each CPU has faster access to its *local* memory node than to a remote node; node-local placement matters. (Ch. 16)
- **osnoise / timerlat / hwlat (`rtla`)** — the kernel's noise-attribution tracers: **osnoise** spins on a CPU and accounts every gap in its execution to IRQ, softirq, NMI, thread or `HW` (firmware); **timerlat** measures a periodic timer's IRQ and thread latency separately; **hwlat** spins with interrupts off to expose SMI/hardware stalls. All three stop the trace on a threshold so the ring buffer holds the spike; `rtla` is their front end. (Ch. 46 §46.3; Ch. 6 §6.4.4)
- **Page fault** — a trap when a touched virtual page isn't mapped/resident; *minor* (map a zero or already-resident page) costs ~0.3–1 µs for 4 KiB, *major* (from disk) far more — both forbidden on the hot path. (Ch. 23, 26)
- **PSI (pressure stall information)** — `/proc/pressure/{cpu,memory,io}`: the fraction of time tasks stalled waiting for a resource; near zero on the housekeeping side of a tuned box, rising when the consumers of the hot threads' rings are oversubscribed. (Ch. 80)
- **`rcu_nocbs`** — kernel cmdline offloading RCU callback processing off specified cores, keeping grace-period work off the isolated hot cores. (Ch. 45, Appendix C)
- **Shared memory (`mmap(MAP_SHARED)` / `shm_open` / `memfd_create`)** — a memory segment mapped into several processes' address spaces: the zero-copy data plane between feed-handler, strategy and gateway processes (single-writer rings, seqlock snapshots), with the pointers-across-address-spaces trap. (Ch. 26, 51, 78)
- **SMI (System Management Interrupt)** — a firmware interrupt that drops the core into System Management Mode: the OS is suspended, sees no interrupt and logs nothing; tens to hundreds of µs later it resumes. Invisible to `/proc/interrupts`; counted by `MSR_SMI_COUNT` (0x34, read from a housekeeping CPU) and by `osnoise`'s `HW` column; disabled only in the BIOS. (Ch. 6 §6.4.4, 46, 79; Appendix C.1)
- **Swappiness / swap** — the kernel's willingness to page memory to disk; on a trading box, disabled entirely (a swapped hot page is a multi-ms fault). (Ch. 23, Appendix C)
- **THP** — Transparent Huge Pages: kernel-managed automatic huge pages; convenient, but fault-time 2 MiB allocation (with the direct compaction the `defrag` setting may allow) and the background `khugepaged` collapse of 4 KiB pages are jitter sources — often disabled in favor of explicit huge pages. (Ch. 15, Appendix C)
- **Tick dependency** — the reason a `nohz_full` CPU's periodic tick cannot stop, named by the `timer:tick_stop` tracepoint: a second runnable task (`SCHED`), an attached perf event (`PERF_EVENTS`), a POSIX interval timer, an unstable clocksource, an expedited RCU grace period. (Ch. 45 §45.5, 46 §46.4.3)
- **TLB shootdown** — the IPI that flushes another core's TLB when a page mapping in a shared address space changes (`munmap`, `mprotect`, `madvise(DONTNEED)`, THP split, compaction, KSM); the most common self-inflicted intrusion on an isolated core, caused by the hot process's own housekeeping threads. (Ch. 26, 46 §46.4.3)

## F.4 Concurrency terms

- **ABA problem** — a lock-free hazard where a value reads as A, changes to B and back to A, fooling a CAS into thinking nothing changed; addressed with tagged pointers / hazard pointers / epochs. (Ch. 34, 36)
- **Acquire / Release** — memory-ordering constraints: an *acquire* load sees all writes that happened-before the matching *release* store; the one-way barriers that make lock-free publication correct (cheap on x86-TSO, real instructions `LDAR`/`STLR` on ARM). (Ch. 30, Appendix A.1)
- **CAS** — Compare-And-Swap (`compare_exchange_weak`/`_strong`): the atomic read-modify-write — "if the location still holds *expected*, store *desired*" — that every lock-free algorithm is built from; the CAS *loop* retries until it wins, and ABA is its characteristic hazard. (Ch. 34, 30)
- **Disruptor** — a high-performance ring-buffer + sequence-barrier pattern (LMAX) for single-producer/multi-consumer pipelines with batching and mechanical sympathy. (Ch. 37)
- **Epoch-based reclamation (EBR)** — a safe-memory-reclamation scheme where readers enter an epoch and memory is freed only after all readers have advanced past it; cheap reads, but reclamation is *not* bounded — one stalled reader pins the epoch for everyone (contrast hazard pointers, which bound memory by construction). (Ch. 36)
- **False sharing** — two unrelated variables on the *same cache line* written by different cores, causing the line to ping-pong (HITM) as if they were shared; fixed by padding to `hardware_destructive_interference_size`. (Ch. 33)
- **Hazard pointer** — a safe-memory-reclamation scheme where readers publish the pointers they're using so a deleter knows what's unsafe to free yet. (Ch. 36)
- **Lock convoy** — under sustained contention threads pile up behind a lock and proceed in lockstep, each wake-up handing the lock to the next sleeper; throughput collapses and latency spikes. Cure: shorter critical sections, sharding, or no lock at all. (Ch. 32)
- **Lock-free / wait-free (progress guarantees)** — *lock-free*: at least one thread always completes in a bounded number of steps, so no thread's stall can block the system; *wait-free*: *every* thread completes in bounded steps — the latency ideal, since it bounds the per-operation tail; *obstruction-free* is weaker than both. (Ch. 34)
- **MCS / CLH queue locks** — fair spinlocks in which each waiter spins on its *own* cache line (a per-thread queue node) and the unlocker hands off to its successor — eliminating the cache-line bounce of a plain spinlock under high contention. (Ch. 32)
- **Memory fence / happens-before** — a *fence* (`atomic_thread_fence`, `mfence`/`DMB`) orders memory operations around it without being tied to one variable; *happens-before* is the C++ memory model's relation — established by a release store synchronizing with the acquire load that reads it — that makes one thread's prior writes visible to another. Correctness on ARM depends on getting it right, not on luck. (Ch. 30; Appendix A.1)
- **Memory model / `memory_order`** — the rules (C++11+) governing how atomic operations on different threads become visible; `relaxed`/`acquire`/`release`/`acq_rel`/`seq_cst`. (Ch. 30)
- **RCU** — Read-Copy-Update: a synchronization scheme giving readers a lock-free fast path; writers publish a new version and reclaim the old after a grace period when no reader can hold it. (Ch. 36, 77)
- **Ring buffer** — a fixed-size circular array indexed by monotonically increasing producer/consumer sequence numbers (masked by a power-of-two size): the zero-allocation, cache-friendly backbone of SPSC/MPMC queues, the Disruptor, shared-memory IPC, NIC descriptor rings and the async logger. (Ch. 34, 37, 51, 73)
- **Seqlock** — sequence lock: a single-writer/multi-reader scheme where the writer bumps a counter around writes and readers retry if the counter changed mid-read; tear-free publication of small snapshots. (Ch. 35, 77)
- **Shared-nothing** — partitioning work so that each thread (or process) owns its data outright and there is no shared mutable state; coordination by message passing/rings — the partitioning that actually scales (the scalability curve stays linear), and the book's default architecture. (Ch. 31, 78)
- **Single-writer principle** — assigning exactly one writer per data structure / memory segment so publication is a simple release store with no inter-writer coordination. (Ch. 35, 78)
- **SMR (Safe Memory Reclamation)** — the family of schemes (RCU, hazard pointers, EBR) that defer freeing a node unlinked from a lock-free structure until no reader can still reference it. (Ch. 36)
- **Spinlock / TTAS / backoff** — a lock acquired by spinning on an atomic; **TTAS** (test-and-test-and-set) spins on a plain *read* until the lock looks free before attempting the atomic, and **exponential backoff** (with `pause`) spaces out retries — both to stop waiters hammering the holder's cache line. Safe only on isolated cores where the holder cannot be preempted — otherwise you are spinning across the scheduler. (Ch. 32)
- **SPSC / MPMC** — Single/Multi Producer, Single/Multi Consumer: the contention classes of lock-free queues/rings; SPSC is the cheapest (no inter-producer/consumer CAS). (Ch. 34)
- **TSO (Total Store Order)** — x86's relatively strong memory model (only store→load reordering); contrast ARM's weaker model where more reorderings are allowed and orderings cost real instructions. (Ch. 30, Appendix A.1)
- **USL / Amdahl's law** — Amdahl: the serial fraction caps speed-up no matter how many cores you add; the Universal Scalability Law (Gunther) adds a *coherence* term, so throughput actually *falls* past some thread count — the model behind the measured scalability curve. (Ch. 31)

## F.5 C++/toolchain terms

- **BOLT** — Binary Optimization and Layout Tool: a post-link optimizer that re-lays-out the final binary using a `perf` profile for I-cache/I-TLB locality. (Ch. 22, Appendix D)
- **Borrow checker / `unsafe`** — Rust's compile-time ownership and borrowing analysis, which rejects use-after-free, data races and aliased `&mut` at zero runtime cost; `unsafe` blocks switch specific checks off for raw pointers, FFI and hardware access, marking where the C++-style freedoms live. (Appendix B.1)
- **Coroutine / HALO** — C++20 stackless coroutines (suspendable functions); HALO (Heap-Allocation eLision Optimization) elides the coroutine frame allocation when the compiler can prove the lifetime. (Ch. 38)
- **CRTP** — Curiously Recurring Template Pattern: static (compile-time) polymorphism via `template<class D> Base`, avoiding the virtual-call indirection of runtime dispatch. (Ch. 19, 14)
- **`constexpr` / `consteval`** — compile-time evaluation: `constexpr` *may* run at compile time, `consteval` *must*; moves work off the runtime hot path. (Ch. 18)
- **Devirtualization / vtable** — a *vtable* is the per-class table of function pointers a `virtual` call indirects through (an indirect branch, BTB-predicted); *devirtualization* is the compiler replacing that indirect call with a direct (inlinable) one when it can prove the dynamic type — via `final`, LTO whole-program analysis, or PGO speculation. (Ch. 14, 22)
- **EBO / `[[no_unique_address]]`** — Empty-Base Optimization: an empty base class occupies no storage in the derived object; C++20's `[[no_unique_address]]` extends that to empty *members* (allocators, policies), so zero-size policy objects don't pad a hot struct. (Ch. 9, 19)
- **`std::execution` (senders / receivers / scheduler)** — the C++26 standard async model: lazily-composed **senders** describe async work, **receivers** are the typed continuation (value/error/stopped), and a **scheduler** places work on an execution context — a zero-cost structured-async abstraction. (Ch. 39)
- **`std::expected`** — a C++23 vocabulary type holding either a value or an error, for hot-path error handling without exceptions or sentinel codes. (Ch. 20)
- **FFI** — Foreign Function Interface: calling across language runtimes, in practice through a C ABI (`extern "C"`); the boundary belongs between latency tiers, never on a per-message hot path. (Appendix B.5)
- **`std::flat_map` / `std::flat_set`** — C++23 sorted-vector adaptors: ordered containers stored contiguously (keys and values in separate vectors), so lookups are cache-friendly binary searches rather than pointer chases through tree nodes; the right shape for small-to-medium ordered hot data such as price levels. (Ch. 25; Appendix H)
- **FTZ / DAZ** — Flush-To-Zero / Denormals-Are-Zero: FPU modes treating subnormal floats as zero to avoid the 10-100× denormal-operation penalty; set explicitly on FP hot paths. (Ch. 27, Appendix D)
- **GC pause** — a stop of the application thread(s) by a managed runtime's garbage collector, from tens of µs (ZGC/Shenandoah/C4) to milliseconds (a full GC): a tail event (Ch. 1) the runtime, not you, schedules — what keeps GC'd languages off the order path. (Appendix B.3–B.4)
- **Heisenbug / observer effect** — a bug that changes or vanishes when you observe it, because a `printf`/breakpoint/heavy probe perturbs timing; debug via low-overhead capture and offline replay instead. (Ch. 5)
- **Intrusive container** — a list/tree whose link fields live *inside* the element (`boost::intrusive`-style) instead of in separately allocated nodes: no per-insert allocation, one fewer pointer chase, and an element can be unlinked in O(1) from a pointer to it — the order-book idiom for orders within a price level. (Ch. 25)
- **JIT vs AOT** — just-in-time compilation (JVM, .NET) optimizes hot code at runtime after interpreting it, so the first executions are slow and a *deoptimization* can re-introduce a stall mid-session — the Ch. 47 cold-start problem built into the runtime; ahead-of-time compilation (C++, Rust, C, Zig, GraalVM native-image) is fully optimized before the first tick. (Appendix B.3–B.4, Ch. 47)
- **LTO** — Link-Time Optimization: optimizing across translation-unit boundaries at link (cross-module inlining, devirtualization); ThinLTO is the scalable variant. (Ch. 22, Appendix D)
- **`noexcept`** — a specifier promising a function won't throw; enables optimizations (no unwinding setup) and is required for some move-aware container fast paths. (Ch. 20)
- **Open addressing / Robin Hood hashing** — hash tables that store entries in the bucket array itself (probing on collision) instead of chaining through heap nodes; *Robin Hood* probing steals slots from entries with shorter probe distances to keep the maximum probe length — the tail — short. The cache-friendly replacement for `std::unordered_map`. (Ch. 25)
- **PGO** — Profile-Guided Optimization: a build that uses a runtime profile (branch/hotness data) to drive inlining, branch layout, and code placement with data instead of heuristics. (Ch. 22, Appendix D)
- **PMR** — Polymorphic Memory Resources (`std::pmr`): standard-library support for custom allocators (arenas/pools) plugged into containers. (Ch. 24)
- **RAII** — Resource Acquisition Is Initialization: tying resource lifetime to object scope (constructor acquires, destructor releases); deterministic cleanup, no GC. (Ch. 39 — RAII applied to async scopes; Ch. 24 for destructor discipline in arenas/pools; Appendix B)
- **`restrict` / `__restrict__`** — a pointer qualifier promising no aliasing through it, letting the optimizer reorder/vectorize loads and stores it otherwise couldn't. (Ch. 21)
- **`rr`** — a record-and-replay debugger: records an execution once, then replays it deterministically forwards *and backwards* (reverse execution) — the reproduce-then-debug tool. (Ch. 5)
- **RTTI** — Run-Time Type Information: the per-type data behind `dynamic_cast` and `typeid`; `-fno-rtti` removes it when static polymorphism (CRTP, `std::variant`) replaces runtime type queries. (Ch. 20; Appendix D.4)
- **Sanitizer (ASan / UBSan / TSan / MSan)** — compiler instrumentation catching, respectively, memory errors, undefined behavior, data races, and uninitialized reads; a test/CI/fuzzing tool (ASan ~2×, TSan ~5–15× — Ch. 40), never production. (Ch. 40, 76)
- **SBO (small-buffer optimization)** — storing a small payload inline inside the handle object (as `std::string`, `std::function` and `std::any` implementations do) instead of heap-allocating it; the hot-path version is a *fixed-capacity* inline buffer that never allocates. (Ch. 14, 25)
- **SoA / AoS** — Structure-of-Arrays vs Array-of-Structures: data layouts; SoA groups each field contiguously for cache/SIMD efficiency, AoS groups whole records. (Ch. 8, 29)
- **`std::span` / `std::mdspan`** — non-owning views over contiguous (1-D) / multi-dimensional data carrying their bounds; bounds-aware zero-copy access. (Ch. 76, 4; availability in Appendix H)
- **Split debug info** — keeping `-g` debug info in a separate file (`-gsplit-dwarf` / `objcopy`) so a stripped production binary's cores and profiles stay symbolizable offline. (Ch. 5, Appendix D)
- **Structured concurrency** — async work with scope-bound lifetime and propagating cancellation (stop tokens) — RAII applied to concurrency; no dangling continuations. (Ch. 39)
- **Zero-cost abstraction** — an abstraction (templates, RAII, iterators) that compiles to the same code you'd write by hand — no runtime penalty for the abstraction. (Ch. 19, 20)

## F.6 Networking, I/O & Transport terms

- **Aeron / eRPC / Homa** — purpose-built low-latency transports: **Aeron** (reliable UDP/multicast/IPC messaging, LMAX lineage), **eRPC** (µs RPC on commodity NICs), **Homa** (receiver-driven, connectionless, HOL-free). (Ch. 67)
- **aRFS** — accelerated Receive Flow Steering: the kernel programs the NIC to steer each flow to the core its socket is consumed on, automatically. (Ch. 59)
- **Busy-poll / poll-mode** — spinning on a NIC/socket for new data instead of taking interrupts — removing interrupt and wakeup latency at the cost of a burned core. (Ch. 48, 64)
- **Cut-through / store-and-forward / layer-1** — switch types: **cut-through** starts forwarding after the header (frame-size-independent, sub-µs); **store-and-forward** receives the whole frame first (serialization delay, grows with size); **layer-1** is a physical cross-connect/replicator (~4–5 ns port-to-port, no parsing). (Ch. 62)
- **DCQCN** — Data Center Quantized Congestion Notification: the RoCEv2 congestion-control algorithm that reacts to ECN marks by cutting rate, keeping buffers below the PFC threshold. (Ch. 66)
- **DDIO (Data Direct I/O)** — Intel feature that DMAs inbound NIC data straight into L3 (skipping DRAM) — a receive-latency win that can also pollute the hot working set. (Ch. 58)
- **devmem TCP** — kernel receive path that splits headers to the host and DMAs the payload directly into device memory (GPU/FPGA), never touching host DRAM. (Ch. 50)
- **DPDK** — Data Plane Development Kit: the vendor-neutral kernel-bypass framework (poll-mode drivers, hugepage mempools, userspace stacks). (Ch. 64)
- **Drop reason (`skb:kfree_skb`)** — the enumerated cause (`SKB_DROP_REASON_*`: `SOCKET_RCVBUFF`, `IP_RPFILTER`, `NO_SOCKET`, `TCP_OFO_DROP` …) and the kernel function recorded by the tracepoint every discarded packet passes through; read with `dropwatch`, `perf trace`/`record` or `bpftrace` — the forensic layer beneath the counters. Hardware drops (ring, port FIFO) never reach it. (Ch. 81 §81.4.2)
- **eBPF** — extended Berkeley Packet Filter: a verified in-kernel VM for low-overhead production tracing (bpftrace) and programmable packet paths (XDP). (Ch. 63)
- **ECN** — Explicit Congestion Notification: a switch *marks* (rather than drops) a congested packet, signaling the sender to slow — the proactive layer of lossless Ethernet. (Ch. 66)
- **EDT (Earliest Departure Time)** — the Linux egress model where each packet carries a departure time; pacing and precise scheduling are both EDT settings. (Ch. 61)
- **ef_vi / Onload** — Solarflare/AMD kernel-bypass APIs: **ef_vi** is the raw, lowest-latency layer-2 API; **Onload** transparently accelerates existing socket apps (userspace TCP). **ExaNIC / `libexanic`** (Cisco/Exablaze) is the other proprietary bypass API — direct access to the NIC's RX/TX rings with hardware timestamps and an on-card FPGA option. (Ch. 57, 64)
- **`epoll` / readiness vs completion** — the Linux readiness-notification multiplexer: register fds, `epoll_wait` reports which are *ready*, then you issue the `read`/`write` yourself (two syscalls per event; level- vs edge-triggered); io_uring's *completion* model instead hands back finished operations. (Ch. 48, 49)
- **Flow steering (Flow Director / `rte_flow`)** — directing a *specific* flow to a *specific* NIC queue/core by exact-match rules, overriding RSS's random hash. (Ch. 59)
- **GRO / GSO / TSO / LRO** — receive/transmit segmentation offloads that batch segments for CPU efficiency at a latency cost; GRO/LRO are turned off on the hot RX path. (Ch. 53)
- **HOL blocking (head-of-line)** — TCP's ordered byte stream stalls *every* later message behind a single lost segment until it's retransmitted; message transports avoid it. (Ch. 67)
- **IGMP snooping / querier** — a switch learns which ports joined a multicast group by snooping IGMP (IPv4) / MLD (IPv6) membership reports and forwards group traffic only there; the state is a soft lease refreshed by hosts' answers to periodic **General Queries** from a **querier**. No querier → entries age out after the Group Membership Interval (~260 s with RFC defaults) → the feed goes dark on every host with the host's own membership table unchanged. (Ch. 56 §56.2, 62)
- **Incast** — many senders hitting one receiver simultaneously (a fan-in / scatter-gather), congesting the fabric and collapsing TCP throughput. (Ch. 66, 67)
- **InfiniBand** — the lossless, credit-flow-controlled fabric RDMA was designed for (native verbs, no PFC/ECN tuning needed); **RoCEv2** carries the same verbs over Ethernet. (Ch. 65)
- **io_uring / SQE / CQE / SQPOLL** — the modern Linux async-I/O interface: the application writes **SQEs** (submission-queue entries) into a shared ring and reads **CQEs** (completion-queue entries) back; **SQPOLL** has a kernel thread poll the submission ring (no `io_uring_enter` syscall), **IOPOLL** polls the device for completions; plus registered/provided buffers, multishot, and zero-copy send/receive. (Ch. 49)
- **IPC (inter-process communication)** — moving data between processes: pipes, sockets and message queues (a syscall per message) vs **shared-memory** rings and seqlock snapshots (no syscall on the hot path) — the latter is the data plane of a process-per-role trading system. Not Instructions Per Cycle (F.2). (Ch. 51; Ch. 26, 78)
- **Kernel bypass** — mapping the NIC's rings into userspace and polling them, sending/receiving packets with no kernel, no syscalls, no copies, no interrupts. (Ch. 64)
- **LAUNCHTIME / `SO_TXTIME`** — NIC-hardware / socket mechanism to transmit a packet at an exact scheduled nanosecond, via the ETF qdisc. (Ch. 61)
- **`MSG_ZEROCOPY` / `TCP_ZEROCOPY_RECEIVE`** — kernel zero-copy send/receive: the NIC DMAs to/from *your* buffer, at the cost of asynchronous buffer-lifetime management (send) or page alignment (receive). (Ch. 50)
- **Nagle / delayed ACK** — TCP throughput heuristics whose interaction can inject a ~40 ms stall into small request-response flows; disabled with `TCP_NODELAY` / `TCP_QUICKACK`. (Ch. 53)
- **NAPI** — the kernel's interrupt-mitigation receive model: the first packet's interrupt disables further RX interrupts and switches the driver to polling the ring until it is drained (then re-arms); `busy_poll` extends the same polling into the socket layer. (Ch. 48 — native I/O; Ch. 57)
- **NAK** — Negative Acknowledgment: a receiver requesting retransmission of specific missing messages (reliable multicast); a *NAK storm* is many subscribers requesting at once. (Ch. 55)
- **One-sided RDMA** — a remote READ/WRITE that completes on the NIC without involving the remote CPU (vs two-sided send/recv). (Ch. 65)
- **Pacing** — spreading sends over time to a smooth rate (`SO_MAX_PACING_RATE` / FQ) to avoid self-inflicted microbursts. (Ch. 61)
- **Pause frame / PFC** — Ethernet flow control: a NIC or switch whose buffers fill sends PAUSE (or per-priority PFC) and the sender stops, moving the drop into the sender's buffers; `tx_pause` on a market-data host means *this host is too slow*, `rx_pause` means the switch is congested toward it. Off on market-data NICs by convention; on, by design and monitored, on lossless RDMA fabrics. (Ch. 66, 81 §81.2.3)
- **PFC (Priority Flow Control)** — link-level pause frames (802.1Qbb) that make Ethernet lossless per traffic class; the backstop under RoCEv2, with head-of-line-blocking and deadlock hazards. (Ch. 66)
- **PGM** — Pragmatic General Multicast: a NAK-based reliable-multicast protocol; a model for feed recovery. (Ch. 55)
- **PMD (poll-mode driver)** — a userspace NIC driver that polls the RX ring instead of taking interrupts — the mechanism of kernel bypass. (Ch. 64)
- **PTP / PHC** — Precision Time Protocol (IEEE-1588): sub-µs clock synchronization across hosts via NIC hardware timestamps; the **PHC** is the PTP Hardware Clock. The basis of true wire-to-wire measurement. (Ch. 60)
- **`recvmmsg` / `sendmmsg`** — receive/send many datagrams in one syscall, amortizing the per-message syscall cost for high-rate UDP. (Ch. 59)
- **RoCEv2** — RDMA over Converged Ethernet: RDMA carried on an Ethernet fabric, requiring PFC/ECN/DCQCN to be lossless. (Ch. 65, 66)
- **RSS (Receive Side Scaling)** — the NIC hashes a flow tuple to spread packets across RX queues/cores — good for bulk load, bad for the hot path (random placement, feed splitting). (Ch. 57, 59)
- **`rx_missed` vs `rx_fifo` / port discards** — the two NIC-side drop addresses: **ring full** (`rx_missed_errors`, `rx_out_of_buffer`, `rx_nodesc_drop_cnt` — the host did not poll fast enough) vs **port FIFO** (`rx_fifo_errors`, `rx_discards_phy`, `port.rx_dropped` — the DMA/PCIe path could not drain). Same symptom, different owner; every driver names them differently. (Ch. 81 §81.2.2)
- **Serialization delay** — the time to clock a frame onto the wire (frame_bits ÷ link_rate); why cut-through and high, uniform link speeds matter. (Ch. 62)
- **`SO_REUSEPORT`** — multiple sockets bound to one port with kernel/BPF load-balancing of flows across threads/cores. (Ch. 59)
- **SSM (source-specific multicast)** — an (S,G) join (`MCAST_JOIN_SOURCE_GROUP`, IGMPv3 source filter, groups in 232/8) that forwards only one sender's traffic for the group: no rendezvous point, no rogue senders, faster first-on-link joins. (Ch. 56 §56.4.1)
- **`time_squeeze`** — `/proc/net/softnet_stat`'s count of NAPI poll rounds that exhausted `netdev_budget`/`netdev_budget_usecs` with packets still in the ring and deferred the rest to `ksoftirqd`; latency, not loss — and the precursor of ring-full drops on the market-data path. (Ch. 48, 81 §81.2.3)
- **TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking)** — IEEE 802.1 standards for scheduled, deterministic Ethernet (the time-aware shaper, 802.1Qbv). (Ch. 61)
- **USDT** — User Statically-Defined Tracing: `DTRACE_PROBE`-style probe points compiled into your binary (a `nop` until a tracer attaches) that `bpftrace`/eBPF can hook with near-zero cost when disabled — the production-safe way to expose hot-path events. (Ch. 63)
- **Verbs / QP / CQ / MR** — the RDMA API: **Queue Pairs** (send/recv work queues), **Completion Queues**, and **Memory Regions** (registered/pinned memory the NIC may DMA). (Ch. 65)
- **White Rabbit / grandmaster** — sub-nanosecond clock distribution extending PTP; the *grandmaster* is the root clock, disciplined by GPS/PPS. (Ch. 60)
- **XDP (eXpress Data Path) / AF_XDP** — in-driver eBPF packet processing (drop/redirect/filter at the earliest hook); **AF_XDP** is a zero-copy socket for userspace fast paths — a middle ground between the kernel stack and full bypass. (Ch. 63)
- **Zero-copy** — the NIC DMAs to/from the *final* buffer, eliminating the intermediate kernel copy (`MSG_ZEROCOPY`, io_uring zero-copy, devmem TCP). (Ch. 50)

## F.7 Hardware Acceleration & Devices

- **BAR (Base Address Register)** — PCIe config that maps a device's registers/memory into the host address space for MMIO. (Ch. 68)
- **CUDA / warp / occupancy** — NVIDIA's GPU programming model; a **warp** is 32 threads executed in lockstep; **occupancy** is how fully the GPU's warp slots are used. (Ch. 69)
- **DMA (Direct Memory Access)** — a device reading/writing host memory without the CPU; the bulk-transfer mechanism behind NICs and accelerators. (Ch. 68)
- **DOCA / P4** — DPU programming models: **DOCA** (NVIDIA BlueField SDK, C/C++ on ARM cores), **P4** (a declarative packet-processing language for match/action pipelines). (Ch. 72)
- **DPU / SmartNIC** — a NIC with a programmable processor on it (ARM cores and/or FPGA) between wire and host, for offloading packet processing, RDMA, security, and even feed handling. (Ch. 72)
- **FPGA / HLS / RTL** — Field-Programmable Gate Array (configurable hardware, deterministic-nanosecond pipelines); described in **RTL** (Verilog/VHDL at register-transfer level — cycle-exact registers and the logic between them, which synthesis lowers to a gate-level netlist; lowest latency) or **HLS** (C/C++ compiled to hardware, easier). (Ch. 71)
- **GPUDirect / P2P DMA** — peer-to-peer DMA directly between devices (NIC↔GPU/FPGA) bypassing host memory. (Ch. 68, 69)
- **IOMMU** — I/O Memory Management Unit: translates and protects device DMA addresses (a TLB for devices). (Ch. 68)
- **MMIO** — Memory-Mapped I/O: accessing device registers as memory; a non-posted *read* is a full PCIe round-trip. (Ch. 68)
- **MPI** — Message Passing Interface: the standard for distributed compute across cluster nodes (point-to-point + collectives), used for backtesting/risk. (Ch. 70)
- **MSI-X** — Message-Signaled Interrupts: a device signals a completion by writing memory (vs polling). (Ch. 68)
- **PCIe** — Peripheral Component Interconnect Express: the bus every NIC/GPU/FPGA sits behind; its round-trip latency is the floor under every offload decision. (Ch. 68)
- **Posted vs non-posted** — a *posted* write is fire-and-forget (~tens of ns); a *non-posted* read stalls for a full round-trip (~hundreds of ns) — why device-register reads are costly. (Ch. 68)
- **VFIO** — the kernel framework that hands a PCIe device to userspace safely (IOMMU-protected DMA, MMIO mapping, interrupts) — how DPDK and other bypass stacks bind a NIC without a kernel driver. (Ch. 64, 68)
- **Write-combining / doorbell** — *write-combining* memory collects adjacent stores into one bus transaction before posting it to the device; a *doorbell* is the single MMIO write that tells a NIC/accelerator new descriptors are ready — the posted (cheap) direction of PCIe. (Ch. 10, 68)

## F.8 Security, Isolation & Operations

- **ASLR / PIE** — Address Space Layout Randomization / Position-Independent Executable: load-time memory-safety hardening, ~free on the hot path. (Ch. 76)
- **CAT (Cache Allocation Technology) / RDT** — Intel Resource Director Technology partitions the shared L3 (and, via MBA, memory bandwidth) so a noisy neighbor can't evict the hot working set even when the core is pinned. (Ch. 44)
- **CET / CFI** — Control-flow Enforcement Technology (a hardware shadow stack) / Control-Flow Integrity: hardening against control-flow hijacking; CFI's cost tracks indirect-call density. (Ch. 76)
- **CLOS (Class of Service)** — an RDT group assigned cache ways (CAT) and/or a bandwidth throttle (MBA); cores or tasks are assigned to a CLOS. (Ch. 44)
- **CMT / MBM** — Cache Monitoring Technology / Memory Bandwidth Monitoring: per-group L3 occupancy and memory-bandwidth counters — the "see who's in the cache" half of RDT. (Ch. 44)
- **Crash-only design** — the only way to stop a process is to crash it, and the only way to start is to recover — so the recovery path is always tested and fast. (Ch. 78)
- **Deterministic state machine** — a pure, side-effect-free decision core (time and randomness passed *in* as inputs) that replays a captured input stream bit-for-bit; the basis of debugging, backtesting, and compliance. (Ch. 78)
- **Drift (configuration)** — a tuned box silently untuning itself: package/kernel updates re-applying profiles, shared configuration roles flipping sysctls, agents attaching perf events, firmware updates resetting BIOS policy, the environment (thermal throttling, aging DIMMs). Prevented by configuration as code (Appendix C.6), detected by the host-health sampler and the pre-open attestation. (Ch. 80 §80.2.3, Appendix M)
- **Fingerprint (host)** — the recorded baseline a box is signed off with: kernel/cmdline/microcode/BIOS/NIC-firmware provenance, isolation masks and IRQ map, sysctls, `ethtool`/`resctrl` settings, steady-state interrupt-row rates, SMI count — plus the micro-suite's latency numbers; generated from the release's declaration and compared against the live box at T-60. (Ch. 46 §46.4.3, 79 §80.4.4, Appendix M.2/M.6)
- **`_FORTIFY_SOURCE`** — compile-time (and light runtime) bounds checks where the buffer size is known; usually free, kept on. (Ch. 76)
- **High-water mark** — the maximum a gauge reached since an epoch (queue depth, worst stage latency, pool occupancy): written by the hot thread with a compare and a conditional store, reset by the scraper bumping an epoch the thread acknowledges — never by writing the thread's line. (Ch. 75 §75.2.1)
- **Hot reload** — changing config / strategy parameters / code in a *running* process without dropping a tick, via atomic pointer swap or seqlock/RCU-published snapshots and safe reclamation. (Ch. 77)
- **Invariant alert ("any nonzero delta")** — the alert class for quantities whose correct value on a tuned, warmed-up box is exactly zero (involuntary switches, migrations, minor faults, IPI rows, SMIs, post-A/B gaps mid-session, `operator new` calls): no window, no percentile, no threshold — the first delta is the ticket, with the fingerprint-table diagnosis attached. (Ch. 75 §75.4.4, 79 §80.2.1)
- **MBA (Memory Bandwidth Allocation)** — RDT throttling of a class's memory bandwidth, to stop a streaming neighbor (capture, logging) from saturating the memory controller. (Ch. 44)
- **Readiness gate / state machine** — the per-process word `cold → warming → syncing → reconciled → armed → trading → disarmed` published in the telemetry segment and read by the order path with one predicted branch, advanced only by the timed pre-open gates (T-60 host attestation, T-30 clock/feed/network, T-5 session/positions/warm-up/kill-switch) and dropped to `disarmed` by any failing invariant. (Appendix M; Ch. 78 §78.4.4)
- **`resctrl`** — the Linux pseudo-filesystem (`/sys/fs/resctrl`) interface to Intel RDT (CAT / MBA / CMT / MBM). (Ch. 44)
- **Scraper (telemetry)** — the off-core process that maps the telemetry segment read-only, snapshots cumulative counters and histogram buckets at 1–10 Hz, diffs for interval histograms, merges by adding buckets, computes percentiles last and exports them — and reads the host and NIC counters on the same timeline. Its interference on the hot core (an RFO per touched line per scrape) is measured, not assumed. (Ch. 75 §75.3, §75.4.3)
- **`seccomp`** — Linux syscall filtering; installed at the steady-state boundary it costs ~nothing (the hot path makes no syscalls) while containing a compromise. (Ch. 76)
- **Spectre / Meltdown** — speculative-execution vulnerabilities; the mitigations (retpoline, IBRS, `mitigations=`) carry a latency cost you may deliberately reduce on an isolated, single-tenant box. (Ch. 6, 76)
- **Telemetry segment** — the versioned shared-memory layout in which each hot thread owns cache lines of monotonic counters, a seqlock-guarded gauge set, high-water marks and per-stage log-linear histograms, written with plain stores and never read by the thread's own peers; the substrate for metrics, heartbeats, progress sequences and the readiness word. (Ch. 75 §75.4.1)
- **UB (Undefined Behavior)** — behavior the standard leaves undefined; in low-latency C++ it is simultaneously a correctness bug, a security vulnerability, and a source of nondeterminism (and the optimizer may delete your checks around it). (Ch. 21, 76)
- **WAL (Write-Ahead Log)** — append-only journaling: persist the event durably *before* acting on it; the capture-journal and replay model. (Ch. 79)
