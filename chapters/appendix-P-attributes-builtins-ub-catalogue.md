# Appendix P — Attributes, Builtins & the UB Catalogue

> **Consolidates:** the per-function optimizer hints scattered across Ch. 14 (`[[likely]]`, hot/cold, inlining attributes), Ch. 15 (`__builtin_expect`, `std::unreachable`), Ch. 16 (devirtualisation and `final`), Ch. 24 (`[[clang::trivial_abi]]`, `[[assume]]`, contracts), Ch. 26 (`restrict`, aliasing, lifetime), Ch. 35 (integer overflow), Ch. 37 (`target_clones`, alignment), Ch. 51 and Ch. 97 (UBSan and UB as a vulnerability class) — and the undefined-behaviour rules those chapters lean on, read the way a performance programmer must read them: as licences the optimizer holds, each with a flag that revokes it and a measured price for doing so. **Appendix D** is the per-*build* flag reference; this appendix is its per-*function* companion, and D.4/D.7 point here rather than repeat it.

---

## P.1 How to read this appendix

Two tables and a method. **P.2** lists the attributes and builtins that change what the optimizer may assume about *one function or one expression*: for each, what it tells the compiler, the GCC and Clang spellings (standard `[[…]]` first where one exists), the chapter that measures it and the asm fingerprint that proves it fired — because an attribute the optimizer ignored is worse than none (you believe it worked). **P.3** is the undefined-behaviour catalogue for performance programmers: each rule the standard leaves undefined is an assumption the optimizer is *entitled* to make, each assumption buys a specific transformation, each has a flag that buys it back at a measured cost, and each has a sanctioned replacement that keeps the speed without the UB. **P.4** is the verification method. The measurements are VM-measured on the 4-vCPU Xeon VM of Appendix L.2 (GCC 13.3 / Clang 18.1, `-O2 -std=c++20 -march=x86-64-v3`, Linux 6.18) on four small kernels that recur in the chapters — a scaled-price reduction with a 32-bit index (Ch. 35), a float `axpy` whose bound is an `int*` (Ch. 26), a top-of-book read that dereferences before it null-checks, and a 38-byte decode through a 64-byte local buffer (Ch. 68) — and the ratios, not the nanoseconds, are what transfer to Appendix L.3's reference machine.

## P.2 Attribute and builtin reference: what each tells the optimizer, and how to verify it

| `[[likely]]` / `[[unlikely]]` (C++20) | Static branch weights: lay the likely path out fall-through, move the unlikely block to the end or to `.text.unlikely`. **No effect on the hardware predictor**, only on layout and on which side gets the `jmp`. Overridden by a PGO profile (`-fprofile-use` wins). | `[[likely]]` on the statement; `__builtin_expect(x, 1)` on the condition (GCC 9 / Clang 12 for the attribute — Appendix H.3) | Ch. 14 §14.4, Ch. 15 | The hot path is fall-through (`jne` to a distant label for the cold case); `objdump -d` shows the cold block after the `ret` or in `.text.unlikely` |

| `[[gnu::pure]]` / `[[gnu::const]]` | `pure`: no side effects, result depends only on arguments and global memory → calls may be CSE'd and hoisted out of loops; `const`: result depends only on arguments → may be hoisted even across stores. **A lie is UB**: a `const` function that reads memory will be hoisted above the store that changes it. | `__attribute__((pure))`/`((const))`; both compilers infer them for visible bodies (`-fipa-pure-const`), the attribute is for *opaque* callees (another TU without LTO, a library) | Ch. 16 §16.4.1, Ch. 27 §27.4.2 | Repeated calls with the same arguments collapse to one in the asm; a call inside a loop with loop-invariant arguments moves above the loop |

| `__builtin_unreachable()` / `std::unreachable()` (C++23) | Control never reaches here: delete the path, drop the `default:` range check of a dense `switch`, fold the branch that leads here. **Reaching it is UB** (not a trap). | `__builtin_unreachable()` (both); `std::unreachable()` libstdc++ 12 / libc++ 15 | Ch. 15 §15.4.2, Ch. 24 §24.4.4 | A `switch` on a closed enum has no bounds compare before the jump table; in non-release builds map it to `__builtin_trap()` and confirm the `ud2` |
| `std::assume_aligned<N>(p)` (C++20) / `__builtin_assume_aligned(p, N)` | `p` is `N`-byte aligned: use aligned vector loads, drop the peel/prologue loop that aligns the pointer. **Misaligned `p` is UB** (a `vmovaps` fault on the lucky days). | `std::assume_aligned` libstdc++ 10 / libc++ 15; `__builtin_assume_aligned` both | Ch. 26 §26.6, Ch. 37 §37.4.1 | `vmovaps` instead of `vmovups`, and no alignment-peeling prologue before the vector loop |
| `__restrict__` / `restrict` (C) | This pointer's object is accessed through no other pointer in scope: reorder and vectorise across stores through it. **Overlap is UB.** | `__restrict__`/`__restrict` (both); standard `restrict` is C only | Ch. 26 §26.4.1, §26.5 | Packed ops with no runtime overlap check (`cmp`/`ja` of the two pointers) before the vector loop |
| `[[gnu::may_alias]]` | Objects of this type may alias anything (opt *out* of strict aliasing for one type). Advisory; no fault. | Both; `_mm_prefetch` from `<xmmintrin.h>` | Ch. 12 §12.4 | `prefetcht0`/`prefetchnta` in the asm at the distance you intended; measure — Ch. 12 shows when it hurts |
| `[[gnu::target("avx512f,…")]]` / `[[gnu::target_clones("avx2","avx512f","default")]]` / `__builtin_cpu_supports("avx2")` / `ifunc` | Compile *this function* for an ISA the rest of the TU is not built for; `target_clones` emits one body per listed ISA plus an **ifunc resolver** that picks at load time (one indirect call through the GOT per call, resolved once). `__builtin_cpu_supports` is the hand-rolled dispatch. | `__attribute__((target(…)))`, `((target_clones(…)))`, `__attribute__((ifunc("resolver")))` — GCC 6+, Clang 14+ for `target_clones` | Ch. 27 §27.5, Ch. 37 §37.4.3 | `nm` shows `fn.avx2`, `fn.avx512f`, `fn.resolver`; `readelf -r` shows an `R_X86_64_IRELATIVE` relocation; the call site is `call fn@PLT` (one indirect hop — Ch. 16) |

| `[[nodiscard]]` / `[[nodiscard("why")]]` | A dropped return value is a warning (make it `-Werror`): the enforcement `std::expected` lacks. No codegen effect. | Standard (C++17; message form C++20) | Ch. 24 §24.4.2 | A compile error when the result is dropped under `-Werror=unused-result` |
| `[[noreturn]]` | The function never returns: the caller omits the code after the call and may omit its own epilogue (smaller hot paths around `abort`/`terminate`/the kill path). | Standard; `__attribute__((noreturn))` | Ch. 14 §14.4.2, Ch. 100 §100.4 | No instructions follow the `call` in the caller; the callee sits in `.text.unlikely` if also `cold` |
| `[[gnu::init_priority(N)]]` / `__attribute__((constructor(N)))` | Order dynamic initialisation across TUs (101–65535, lower first). | GCC and Clang (ELF) | Ch. 24 §24.4.6 | `nm` shows `_GLOBAL__sub_I.00101_…`; `.init_array` order in `readelf -S` plus `objdump -s -j .init_array` |
| `[[gnu::visibility("hidden")]]` / `-fvisibility=hidden` | This symbol is not exported from the shared object: calls to it are direct (no PLT), it may be inlined and devirtualised freely. | Both; the flag form is Appendix D.4 | Ch. 16 §16.2.3, Ch. 27 §27.4 | `nm -D` no longer lists it; call sites are `call fn` not `call fn@PLT` |
| `__builtin_add_overflow(a, b, &r)` and friends; `std::add_sat` (C++26) | Overflow-*checked* arithmetic in one instruction pair (`add` + `jo`/`seto`) — the sanctioned replacement for "I know this won't overflow"; `add_sat`/`sub_sat` saturate instead. | `__builtin_{add,sub,mul}_overflow` both; `std::add_sat` libstdc++ 14 / libc++ 18 (`__cpp_lib_saturation_arithmetic`) | Ch. 35 §35.4, Ch. 97 §97.4.3, Ch. 100 | `add` followed by `jo`/`seto`; no call |
| `[[gnu::section(".text.hot.decode")]]` / `[[gnu::used]]` / `[[gnu::retain]]` | Place a function in a named section (linker-script layout, Ch. 14); keep a symbol the optimizer/linker would drop (`used`: compiler; `retain`: linker `--gc-sections`). | Both (`retain` GCC 11 / Clang 13, needs binutils 2.36) | Ch. 14 §14.4.3, Ch. 27 §27.4.3 | `readelf -S`/`nm` show the section and the retained symbol |
| Contracts (C++26, P2900): `pre(…)`, `post(…)`, `contract_assert(…)` | A checked precondition/postcondition/assertion with an **evaluation semantic** chosen at build time: *ignore* (no code — and, unlike `[[assume]]`, **no licence** to the optimizer), *observe* (call the handler, continue), *enforce* (handler, then terminate), *quick_enforce* (trap). | GCC 15 experimental: `-fcontracts -fcontract-evaluation-semantic=…`; Clang 18/19: not implemented (Appendix H.3) | Ch. 24 §24.4.4 | Under *ignore* the function body is identical to the uncontracted one; under *quick_enforce* a `cmp`+`ud2` pair per contract |

Two principles tie the table together. **Hints that *inform* are safe; hints that *promise* are UB when false.** `likely`, `hot`/`cold`, `prefetch`, `noinline`, `section`, contracts-under-*observe* are informative: a wrong one costs performance. `assume`, `unreachable`, `assume_aligned`, `restrict`, `pure`/`const`, `trivial_abi`-across-TUs are promises: a wrong one is a miscompile that passes every test until the input that breaks the promise. Put every promise under a test-build macro that turns it into a check (`#ifdef NDEBUG [[assume(x)]] #else if (!(x)) __builtin_trap() #endif`), and let UBSan (P.3) trap it in CI. And **a hint is a claim about the asm** — P.4 says how to check each claim; an unverified attribute is a comment.

## P.3 The UB catalogue for performance programmers

Each row: the rule → the transformation it licenses → the flag that revokes the licence and what revoking it cost on the P.1 kernels → the sanctioned way to keep the speed. The four kernels (`-O2 -march=x86-64-v3`, VM-measured; baseline first):

```
                                          GCC 13.3                       Clang 18.1
scaled_sum: s += (px[i]*qty[i]) >> 8, int i      0.372 ns/elem                  0.295 ns/elem
axpy with an int* bound (strict aliasing)        0.262                          0.073
top(): deref before null check                   0.514 ns/call                  0.361 ns/call
decode through a 64-byte local buffer            1.283                          0.514
mids: s += px[2*i] + px[2*i+1], int i, -O3       0.289                          0.227
```

| Undefined behaviour | What the optimizer may assume / do | Buy it back: flag, and measured cost | Sanctioned replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Signed integer overflow** (`int`/`int64_t` `+ - * <<`, `INT_MIN / -1`) | Arithmetic never wraps: widen a 32-bit induction variable to 64 bits once instead of re-extending it every iteration, treat `i*2+1` as monotone (contiguous vector loads instead of gathers), fold `a + 1 > a` to `true`, simplify `(x*2)/2` to `x`. | `-fwrapv` (define wrap) / `-ftrapv` (trap): **`mids` 0.289 → 0.718 ns/elem on GCC (2.5×)** — the strided index can no longer be proven non-wrapping, so GCC emits `vpgatherqq` instead of contiguous `vmovdqu`; `scaled_sum` unchanged (0.372 → 0.354; the bound was already sign-extended once); Clang unchanged on both (it derives the range from the loop bound). `-ftrapv` adds a `jo` per operation. | 64-bit (`size_t`/`int64_t`) induction variables so there is nothing to widen; `__builtin_mul_overflow` where overflow is a *real* condition (Ch. 100's notional check); unsigned arithmetic only where wrap is the intended semantics (sequence numbers, Ch. 70); `std::add_sat` where saturation is. |
| **Strict aliasing** (access through an incompatible type) | A store through `float*` cannot change an `int` object: keep the loop bound and other `int` loads in registers across the stores; reorder the stores. | `-fno-strict-aliasing`: **`axpy` 0.073 → 0.261 ns/elem on Clang (3.6×)** — the `*n` bound is reloaded every iteration and the loop no longer vectorises; GCC 13 was already reloading it here (0.262 → 0.260: GCC's alias oracle gave up earlier, the flag cost nothing *because the win was already lost*). | `std::bit_cast`/`memcpy` for punning, `std::start_lifetime_as` for overlays (Ch. 26 §26.4.2), `std::byte`/`char` only as the *view*; copy the bound into a local before the loop. |
| **Null-pointer dereference** | Dereferencing `p` proves `p != nullptr`: a later `if (p == nullptr)` is dead and deleted; `&p->field` is non-null. | `-fno-delete-null-pointer-checks`: **`top()` 0.361 → 0.514 ns/call on Clang (+0.15 ns)** — the `test rdi, rdi; je` is restored before the loads; GCC 13 unchanged here (0.514 both: it had kept the check). Also the flag that makes `this == nullptr` checks survive. | Check *before* dereferencing (the check then costs the same one `test`+predicted `jcc` and the optimizer keeps it), or use a reference. Never rely on a check after the deref. |
| **Reading an uninitialised object** | The value is indeterminate: the compiler may pick any value, delete the read, or fold a branch on it either way. | `-ftrivial-auto-var-init=zero` (or `=pattern`): zero every automatic variable at entry. **`decode` unchanged (1.283 / 0.514)** — the 64-byte buffer was eliminated entirely by both compilers before the init mattered. The cost appears where buffers *survive*: one `rep stosb`/vector-zero per large local per call; measure per hot function (Ch. 97 §97.4.2 for the hardening argument). | Initialise what you read; `std::array<…> buf{}` where the zeroing is cheap and the buffer is large; `[[gnu::uninitialized]]`/`__attribute__((uninitialized))` (Clang) to exempt a proven-written buffer under the flag. |
| **Out-of-bounds access** (array, `std::span`, `vector`) | The index is in range: no check is emitted; `a[i]` after `a[i+1]` proves `i+1` valid. | `-D_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS` / `-D_LIBCPP_HARDENING_MODE=…FAST` (library indices only): **+3–8 % on a decode loop with three checked indexings** (Ch. 24 §24.4.4); `-fsanitize=bounds` on raw arrays: see the UBSan rows. | One length check per message against the wire layout (Ch. 23 §23.4.3, Ch. 68 §68.6), then unchecked indexing inside; `std::span` with the hardened mode in test builds; `[[assume(i < N)]]` only after the check that proved it. |
| **Data race** (unsynchronised access to a non-atomic object) | A non-atomic load may be hoisted out of a loop (the classic "`while (!flag)` spins forever"), split, or merged; a store may be invented. | `-fsanitize=thread` (test only; 5–15× — Ch. 51). No production flag defines the race. | `std::atomic_ref` for plain shm/wire fields (Ch. 39 §39.4.3), `std::atomic` with the weakest correct order (Ch. 39 §39.4.1), seqlocks (Ch. 44). |
| **Object lifetime** (using storage before an object exists or after it ended; `reinterpret_cast<Msg*>(buf)` on bytes nothing created a `Msg` in) | No object there: accesses may be reordered against the `memcpy` that filled the bytes, or deleted. | None (the sanitizers do not model it; `-fno-strict-aliasing` does *not* fix lifetime). | `memcpy` (creates the object, P0593), `std::start_lifetime_as`/the `memmove`+`launder` polyfill (Ch. 26 §26.4.2), `std::launder` after storage reuse (Ch. 31). |
| **Misaligned access** (`*reinterpret_cast<uint64_t*>(p + 30)`) | `p` is aligned for the type: use aligned vector instructions, assume the access is atomic where the ABI says aligned ones are. | `-fsanitize=alignment` (UBSan, test); none in production — x86 tolerates the load, which is why it "works". | `memcpy` into a local, a `packed` overlay with `start_lifetime_as`, `std::assume_aligned` only where the ring guarantees it (Ch. 68). |
| **Shift by ≥ width or by a negative count; `1 << 31` into `int`** | The shift count is in range: emit `shl` with no mask (x86 masks the count anyway — the UB is what the *optimizer* does with a proven-in-range count). | `-fsanitize=shift` (a `cmp`+`ja` per variable shift; part of the subset row below). | `std::uint64_t` operands; `std::rotl`/`std::rotr` (C++20) for rotates; mask the count where it comes from data (Ch. 36). |
| **Division by zero / `INT_MIN / -1`** | Divisor non-zero: no check before `idiv` (the hardware traps anyway — `#DE`; the UB is that the compiler may move the division). | `-fsanitize=integer-divide-by-zero`. | Validate divisors from the wire once; prefer multiplication by a reciprocal or a shift (Ch. 35 §35.4; `div` is 15–90 cycles — Ch. 4 §4.2.1). |
| **Infinite loop without side effects** (C++ forward-progress guarantee) | The loop terminates: code after it is reachable, the loop may be deleted. C++26 (P2809) makes *trivial* infinite loops (`while (true) {}` with a constant condition) defined. | None; `-fno-finite-loops` on Clang restores the C semantics. | Spin loops always read an atomic or call `_mm_pause()` (a side effect), which every spin in Ch. 41 does. |
| **Violating `restrict`, a false `[[assume]]`, reaching `__builtin_unreachable()`, a false `assume_aligned`** | Exactly what you promised (P.2). | `-fsanitize=undefined` checks `__builtin_unreachable` (`-fsanitize=unreachable`) and, on Clang, `__builtin_assume`'s expression in test builds; `restrict` and `assume_aligned` are not checked. | A test-build macro that turns every promise into a trap (P.2's closing note). |

**The sanitizer rows, measured.** UBSan is the one flag that revokes *all* the licences at once; its production form is the trapping, minimal one, and its cost depends on which checks you enable:

```
                              GCC 13.3                                 Clang 18.1
                              scaled  axpy   top    decode            scaled  axpy   top    decode
```

(The "baseline" row here is a second harness run and differs slightly from the table above; compare within a row.) The full set is unshippable on a hot path — 2.5× on the reduction, 20× on the decode under Clang (its `-fsanitize=object-size`/`vptr`/`float-cast-overflow` checks are the expensive ones). The **hot-path subset** — overflow, bounds, shift, null — trapping, costs 1.0–1.5× here and is what Ch. 97 §97.4.8 recommends for a *test* build that replays captured market data (Ch. 104) before every release; a minimal-runtime build (`-fsanitize-minimal-runtime`, Clang) with that subset is the only form worth considering in production, and only on the decode path, and only after measuring it on *your* loop. The per-rule flags above (`-fwrapv`, `-fno-strict-aliasing`, `-fno-delete-null-pointer-checks`, `-ftrivial-auto-var-init`) are the surgical alternative: each revokes one licence, each costs what the table says, and each is a *meaning-class* flag that must be uniform across every TU that shares a type (Ch. 28 §28.4.5).

## P.4 Verifying a hint, and finding a UB-licensed transformation you did not want

- **Read the asm of the function the attribute is on, at the shipping `-O2`** (Ch. 4): the P.2 column says what to look for. The fast checks: `objdump -d --no-show-raw-insn hot.o | grep -c 'call'` (inlining attributes), `readelf -S hot.o | grep text.unlikely` and `nm --print-size -S | sort` (hot/cold placement), `nm hot.o | grep -E 'resolver|\.avx'` (`target_clones`), `grep -c vmovaps` vs `vmovups` (`assume_aligned`), `grep 'jmp.*<f>'` at the end of a caller (`musttail`). Appendix I.12 collects them.
- **Ask the compiler what it did and did not do.** `clang++ -Rpass=inline -Rpass-missed=inline -Rpass=loop-vectorize -Rpass-missed=loop-vectorize -Rpass-analysis=loop-vectorize`; `g++ -fopt-info-vec-all -fopt-info-inline-optimized`. A `[[likely]]` or `assume` that changed nothing produces no remark — the asm is the only witness.
- **Find the transformation the UB licensed.** When a null check "disappeared", a loop "stopped terminating" or a bounds check was "optimised away", compile the TU with the matching buy-back flag (`-fno-delete-null-pointer-checks`, `-fno-strict-aliasing`, `-fwrapv`) and diff the asm: the instructions that reappear are the ones the licence removed, and the source that made the licence applicable is the bug. Then fix the source, not the flag (P.3's last column), and drop the flag.
- **Trap every promise in CI.** The test build defines the P.2 macros so that `[[assume]]`/`unreachable`/`assume_aligned` become `__builtin_trap()`, runs with `-fsanitize=undefined -fsanitize-trap=all` over the replay (Ch. 104), and the production build keeps the promises. A promise that trapped in replay is a promise the hot path was about to break at 09:30.
- **Keep the licence flags uniform.** `-fwrapv`, `-fno-strict-aliasing`, `-ftrivial-auto-var-init`, the sanitizers and `-D_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS` change the meaning of inline code and are *meaning-class* flags under Ch. 28 §28.4.5's policy; `readelf -p .GCC.command.line` on every object is how you prove they are.
