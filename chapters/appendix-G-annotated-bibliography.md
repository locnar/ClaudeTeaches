# Appendix G — Annotated Bibliography

> The consolidated reading list behind the whole book — the per-chapter *References* and *Additional Reading* gathered, deduplicated, and annotated. Each entry says **what it's good for** and **which chapters/Parts draw on it**, so you can go deeper on any topic from one place. Organized by kind — foundational papers (G.1), vendor & reference manuals (G.2), books (G.3), influential talks & blogs (G.4), and tooling documentation (G.5) — and within each, roughly by where the book first leans on it.

This is a *map*, not a syllabus: you don't read it front to back. When a chapter sent you here, find the source it cited and use the annotation to decide whether it's the deep-dive you want. The handful of works marked **★** are the ones that repay reading in full for anyone serious about low-latency systems.

---

## G.1 Foundational papers

- **★ Ulrich Drepper, *What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory* (2007).** The definitive long-form treatment of caches, memory hierarchy, NUMA, and access patterns — dated in specifics, timeless in fundamentals. The intellectual backbone of **Part II** (Ch. 7–10) and the NUMA chapter (Ch. 16). If you read one thing from this list, read this.
- **★ Jeff Dean, "Numbers Everyone Should Know" / Peter Norvig's latency table.** The orders-of-magnitude latency intuition the whole book trades on; refreshed and trading-annotated in **Appendix E**. Underlies Ch. 1's "reason about the ratios."
- **★ Jeff Dean & Luiz André Barroso, "The Tail at Scale" (CACM 56(2), 2013).** The canonical argument that tail latency, not the mean, governs latency-critical systems, and why fan-out multiplies rare slowness — the distribution-first framing of **Ch. 1** and the p99/p99.9 discipline of Ch. 3 and 98.
- **★ The LMAX *Disruptor* technical paper (Thompson, Farley, Barker, Gee, Stewart, 2011).** The ring-buffer + sequence-barrier mechanical-sympathy design that anchors **Ch. 40**, and whose ideas recur in the lock-free queues (Ch. 37), logging (Ch. 84), the shared-memory IPC rings (Ch. 57), and the Aeron lineage (Ch. 73). Essential HFT-adjacent reading.
- **Fred B. Schneider, "Implementing Fault-Tolerant Services Using the State Machine Approach: A Tutorial" (ACM Computing Surveys 22(4), 1990)** — state-machine replication: deterministic replicas fed one total order hold identical state. The theory under **Ch. 94**'s sequencer and replicas and Ch. 89's deterministic core; read with Lamport's "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events" (CACM 1978) for where the total order comes from, and Défago, Schiper & Urbán's "Total Order Broadcast and Multicast Algorithms: Taxonomy and Survey" (ACM Computing Surveys 36(4), 2004) for the fixed-sequencer class Ch. 94 implements.
- **George Varghese & Tony Lauck, "Hashed and Hierarchical Timing Wheels" (SOSP 1987; IEEE/ACM ToN 1997).** The timer data structure every busy-poll engine and every OS kernel uses; the complexity analysis behind **Ch. 47**'s wheel-vs-heap measurements. Pair with Gleixner & Niehaus, "Hrtimers and Beyond" (OLS 2006) and Gleixner's 2016 "timers: Refactor the timer wheel" series for the kernel's two timer subsystems and why the wheel stopped cascading.
- **Pat Helland, "Idempotence Is Not a Medical Condition" (ACM Queue 10(4), 2012)** and **Gray & Reuter, *Transaction Processing*, Ch. 9–10 (1993)** — idempotent messaging and write-ahead logging: the reasoning behind **Ch. 91**'s journal-before-send, slot+generation ClOrdIDs and the unknown-state matrix.
- **Matt Welsh, David Culler & Eric Brewer, "SEDA" (SOSP 2001)** and **Leonard Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems*, Vol. I (1975)** — staged pipelines with admission control, and the arrival-vs-service arithmetic that **Ch. 41–42** measure (hop cost, queueing under a burst, conflation's bounded staleness).
- **Paul McKenney, *Is Parallel Programming Hard, And, If So, What Can You Do About It?* and his RCU papers.** The authoritative source on RCU and safe memory reclamation — **Ch. 39**, with ties to seqlocks (Ch. 38) and hot reload (Ch. 88). Deep, rigorous, free online.
- **Leslie Lamport, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System" (CACM 1978) and TLA+.** The happens-before idea beneath every memory-model argument in the book, and the TLA+ / model-checking resources that **Ch. 45** (§45.4.3) cites for checking lock-free designs — the one place a chapter leans on him directly.
- **Maranget, Sarkar & Sewell, *A Tutorial Introduction to the ARM and POWER Relaxed Memory Models*.** Why weakly-ordered hardware reorders, and how to reason about it — the reference behind **Appendix A.1** (and a sharp complement to Ch. 33).
- **Candea & Fox, "Crash-Only Software" (2003).** The crash-only design philosophy of **Ch. 89** — recovery as the only path, fast restart, fault containment.
- **Michael & Scott, "Simple, Fast, and Practical Non-Blocking and Blocking Concurrent Queue Algorithms" (PODC 1996).** The MS-queue — the reference unbounded lock-free queue and the baseline every bounded ring is measured against — **Ch. 37** (§37.4.1), with its ABA/reclamation consequences in Ch. 39 and the Disruptor's contrast in Ch. 40.
- **Hans Boehm & Sarita Adve, "Foundations of the C++ Concurrency Memory Model" (PLDI 2008)** and **Boehm, "Can Seqlocks Get Along with Programming Language Memory Models?"** The rationale and semantics behind C++11 atomics and data-race-freedom — **Ch. 33**; the seqlock paper is the definitive analysis of why a naive seqlock is a data race and how to write one the memory model accepts — **Ch. 38** (§38.5).
- **Maged Michael, "Hazard Pointers: Safe Memory Reclamation for Lock-Free Objects" (IEEE TPDS 15(6), 2004)** and **Keir Fraser, *Practical Lock-Freedom* (PhD thesis, University of Cambridge, 2004).** The two original safe-memory-reclamation schemes — per-pointer protection with bounded memory, and epoch-based reclamation with cheap readers but unbounded deferral — compared and measured in **Ch. 39** (§39.4.2–§39.4.3).
- **Thomas Anderson, "The Performance of Spin Lock Alternatives for Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" (IEEE TPDS 1990)** and **Mellor-Crummey & Scott, "Algorithms for Scalable Synchronization on Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" (ACM TOCS 1991).** TAS vs TTAS vs backoff vs queue locks, and the MCS lock that gives each waiter its own cache line — the measurements and techniques of **Ch. 35** (§35.3–§35.4).
- **David Goldberg, "What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic" (ACM Computing Surveys, 1991).** IEEE 754 representability, rounding and accumulated error — the floating-point half of **Ch. 30** (§30.2) and the case for scaled-integer prices.
- **Yang, Park & Ousterhout, "NanoLog: A Nanosecond Scale Logging System" (USENIX ATC 2018).** Compile-time format extraction, binary records and an offline decoder — the deferred/binary-logging design that **Ch. 84** builds (§84.2, §84.4.2).
- **The low-latency transport and congestion papers** — Zhu et al., "Congestion Control for Large-Scale RDMA Deployments" (**DCQCN**, SIGCOMM 2015); Kalia, Kaminsky & Andersen, "Datacenter RPCs can be General and Fast" (**eRPC**, NSDI 2019); Montazeri, Li, Alizadeh & Ousterhout, "Homa: A Receiver-Driven Low-Latency Transport Protocol Using Network Priorities" (SIGCOMM 2018). Why RoCEv2 needs ECN-driven rate control — **Ch. 72** (§72.2) — and what a µs-scale, head-of-line-free transport looks like beyond TCP — **Ch. 73** (§73.4.2–§73.4.3).
- **Gil Tene, "How NOT to Measure Latency" (and the coordinated-omission material).** Why naive latency measurement lies and how HdrHistogram fixes it — the measurement rigor of **Ch. 3** and the tail-latency framing of Ch. 1.
- **Daniel Bristot de Oliveira et al., "Demystifying the Real-Time Linux Scheduling Latency" (ECRTS 2020), and the osnoise/timerlat write-ups (Linux Plumbers 2021; LWN).** Latency as a sum of attributable intervals — the model the `osnoise` and `timerlat` tracers implement and that **Ch. 52** uses to name the source of the last spike on an isolated core.

## G.2 Vendor & reference manuals

- **SEC Rule 15c3-5 (17 CFR 240.15c3-5, 2010) and its 2014 FAQ; Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589 (MiFID II RTS 6), Arts. 12, 15–20; FIA, *Best Practices for Automated Trading Risk Controls*.** The check inventory and the kill-functionality requirement that **Ch. 90** implements; the SEC's Knight Capital order (Release 34-70694, 2013) is the case study Ch. 91 opens with.

- **★ Intel® 64 and IA-32 Architectures Software Developer's Manual (SDM)** and the **Intel® 64 and IA-32 Optimization Reference Manual.** The authoritative x86-64 reference — instruction semantics, the memory model, PMU events, and microarchitectural optimization guidance. Cited across **Parts II-VI** (Ch. 7, 11–13, 31–33) and Appendix E. The optimization manual especially repays study.
- **★ Agner Fog's optimization manuals** (*Optimizing software in C++*, *The microarchitecture of Intel/AMD CPUs*, and the **instruction tables**). The practical bible of instruction latencies/throughputs and per-microarchitecture behavior — the source for "how many cycles does this cost" throughout **Ch. 11–14, 31–32**, Appendix D-E. The instruction tables are a daily-reference artifact.
- **AMD64 Architecture Programmer's Manual** and AMD optimization guides. The AMD counterpart to the Intel SDM — relevant wherever the book says "Intel/AMD" (Ch. 6, 7, 11), and for Zen-specific tuning (Appendix D).
- **Arm Architecture Reference Manual (ARMv8-A/ARMv9-A)** and the **Arm Neoverse / Ampere optimization guides.** The memory model, NEON/SVE, LSE atomics, `CNTVCT_EL0`, and `-mcpu` tuning behind **Appendix A**.
- **The Linux kernel documentation** — especially `Documentation/admin-guide/` (kernel parameters, `nohz_full`/`isolcpus`/RCU), the scheduler/RT docs, and the networking stack docs. The basis of **Part VII** (Ch. 46–53) and **Appendix C**.
- **The `perf` / `perf_events` documentation and the PMU event references.** How to drive the hardware counters and top-down analysis of **Ch. 2** (and the measurement methods of Appendix E).
- **The exchange protocol specifications** — Nasdaq **ITCH/OUCH**, the **FIX**, **SBE**, and **FAST** specs. The wire formats decoded in **Ch. 60**, glossaried in Appendix F.1 and given a field-level quick reference in Appendix J.5.
- **PCIe base specification (and vendor PCIe tuning notes).** The host-device boundary, posted/non-posted transactions, and round-trip cost of **Ch. 74** (and the offload Part XI).
- **Intel *Intrinsics Guide*.** The searchable per-intrinsic reference (semantics, CPUID flags, latency/throughput) behind the SSE/AVX/AVX-512 work of **Ch. 32**, the BMI/BMI2 tricks of Ch. 31 and the prefetch/streaming-store intrinsics of Ch. 10.
- **IEEE 1588 (Precision Time Protocol), including the 2019 revision (High Accuracy / White Rabbit profile).** The protocol behind cross-host timestamps and true wire-to-wire tick-to-trade — **Ch. 66** (§66.2, §66.4.3), with the NIC PHC of Ch. 63; tooling in `linuxptp` (G.5).
- **The RDMA verbs documentation** — `libibverbs`/`librdmacm` (rdma-core) and the `perftest` suite (`ib_*_lat`), the *RDMA Aware Networks Programming User Manual* (NVIDIA/Mellanox), the InfiniBand Architecture Specification and the RoCE v1/v2 standards. QPs, CQs, MRs and work requests, one-sided vs two-sided — **Ch. 71** (§71.2, §71.4), with UCX as the layer MPI runs on (Ch. 76).
- **NVIDIA *CUDA C++ Programming Guide* and *CUDA C++ Best Practices Guide*.** The execution/memory model, warps/occupancy, coalescing, streams, pinned/unified memory — **Ch. 75** (§75.2, §75.4) — and the PCIe transfer cost it shares with Ch. 74.
- **The MPI Standard (MPI Forum) and the Open MPI / MPICH / Intel MPI and UCX documentation.** Communicators, point-to-point, collectives, non-blocking and one-sided RMA, and the RDMA transport underneath — **Ch. 76** (§76.2, §76.4).
- **The FPGA toolchain documentation** — AMD/Xilinx Vivado and Vitis HLS, Intel/Altera Quartus. RTL vs HLS development, synthesis, place-and-route, partial reconfiguration — **Ch. 77** (§77.2, §77.4.2).
- **NVIDIA BlueField / DOCA SDK documentation and the P4 language specification (p4.org).** DPU architecture and the two programming models — C/C++ on the ARM cores vs declarative match/action pipelines — of **Ch. 78** (§78.2, §78.4.1).
- **Intel Resource Director Technology (RDT) documentation and the kernel's `resctrl` interface** (`Documentation/arch/x86/resctrl.rst`), with the `intel-cmt-cat` / `pqos` toolkit and the AMD64 QoS Extensions. CAT/MBA/CMT/MBM, CLOS and capacity masks — **Ch. 50** (§50.2, §50.4).
- **The market-access and timestamping rules** — SEC Rule 15c3-5 ("market access": pre-trade risk controls and the kill-switch) and MiFID II RTS 25 (clock synchronization traceable to UTC), with the Reg NMS / CAT record-keeping requirements. The compliance drivers behind the kill-switch of **Ch. 89** (§89.4.4), the PTP accuracy targets of Ch. 66 and the complete, timestamped capture of Ch. 93.
- **RFC 2236 / 3376 (IGMPv2/v3), RFC 4541 (IGMP/MLD snooping switches), RFC 3678 (multicast source-filter socket API), RFC 7761 (PIM-SM).** The membership protocol, the querier requirement and the SSM socket API behind every market-data join — **Ch. 62**.
- **Linux kernel documentation: `kernel-per-CPU-kthreads.rst`, `osnoise-tracer.rst`/`timerlat-tracer.rst`/`hwlat_detector.rst`, `tools/rtla/`, `ftrace.rst`; `snmp_counter.rst`, `statistics.rst`, `scaling.rst`, `dropreason-core.h`; `proc.rst`, `psi.rst`, `ras.rst`, `pcieaer-howto.rst`.** The noise catalog and tracers of **Ch. 52**, the network counter semantics of **Ch. 97**, and the host-health signals of **Ch. 96**.
- **Prometheus documentation (histograms, exposition format, `histogram_quantile`, native histograms) and the OpenTelemetry exponential-histogram specification.** Why the unit of export is bucket counts and never a percentile — **Ch. 86 §86.4.3**.
- **SEC Rule 15c3-5 (Market Access) and ESMA MiFID II RTS 6.** Pre-trade controls, kill functionality, real-time monitoring and the annual self-assessment that a readiness gate and kill-switch test satisfy — **Appendix M**, Appendix K.4.
- **Exchange timestamp and protocol references: CME *MDP 3.0* and *iLink 3* documentation, Deutsche Börse *T7 EOBI* and *ETI* manuals, Nasdaq *TotalView-ITCH 5.0*, *OUCH 5.0* and *MoldUDP64* specifications.** The field definitions (`SendingTime`, `TransactTime`, `TrdRegTS*`, ITCH timestamps) behind the you/wire/matching-engine attribution of **Ch. 85 §85.4.5** and the table in Appendix J.5; also the decoders of Ch. 60.
- **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574 (MiFID II RTS 25).** The clock-accuracy tolerances (100 µs / 1 µs granularity for HFT-class activity) that bound how far a venue's timestamp can be trusted — **Ch. 85 §85.4.5, Ch. 66**; the record-keeping horizon of Appendix N.7 comes from RTS 6 / RTS 25.
- **Linux man pages for the crash and signal path: `signal-safety(7)`, `sigaction(2)`, `sigaltstack(2)`, `core(5)`, `madvise(2)`, `prctl(2)`, `backtrace(3)`; `fs/coredump.c` and `arch/x86/kernel/signal.c`.** The async-signal-safe function list, the `core_pattern`/`coredump_filter` semantics (including the pipe-ignores-`RLIMIT_CORE` rule) and the sigframe path — **Ch. 92, Ch. 46 §46.4.3**.
- **Intel SDM, Vol. 1 Ch. 13 (XSAVE) and Vol. 3 Ch. 36 (Processor Trace).** Why a signal frame is full-size even with clean vector state (**Ch. 92 §92.3**), and the packet/ToPA/PTWRITE mechanics behind Intel PT snapshots (**Ch. 2 §2.4.4, Ch. 95 §95.4.3**).
- **`perf-record(1)`, `perf-intel-pt(1)`, `perf-script(1)` and `Documentation/trace/ftrace.rst`.** Overwrite/snapshot/`--switch-output`/`--control` modes, `-k CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, `trace_clock x86-tsc`, per-CPU rings — the always-on-frozen-on-trigger recipes of **Ch. 95 §95.4.3** and the uncore/IBS/RAPL/`msr` event syntax of **Ch. 2 §2.4.4**; `perf_event_open(2)`'s mmap page for `rdpmc` (**Ch. 98 §98.4.4**).


- **GCC manual, *Extended Asm*, *Constraints for asm Operands*, *x86 Operand Modifiers*; Clang *Inline Assembly* extensions.** The normative contract behind every `asm` statement in the book — operands, clobbers, `volatile`, `asm goto` — **Ch. 79**.
- **System V ABI, AMD64 Processor Supplement; Itanium C++ ABI; Drepper, *How to Write Shared Libraries*.** Registers, stack, struct passing and return, name mangling, the PLT/GOT and symbol visibility — the C waist of **Ch. 81** and the hand-written functions of **Ch. 79**.
- **JEP 442/454 (Foreign Function & Memory API), JEP 439 (Generational ZGC), JEP 318 (Epsilon), JEP 483 (AOT Class Loading & Linking); the HotSpot GC Tuning Guide; the .NET GC/tiered-compilation/NativeAOT documentation; the Go GC Guide; the OCaml manual's runtime and C-interface chapters; the Rust Reference and rustc codegen options; the Zig language reference.** The primary documentation behind **Ch. 80–83**'s measurements and Appendix B's tables.

## G.3 Books

- **★ Hennessy & Patterson, *Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach* (6th ed., 2017).** The foundational architecture text — pipelines, caches, ILP, memory systems, multiprocessors. The "why" beneath **Part II** (Ch. 7–17) and the quantitative mindset of the whole book.
- **★ Anthony Williams, *C++ Concurrency in Action* (2nd ed.).** The definitive practical guide to the C++ memory model, atomics, and lock-free programming — **Part VI** (Ch. 33–45). The book to own for the concurrency chapters.
- **★ Maurice Herlihy & Nir Shavit, *The Art of Multiprocessor Programming* (2nd ed., with Luchangco & Spear).** The theory behind lock-free programming — linearizability, progress guarantees (obstruction-/lock-/wait-free), CAS, ABA and the memory-reclamation problem — **Ch. 37** (§37.2) and Ch. 39.
- **Agner Fog's optimization series** (also in G.2, as books) — *Optimizing software in C++* reads as a coherent text on the abstraction-cost and codegen concerns of **Part III** (Ch. 18–24).
- **Vandevoorde, Josuttis & Gregor, *C++ Templates: The Complete Guide* (2nd ed.)** and **Andrei Alexandrescu, *Modern C++ Design*.** The template instantiation/cost model, CRTP, expression templates and policy-based design of **Ch. 18–19**, and the small-object (Loki) allocator behind the pools of Ch. 27 (§27.4.2).
- **Fedor Pikus, *The Art of Writing Efficient Programs* (Packt, 2021) and *Hands-On Design Patterns with C++* (2nd ed., Packt, 2023).** Modern, practical performance-oriented C++ — measurement, memory, concurrency and the design patterns beneath zero-cost abstractions. A general companion to Ch. 3, 26–28 and 33–36 rather than a chapter-cited source.
- **Scott Meyers, *Effective Modern C++*** and **the C++ Core Guidelines.** Idiomatic modern C++ underpinning the language-mechanics Parts (Ch. 18–23) and the safety discipline of Ch. 87.
- **Brendan Gregg, *Systems Performance* (2nd ed.)** and ***BPF Performance Tools*.** The methodology of production performance analysis — the USE method, flame graphs, and the eBPF tooling of **Ch. 69** and the production-monitoring stance of **Ch. 98**.
- **Robert Love, *Linux System Programming*** and **Michael Kerrisk, *The Linux Programming Interface*.** The syscall-level reference behind **Part VIII** (Ch. 54, 55, 57, 58) and the memory/process chapters (Ch. 26, 29, 89).
- **Daniel Lemire's work and *Hacker's Delight* (Warren).** Bit-manipulation and branch-free integer tricks — **Ch. 31** (and the SWAR/SIMD parsing of Ch. 60).
- **Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges: Market Microstructure for Practitioners*.** The standard market-structure text — matching, order types, maker/taker, the participants' roles — behind **Appendix J** (J.1–J.3) and the trading framing of Ch. 28, 60 and the case studies; the FIX Trading Community specifications (G.2) cover the wire side.
- **Beyer, Jones, Petoff, Murphy (eds.), *Site Reliability Engineering* (2016) and *The Site Reliability Workbook* (2018).** SLOs, error budgets and multi-window burn-rate alerting — adapted to session phases in **Ch. 86 §86.4.4**; the production-readiness-review idea behind **Appendix M**.
- **Beau Williamson, *Developing IP Multicast Networks* (Cisco Press).** PIM, RPF and rendezvous points — the routed-multicast background of the venue distribution tree in **Ch. 62 §62.2.3**.
- **Raj Jain, *The Art of Computer Systems Performance Analysis* (1991).** Ch. 2's catalogue of measurement mistakes — comparing unconverted or mis-aligned measurements chief among them — is the methodological footing of the one-timeline rule in **Ch. 95 §95.2.2**.


- **Mara Bos, *Rust Atomics and Locks* (O'Reilly, 2023).** The Rust memory model and its x86/ARM codegen — the companion to Ch. 33 for **Ch. 80** (§80.2.3, §80.4.3).
- **Nicholas Nethercote, *The Rust Performance Book*; Jon Gjengset, *Rust for Rustaceans*.** Bounds-check elision, allocation avoidance, `unsafe` abstraction and FFI engineering — **Ch. 80**, **Ch. 81**.
- **Konrad Kokosa, *Pro .NET Memory Management*; Minsky, Madhavapeddy & Hickey, *Real World OCaml* (memory representation, GC chapters).** The .NET and OCaml runtimes at the depth **Ch. 82** (§82.4.4, §82.4.6) can only summarise.

- **Dave Cheney, "cgo is not Go"; Austin Clements, non-cooperative goroutine preemption proposal.** Why a Go→C call costs 50 ns and how Go preempts a hot loop — **Ch. 81 §81.3**, **Ch. 82 §82.4.5**.
