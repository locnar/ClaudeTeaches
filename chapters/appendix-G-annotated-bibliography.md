# Appendix G — Annotated Bibliography

> The consolidated reading list behind the whole book — the per-chapter *References* and *Additional Reading* gathered, deduplicated, and annotated. Each entry says **what it's good for** and **which chapters/Parts draw on it**, so you can go deeper on any topic from one place. Organized by kind — foundational papers (G.1), vendor & reference manuals (G.2), books (G.3), influential talks & blogs (G.4), and tooling documentation (G.5) — and within each, roughly by where the book first leans on it.

This is a *map*, not a syllabus: you don't read it front to back. When a chapter sent you here, find the source it cited and use the annotation to decide whether it's the deep-dive you want. The handful of works marked **★** are the ones that repay reading in full for anyone serious about low-latency systems.

---

## G.1 Foundational papers

- **★ Ulrich Drepper, *What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory* (2007).** The definitive long-form treatment of caches, memory hierarchy, NUMA, and access patterns — dated in specifics, timeless in fundamentals. The intellectual backbone of **Part II** (Ch. 8–11) and the NUMA chapter (Ch. 17). If you read one thing from this list, read this.
- **★ Jeff Dean, "Numbers Everyone Should Know" / Peter Norvig's latency table.** The orders-of-magnitude latency intuition the whole book trades on; refreshed and trading-annotated in **Appendix E**. Underlies Ch. 1's "reason about the ratios."
- **★ Jeff Dean & Luiz André Barroso, "The Tail at Scale" (CACM 56(2), 2013).** The canonical argument that tail latency, not the mean, governs latency-critical systems, and why fan-out multiplies rare slowness — the distribution-first framing of **Ch. 1** and the p99/p99.9 discipline of Ch. 3 and 106.
- **★ The LMAX *Disruptor* technical paper (Thompson, Farley, Barker, Gee, Stewart, 2011).** The ring-buffer + sequence-barrier mechanical-sympathy design that anchors **Ch. 42**, and whose ideas recur in the lock-free queues (Ch. 39), logging (Ch. 86), the shared-memory IPC rings (Ch. 59), and the Aeron lineage (Ch. 75). Essential HFT-adjacent reading.
- **Fred B. Schneider, "Implementing Fault-Tolerant Services Using the State Machine Approach: A Tutorial" (ACM Computing Surveys 22(4), 1990)** — state-machine replication: deterministic replicas fed one total order hold identical state. The theory under **Ch. 98**'s sequencer and replicas and Ch. 91's deterministic core; read with Lamport's "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events" (CACM 1978) for where the total order comes from, and Défago, Schiper & Urbán's "Total Order Broadcast and Multicast Algorithms: Taxonomy and Survey" (ACM Computing Surveys 36(4), 2004) for the fixed-sequencer class Ch. 98 implements.
- **George Varghese & Tony Lauck, "Hashed and Hierarchical Timing Wheels" (SOSP 1987; IEEE/ACM ToN 1997).** The timer data structure every busy-poll engine and every OS kernel uses; the complexity analysis behind **Ch. 49**'s wheel-vs-heap measurements. Pair with Gleixner & Niehaus, "Hrtimers and Beyond" (OLS 2006) and Gleixner's 2016 "timers: Refactor the timer wheel" series for the kernel's two timer subsystems and why the wheel stopped cascading.
- **Pat Helland, "Idempotence Is Not a Medical Condition" (ACM Queue 10(4), 2012)** and **Gray & Reuter, *Transaction Processing*, Ch. 10–11 (1993)** — idempotent messaging and write-ahead logging: the reasoning behind **Ch. 93**'s journal-before-send, slot+generation ClOrdIDs and the unknown-state matrix.
- **Matt Welsh, David Culler & Eric Brewer, "SEDA" (SOSP 2001)** and **Leonard Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems*, Vol. I (1975)** — staged pipelines with admission control, and the arrival-vs-service arithmetic that **Ch. 43–44** measure (hop cost, queueing under a burst, conflation's bounded staleness).
- **Paul McKenney, *Is Parallel Programming Hard, And, If So, What Can You Do About It?* and his RCU papers.** The authoritative source on RCU and safe memory reclamation — **Ch. 41**, with ties to seqlocks (Ch. 40) and hot reload (Ch. 90). Deep, rigorous, free online.
- **Leslie Lamport, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System" (CACM 1978) and TLA+.** The happens-before idea beneath every memory-model argument in the book, and the TLA+ / model-checking resources that **Ch. 47** (§47.4.3) cites for checking lock-free designs — the one place a chapter leans on him directly.
- **Maranget, Sarkar & Sewell, *A Tutorial Introduction to the ARM and POWER Relaxed Memory Models*.** Why weakly-ordered hardware reorders, and how to reason about it — the reference behind **Appendix A.1** (and a sharp complement to Ch. 35).
- **Candea & Fox, "Crash-Only Software" (2003).** The crash-only design philosophy of **Ch. 91** — recovery as the only path, fast restart, fault containment.
- **Michael & Scott, "Simple, Fast, and Practical Non-Blocking and Blocking Concurrent Queue Algorithms" (PODC 1996).** The MS-queue — the reference unbounded lock-free queue and the baseline every bounded ring is measured against — **Ch. 39** (§39.4.1), with its ABA/reclamation consequences in Ch. 41 and the Disruptor's contrast in Ch. 42.
- **Hans Boehm & Sarita Adve, "Foundations of the C++ Concurrency Memory Model" (PLDI 2008)** and **Boehm, "Can Seqlocks Get Along with Programming Language Memory Models?"** The rationale and semantics behind C++11 atomics and data-race-freedom — **Ch. 35**; the seqlock paper is the definitive analysis of why a naive seqlock is a data race and how to write one the memory model accepts — **Ch. 40** (§40.5).
- **Maged Michael, "Hazard Pointers: Safe Memory Reclamation for Lock-Free Objects" (IEEE TPDS 15(6), 2004)** and **Keir Fraser, *Practical Lock-Freedom* (PhD thesis, University of Cambridge, 2004).** The two original safe-memory-reclamation schemes — per-pointer protection with bounded memory, and epoch-based reclamation with cheap readers but unbounded deferral — compared and measured in **Ch. 41** (§41.4.2–§41.4.3).
- **Thomas Anderson, "The Performance of Spin Lock Alternatives for Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" (IEEE TPDS 1990)** and **Mellor-Crummey & Scott, "Algorithms for Scalable Synchronization on Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" (ACM TOCS 1991).** TAS vs TTAS vs backoff vs queue locks, and the MCS lock that gives each waiter its own cache line — the measurements and techniques of **Ch. 37** (§37.3–§37.4).
- **David Goldberg, "What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic" (ACM Computing Surveys, 1991).** IEEE 754 representability, rounding and accumulated error — the floating-point half of **Ch. 32** (§32.2) and the case for scaled-integer prices.
- **Yang, Park & Ousterhout, "NanoLog: A Nanosecond Scale Logging System" (USENIX ATC 2018).** Compile-time format extraction, binary records and an offline decoder — the deferred/binary-logging design that **Ch. 86** builds (§86.2, §86.4.2).
- **The low-latency transport and congestion papers** — Zhu et al., "Congestion Control for Large-Scale RDMA Deployments" (**DCQCN**, SIGCOMM 2015); Kalia, Kaminsky & Andersen, "Datacenter RPCs can be General and Fast" (**eRPC**, NSDI 2019); Montazeri, Li, Alizadeh & Ousterhout, "Homa: A Receiver-Driven Low-Latency Transport Protocol Using Network Priorities" (SIGCOMM 2018). Why RoCEv2 needs ECN-driven rate control — **Ch. 74** (§74.2) — and what a µs-scale, head-of-line-free transport looks like beyond TCP — **Ch. 75** (§75.4.2–§75.4.3).
- **Gil Tene, "How NOT to Measure Latency" (and the coordinated-omission material).** Why naive latency measurement lies and how HdrHistogram fixes it — the measurement rigor of **Ch. 3** and the tail-latency framing of Ch. 1.
- **Daniel Bristot de Oliveira et al., "Demystifying the Real-Time Linux Scheduling Latency" (ECRTS 2020), and the osnoise/timerlat write-ups (Linux Plumbers 2021; LWN).** Latency as a sum of attributable intervals — the model the `osnoise` and `timerlat` tracers implement and that **Ch. 54** uses to name the source of the last spike on an isolated core.
- **P0593R6, *Implicit creation of objects for low-level object manipulation* (R. Smith, W. Brown, et al., C++20/DR) and P2590R2, *Explicit lifetime management* (`std::start_lifetime_as`, T. Köppe, C++23).** The object-lifetime rules that make `memcpy` of a wire struct well-defined and give the struct-over-receive-buffer overlay a sanctioned, zero-instruction form — **Ch. 25 §25.2, §25.4.2**, Ch. 62.
- **P0732R2, *Class Types in Non-Type Template Parameters* (J. Maurer, C++20) and P2996, *Reflection for C++26* (B. Revzin, W. Childers, P. Dimov, A. Katz, D. Vandevoorde, D. Stone).** The language machinery behind the compile-time protocol decoder — string literals as template arguments today, member enumeration tomorrow — **Ch. 22**.
- **P0792R14, *`function_ref`: a type-erased callable reference* (V. Zverovich, T. Brindle, et al., C++26); P0288R9, *`move_only_function`* (M. Klimek et al., C++23).** The lighter type erasers measured against `std::function` and a template parameter — **Ch. 15 §15.4.5**, Ch. 24 §24.4.1.
- **P0896R4, *The One Ranges Proposal* (E. Niebler, C. Carter, C. Di Bella, C++20).** Iterators, sentinels, views and adaptors — and, read with the asm, why `views::filter` costs what a searched loop costs — **Ch. 24 §24.2.2**.
- **J. Regehr, *A Guide to Undefined Behavior in C and C++* (2010) and C. Lattner, *What Every C Programmer Should Know About Undefined Behavior* (LLVM blog, 2011); X. Wang et al., *Towards Optimization-Safe Systems* (SOSP 2013).** UB as the optimizer's licence, with the catalogue of real deletions (null checks, overflow checks) it produced in shipped code — the reading **Appendix P.3** follows; ties Ch. 25, 47, 89.

- **Cary G. Gray and David R. Cheriton, "Leases: An Efficient Fault-Tolerant Mechanism for Distributed File Cache Consistency" (SOSP 1989); Mike Burrows, "The Chubby Lock Service" (OSDI 2006); Diego Ongaro and John Ousterhout, "In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm" (Raft, USENIX ATC 2014); Martin Kleppmann, "How to do distributed locking" (2016).** Leases, sequencers/fencing tokens, terms and the zombie problem — the failover design of **Ch. 99** (§99.2.3, §99.4.1, §99.4.6).
- **Tushar D. Chandra and Sam Toueg, "Unreliable Failure Detectors for Reliable Distributed Systems" (JACM 1996); N. Hayashibara, X. Défago, R. Yared and T. Katayama, "The φ Accrual Failure Detector" (SRDS 2004).** Why dead and slow cannot be told apart, and the detector that reports a suspicion level instead of a verdict — **Ch. 99 §99.2.2**, §99.4.2.
- **Koen Claessen and John Hughes, "QuickCheck" (ICFP 2000); Andreas Zeller and Ralf Hildebrandt, "Simplifying and Isolating Failure-Inducing Input" (IEEE TSE 2002); Will Wilson, "Testing Distributed Systems with Deterministic Simulation" (Strange Loop 2014) and the FoundationDB testing documentation.** Property-based testing, the delta-debugging shrinker, and deterministic simulation testing — **Ch. 103** (§103.2.2–§103.2.3, §103.4.2–§103.4.3).
- **Tomas Kalibera and Richard Jones, "Rigorous Benchmarking in Reasonable Time" (ISMM 2013); H. B. Mann and D. R. Whitney (1947); Mytkowicz et al., "Producing Wrong Data Without Doing Anything Obviously Wrong!" (ASPLOS 2009); Chen et al., "AutoFDO" (CGO 2016); Panchenko et al., "BOLT" (CGO 2019).** The statistics and the noise sources behind a CI performance gate, and the production-refresh path for profiles — **Ch. 104** (§104.2.1, §104.4.2, §104.4.5).
- **Emmerich et al., "MoonGen: A Scriptable High-Speed Packet Generator" (IMC 2015).** Timestamped, paced pcap replay over DPDK — the hardware-paced end of **Ch. 97 §97.4.3**.

## G.2 Vendor & reference manuals

Cited across **Parts II-VI** (Ch. 8, 12–14, 33–35) and Appendix E. The optimization manual especially repays study.
- **★ Agner Fog's optimization manuals** (*Optimizing software in C++*, *The microarchitecture of Intel/AMD CPUs*, and the **instruction tables**). - **Intel *Intrinsics Guide*.** The searchable per-intrinsic reference (semantics, CPUID flags, latency/throughput) behind the SSE/AVX/AVX-512 work of **Ch. 34**, the BMI/BMI2 tricks of Ch. 33 and the prefetch/streaming-store intrinsics of Ch. 11.
- **IEEE 1588 (Precision Time Protocol), including the 2019 revision (High Accuracy / White Rabbit profile).** The protocol behind cross-host timestamps and true wire-to-wire tick-to-trade — **Ch. 68** (§68.2, §68.4.3), with the NIC PHC of Ch. 65; tooling in `linuxptp` (G.5).

- **NVIDIA *CUDA C++ Programming Guide* and *CUDA C++ Best Practices Guide*.** The execution/memory model, warps/occupancy, coalescing, streams, pinned/unified memory — **Ch. 77** (§77.2, §77.4) — and the PCIe transfer cost it shares with Ch. 76.

## G.3 Books

- **★ Hennessy & Patterson, *Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach* (6th ed., 2017).** The foundational architecture text — pipelines, caches, ILP, memory systems, multiprocessors. The "why" beneath **Part II** (Ch. 8–19) and the quantitative mindset of the whole book.
- **★ Anthony Williams, *C++ Concurrency in Action* (2nd ed.).** The definitive practical guide to the C++ memory model, atomics, and lock-free programming — **Part VI** (Ch. 35–47). The book to own for the concurrency chapters.
- **★ Maurice Herlihy & Nir Shavit, *The Art of Multiprocessor Programming* (2nd ed., with Luchangco & Spear).** The theory behind lock-free programming — linearizability, progress guarantees (obstruction-/lock-/wait-free), CAS, ABA and the memory-reclamation problem — **Ch. 39** (§39.2) and Ch. 41.
- **Agner Fog's optimization series** (also in G.2, as books) — *Optimizing software in C++* reads as a coherent text on the abstraction-cost and codegen concerns of **Part III** (Ch. 20–26).
- **Vandevoorde, Josuttis & Gregor, *C++ Templates: The Complete Guide* (2nd ed.)** and **Andrei Alexandrescu, *Modern C++ Design*.** The template instantiation/cost model, CRTP, expression templates and policy-based design of **Ch. 20–21**, and the small-object (Loki) allocator behind the pools of Ch. 29 (§29.4.2).
- **Fedor Pikus, *The Art of Writing Efficient Programs* (Packt, 2021) and *Hands-On Design Patterns with C++* (2nd ed., Packt, 2023).** Modern, practical performance-oriented C++ — measurement, memory, concurrency and the design patterns beneath zero-cost abstractions. A general companion to Ch. 3, 28–30 and 35–38 rather than a chapter-cited source.
- **Scott Meyers, *Effective Modern C++*** and **the C++ Core Guidelines.** Idiomatic modern C++ underpinning the language-mechanics Parts (Ch. 20–25) and the safety discipline of Ch. 89.
- **Brendan Gregg, *Systems Performance* (2nd ed.)** and ***BPF Performance Tools*.** The methodology of production performance analysis — the USE method, flame graphs, and the eBPF tooling of **Ch. 71** and the production-monitoring stance of **Ch. 106**.
- **Robert Love, *Linux System Programming*** and **Michael Kerrisk, *The Linux Programming Interface*.** The syscall-level reference behind **Part VIII** (Ch. 56, 57, 59, 60) and the memory/process chapters (Ch. 28, 31, 91).
- **Daniel Lemire's work and *Hacker's Delight* (Warren).** Bit-manipulation and branch-free integer tricks — **Ch. 33** (and the SWAR/SIMD parsing of Ch. 62).
- **Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges: Market Microstructure for Practitioners*.** The standard market-structure text — matching, order types, maker/taker, the participants' roles — behind **Appendix J** (J.1–J.3) and the trading framing of Ch. 30, 62 and the case studies; the FIX Trading Community specifications (G.2) cover the wire side.
- **Beyer, Jones, Petoff, Murphy (eds.), *Site Reliability Engineering* (2016) and *The Site Reliability Workbook* (2018).** SLOs, error budgets and multi-window burn-rate alerting — adapted to session phases in **Ch. 88 §88.4.4**; the production-readiness-review idea behind **Appendix M**.
- **Beau Williamson, *Developing IP Multicast Networks* (Cisco Press).** PIM, RPF and rendezvous points — the routed-multicast background of the venue distribution tree in **Ch. 64 §64.2.3**.
- **Raj Jain, *The Art of Computer Systems Performance Analysis* (1991).** Ch. 2's catalogue of measurement mistakes — comparing unconverted or mis-aligned measurements chief among them — is the methodological footing of the one-timeline rule in **Ch. 100 §100.2.2**.


- **Mara Bos, *Rust Atomics and Locks* (O'Reilly, 2023).** The Rust memory model and its x86/ARM codegen — the companion to Ch. 35 for **Ch. 82** (§82.2.3, §82.4.3).
- **Nicholas Nethercote, *The Rust Performance Book*; Jon Gjengset, *Rust for Rustaceans*.** Bounds-check elision, allocation avoidance, `unsafe` abstraction and FFI engineering — **Ch. 82**, **Ch. 83**.
- **Konrad Kokosa, *Pro .NET Memory Management*; Minsky, Madhavapeddy & Hickey, *Real World OCaml* (memory representation, GC chapters).** The .NET and OCaml runtimes at the depth **Ch. 84** (§84.4.4, §84.4.6) can only summarise.
- **T. Winters, T. Manshreck, H. Wright, *Software Engineering at Google* (O'Reilly, 2020), chs. 18–21.** The case for hermetic, declared-input builds and content-addressed caching — the policy half of **Ch. 27**.

Everything else in this book is a path between those landmarks and the specific nanosecond you're chasing.
