# Appendix G — Annotated Bibliography

> The consolidated reading list behind the whole book — the per-chapter *References* and *Additional Reading* gathered, deduplicated, and annotated. Each entry says **what it's good for** and **which chapters/Parts draw on it**, so you can go deeper on any topic from one place. Organized by kind — foundational papers (G.1), vendor & reference manuals (G.2), books (G.3), influential talks & blogs (G.4), and tooling documentation (G.5) — and within each, roughly by where the book first leans on it.

This is a *map*, not a syllabus: you don't read it front to back. When a chapter sent you here, find the source it cited and use the annotation to decide whether it's the deep-dive you want. The handful of works marked **★** are the ones that repay reading in full for anyone serious about low-latency systems.

## G.1 Foundational papers

- **I. Stoica and H. Abdel-Wahab, *Earliest Eligible Virtual Deadline First: A Flexible and Accurate Mechanism for Proportional Share Resource Allocation* (Old Dominion TR 95-22, 1995); L. Abeni and G. Buttazzo, *Integrating Multimedia Applications in Hard Real-Time Systems* (RTSS 1998 — the Constant Bandwidth Server); G. Lipari and S. Baruah, *Greedy Reclamation of Unused Bandwidth in Constant-Bandwidth Servers* (ECRTS 2000 — GRUB).** The three papers the Linux scheduler now implements: EEVDF (the fair class since 6.6 — lag, eligibility, virtual deadlines), CBS (`SCHED_DEADLINE`'s throttle) and GRUB (`SCHED_FLAG_RECLAIM`); **Ch. 59 §59.2.1–§59.2.2** is their operational reading.
- **B. Jacob, S. Ng and D. Wang, *Memory Systems: Cache, DRAM, Disk* (Morgan Kaufmann, 2008); Y. Kim et al., *A Case for Exploiting Subarray-Level Parallelism in DRAM* (ISCA 2012); JEDEC JESD79-4 (DDR4) and JESD79-5 (DDR5).** The DRAM organisation, timing parameters (tCL/tRCD/tRP/tRFC/tREFI), page policies, refresh and bank-group architecture behind **Ch. 9**'s row-hit/row-conflict bimodality and refresh tail; the JEDEC standards are the authority for the numbers.
- **★ Ulrich Drepper, *What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory* (2007).** The definitive long-form treatment of caches, memory hierarchy, NUMA, and access patterns — dated in specifics, timeless in fundamentals. The intellectual backbone of **Part II**'s memory chapters (Ch. 8–12) and the NUMA chapter (Ch. 18). If you read one thing from this list, read this.
- **★ Jeff Dean, "Numbers Everyone Should Know" / Peter Norvig's latency table.** The orders-of-magnitude latency intuition the whole book trades on; refreshed and trading-annotated in **Appendix E**. Underlies Ch. 1's "reason about the ratios."
- **★ Jeff Dean & Luiz André Barroso, "The Tail at Scale" (CACM 56(2), 2013).** The canonical argument that tail latency, not the mean, governs latency-critical systems, and why fan-out multiplies rare slowness — the distribution-first framing of **Ch. 1** and the p99/p99.9 discipline of Ch. 3 and 120.
- **★ The LMAX *Disruptor* technical paper (Thompson, Farley, Barker, Gee, Stewart, 2011).** The ring-buffer + sequence-barrier mechanical-sympathy design that anchors **Ch. 48**, and whose ideas recur in the lock-free queues (Ch. 45), logging (Ch. 98), the shared-memory IPC rings (Ch. 67), and the Aeron lineage (Ch. 85). Essential HFT-adjacent reading.
- **Fred B. Schneider, "Implementing Fault-Tolerant Services Using the State Machine Approach: A Tutorial" (ACM Computing Surveys 22(4), 1990)** — state-machine replication: deterministic replicas fed one total order hold identical state. The theory under **Ch. 111**'s sequencer and replicas and Ch. 103's deterministic core; read with Lamport's "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events" (CACM 1978) for where the total order comes from, and Défago, Schiper & Urbán's "Total Order Broadcast and Multicast Algorithms: Taxonomy and Survey" (ACM Computing Surveys 36(4), 2004) for the fixed-sequencer class Ch. 111 implements.
- **George Varghese & Tony Lauck, "Hashed and Hierarchical Timing Wheels" (SOSP 1987; IEEE/ACM ToN 1997).** The timer data structure every busy-poll engine and every OS kernel uses; the complexity analysis behind **Ch. 55**'s wheel-vs-heap measurements. Pair with Gleixner & Niehaus, "Hrtimers and Beyond" (OLS 2006) and Gleixner's 2016 "timers: Refactor the timer wheel" series for the kernel's two timer subsystems and why the wheel stopped cascading.
- **Pat Helland, "Idempotence Is Not a Medical Condition" (ACM Queue 10(4), 2012)** and **Gray & Reuter, *Transaction Processing*, ch. 9–10 (1993)** — idempotent messaging and write-ahead logging: the reasoning behind **Ch. 105**'s journal-before-send, slot+generation ClOrdIDs and the unknown-state matrix.
- **Matt Welsh, David Culler & Eric Brewer, "SEDA" (SOSP 2001)** and **Leonard Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems*, Vol. I (1975)** — staged pipelines with admission control, and the arrival-vs-service arithmetic that **Ch. 49–50** measure (hop cost, queueing under a burst, conflation's bounded staleness).
- **Paul McKenney, *Is Parallel Programming Hard, And, If So, What Can You Do About It?* and his RCU papers.** The authoritative source on RCU and safe memory reclamation — **Ch. 47**, with ties to seqlocks (Ch. 46) and hot reload (Ch. 102). Deep, rigorous, free online.
- **Leslie Lamport, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System" (CACM 1978) and TLA+.** The happens-before idea beneath every memory-model argument in the book, and the TLA+ / model-checking resources that **Ch. 53** (§53.4.3) cites for checking lock-free designs — the one place a chapter leans on him directly.
- **Maranget, Sarkar & Sewell, *A Tutorial Introduction to the ARM and POWER Relaxed Memory Models*.** Why weakly-ordered hardware reorders, and how to reason about it — the reference behind **Appendix A.1** (and a sharp complement to Ch. 41).
- **Candea & Fox, "Crash-Only Software" (2003).** The crash-only design philosophy of **Ch. 103** — recovery as the only path, fast restart, fault containment.
- **Michael & Scott, "Simple, Fast, and Practical Non-Blocking and Blocking Concurrent Queue Algorithms" (PODC 1996).** The MS-queue — the reference unbounded lock-free queue and the baseline every bounded ring is measured against — **Ch. 45** (§45.4.1), with its ABA/reclamation consequences in Ch. 47 and the Disruptor's contrast in Ch. 48.
- **Hans Boehm & Sarita Adve, "Foundations of the C++ Concurrency Memory Model" (PLDI 2008)** and **Boehm, "Can Seqlocks Get Along with Programming Language Memory Models?"** The rationale and semantics behind C++11 atomics and data-race-freedom — **Ch. 41**; the seqlock paper is the definitive analysis of why a naive seqlock is a data race and how to write one the memory model accepts — **Ch. 46** (§46.5).
- **Maged Michael, "Hazard Pointers: Safe Memory Reclamation for Lock-Free Objects" (IEEE TPDS 15(6), 2004)** and **Keir Fraser, *Practical Lock-Freedom* (PhD thesis, University of Cambridge, 2004).** The two original safe-memory-reclamation schemes — per-pointer protection with bounded memory, and epoch-based reclamation with cheap readers but unbounded deferral — compared and measured in **Ch. 47** (§47.4.2–§47.4.3).
- **Thomas Anderson, "The Performance of Spin Lock Alternatives for Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" (IEEE TPDS 1990)** and **Mellor-Crummey & Scott, "Algorithms for Scalable Synchronization on Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" (ACM TOCS 1991).** TAS vs TTAS vs backoff vs queue locks, and the MCS lock that gives each waiter its own cache line — the measurements and techniques of **Ch. 43** (§43.3–§43.4).
- **David Goldberg, "What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic" (ACM Computing Surveys, 1991).** IEEE 754 representability, rounding and accumulated error — the floating-point half of **Ch. 35** (§35.2) and the case for scaled-integer prices.
- **Yang, Park & Ousterhout, "NanoLog: A Nanosecond Scale Logging System" (USENIX ATC 2018).** Compile-time format extraction, binary records and an offline decoder — the deferred/binary-logging design that **Ch. 98** builds (§98.2, §98.4.2).
- **The low-latency transport and congestion papers** — Zhu et al., "Congestion Control for Large-Scale RDMA Deployments" (**DCQCN**, SIGCOMM 2015); Kalia, Kaminsky & Andersen, "Datacenter RPCs can be General and Fast" (**eRPC**, NSDI 2019); Montazeri, Li, Alizadeh & Ousterhout, "Homa: A Receiver-Driven Low-Latency Transport Protocol Using Network Priorities" (SIGCOMM 2018). Why RoCEv2 needs ECN-driven rate control — **Ch. 84** (§84.2) — and what a µs-scale, head-of-line-free transport looks like beyond TCP — **Ch. 85** (§85.4.2–§85.4.3).
- **Gil Tene, "How NOT to Measure Latency" (and the coordinated-omission material).** Why naive latency measurement lies and how HdrHistogram fixes it — the measurement rigor of **Ch. 3** and the tail-latency framing of Ch. 1.
- **Daniel Bristot de Oliveira et al., "Demystifying the Real-Time Linux Scheduling Latency" (ECRTS 2020), and the osnoise/timerlat write-ups (Linux Plumbers 2021; LWN).** Latency as a sum of attributable intervals — the model the `osnoise` and `timerlat` tracers implement and that **Ch. 61** uses to name the source of the last spike on an isolated core.
- **P0593R6, *Implicit creation of objects for low-level object manipulation* (R. Smith, W. Brown, et al., C++20/DR) and P2590R2, *Explicit lifetime management* (`std::start_lifetime_as`, T. Köppe, C++23).** The object-lifetime rules that make `memcpy` of a wire struct well-defined and give the struct-over-receive-buffer overlay a sanctioned, zero-instruction form — **Ch. 26 §26.2, §26.4.2**, Ch. 70.
- **P0732R2, *Class Types in Non-Type Template Parameters* (J. Maurer, C++20) and P2996, *Reflection for C++26* (B. Revzin, W. Childers, P. Dimov, A. Katz, D. Vandevoorde, D. Stone).** The language machinery behind the compile-time protocol decoder — string literals as template arguments today, member enumeration tomorrow — **Ch. 23**.
- **P0792R14, *`function_ref`: a type-erased callable reference* (V. Zverovich, T. Brindle, et al., C++26); P0288R9, *`move_only_function`* (M. Klimek et al., C++23).** The lighter type erasers measured against `std::function` and a template parameter — **Ch. 16 §16.4.5**, Ch. 25 §25.4.1.
- **P0896R4, *The One Ranges Proposal* (E. Niebler, C. Carter, C. Di Bella, C++20).** Iterators, sentinels, views and adaptors — and, read with the asm, why `views::filter` costs what a searched loop costs — **Ch. 25 §25.2.2**.
- **J. Regehr, *A Guide to Undefined Behavior in C and C++* (2010) and C. Lattner, *What Every C Programmer Should Know About Undefined Behavior* (LLVM blog, 2011); X. Wang et al., *Towards Optimization-Safe Systems* (SOSP 2013).** UB as the optimizer's licence, with the catalogue of real deletions (null checks, overflow checks) it produced in shipped code — the reading **Appendix P.3** follows; ties Ch. 26, 53, 101.

- **Cary G. Gray and David R. Cheriton, "Leases: An Efficient Fault-Tolerant Mechanism for Distributed File Cache Consistency" (SOSP 1989); Mike Burrows, "The Chubby Lock Service" (OSDI 2006); Diego Ongaro and John Ousterhout, "In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm" (Raft, USENIX ATC 2014); Martin Kleppmann, "How to do distributed locking" (2016).** Leases, sequencers/fencing tokens, terms and the zombie problem — the failover design of **Ch. 112** (§112.2.3, §112.4.1, §112.4.6).
- **Tushar D. Chandra and Sam Toueg, "Unreliable Failure Detectors for Reliable Distributed Systems" (JACM 1996); N. Hayashibara, X. Défago, R. Yared and T. Katayama, "The φ Accrual Failure Detector" (SRDS 2004).** Why dead and slow cannot be told apart, and the detector that reports a suspicion level instead of a verdict — **Ch. 112 §112.2.2**, §112.4.2.
- **Koen Claessen and John Hughes, "QuickCheck" (ICFP 2000); Andreas Zeller and Ralf Hildebrandt, "Simplifying and Isolating Failure-Inducing Input" (IEEE TSE 2002); Will Wilson, "Testing Distributed Systems with Deterministic Simulation" (Strange Loop 2014) and the FoundationDB testing documentation.** Property-based testing, the delta-debugging shrinker, and deterministic simulation testing — **Ch. 116** (§116.2.2–§116.2.3, §116.4.2–§116.4.3).
- **Tomas Kalibera and Richard Jones, "Rigorous Benchmarking in Reasonable Time" (ISMM 2013); H. B. Mann and D. R. Whitney (1947); Mytkowicz et al., "Producing Wrong Data Without Doing Anything Obviously Wrong!" (ASPLOS 2009); Chen et al., "AutoFDO" (CGO 2016); Panchenko et al., "BOLT" (CGO 2019).** The statistics and the noise sources behind a CI performance gate, and the production-refresh path for profiles — **Ch. 118** (§118.2.1, §118.4.2, §118.4.5).
- **Emmerich et al., "MoonGen: A Scriptable High-Speed Packet Generator" (IMC 2015).** Timestamped, paced pcap replay over DPDK — the hardware-paced end of **Ch. 110 §110.4.3**.
- **B. P. Welford, "Note on a Method for Calculating Corrected Sums of Squares and Products" (Technometrics 4(3), 1962); D. H. D. West, "Updating Mean and Variance Estimates: An Improved Method" (CACM 22(9), 1979).** The online mean/variance recurrence and its weighted form — the reason the strategy core of **Ch. 40** (§40.4.2) never computes `E[x²] − E[x]²`. Chan, Golub & LeVeque (1983) for the exact merge of partial accumulators.
- **P.-V. Khuong & P. Morin, "Array Layouts for Comparison-Based Searching" (ACM J. Experimental Algorithmics 22, 2017).** Branchless binary search, the Eytzinger/B-tree/van Emde Boas layouts and the measurements — including the "branchless loses on predictable keys" caveat — behind **Ch. 32 §32.4.6** and the §32.3 row that closes Ch. 3's promise.
- **R. Cont, A. Kukanov & S. Stoikov, "The Price Impact of Order Book Events" (J. Financial Econometrics 12(1), 2014).** Order-flow imbalance as a BBO-delta signal — the integer computation of **Ch. 40 §40.4.4**, read straight off the Ch. 33 book's BBO line.
- **D. Lemire, "Number Parsing at a Gigabyte per Second" (Software: Practice & Experience 51(8), 2021); G. Langdale & D. Lemire, "Parsing Gigabytes of JSON per Second" (VLDB Journal 28, 2019).** The SWAR/SIMD digit-conversion constructions and the bitmask tokeniser of **Ch. 71** — the second measured there as the technique that *loses* on a 145-byte FIX message and wins on kilobytes.

## G.7 A short shelf

If you build a low-latency C++ shelf from this list, start here: **Drepper** (memory), **Hennessy & Patterson** (architecture), **Williams** (concurrency), **Agner Fog's manuals** (the cycle-level reality), the **Intel optimization manual** (x86 specifics), **Gregg's *Systems Performance*** (production methodology), the **LMAX Disruptor paper** and **Carl Cook's talk** (the HFT mindset), and **McKenney** (reclamation). Everything else in this book is a path between those landmarks and the specific nanosecond you're chasing.
